# Style guide for drafting the Pact

> **Numbering note (2026-09-06).** This file predates the insertion of Article 10
> (Mines) and Article 15 (Schooling). Where a number is paired with a name below,
> the name is authoritative. Current numbering: 10 Mines, 11 Supply, 12 Commerce,
> 13 Labor, 14 Housing, 15 Schooling, 16 Public Order, 17 Crimes, 18 Cleaning,
> 19 Health, 20 Assembly, 21 Records, 22 Emergency, 23 Amendment.

Practical, checklist-form guidance for writing Articles 2–22. Distilled
from `CANON.md` section 17 (the show's prop "Pact" text, written in
consultation with Hugh Howey — the closest thing to an authorial style
precedent found) and from what Article 1 already establishes in practice.
Read `CANON.md` for the canon reasoning; this file is just the "how to
write the next sentence" version.

## Meta-principle: the guidelines have their own "why"

The same discipline applied to the Pact's content (`HYPOTHESES.md`'s
"why is this true, and what does that imply") also applies to *this
project's own guidelines*. Occasionally ask why a given guideline was
set for this project, and if a good answer emerges, look for further
principles that would cohere with that same answer — the same way a
good `HYPOTHESES.md` entry is one that explains multiple already-known
facts at once, not just the one it was written for.

**Worked example (2026-09-01)**: why coherence/consilience and the "why"
heuristic at all — why does *this* project care so much, more than most
tie-in worldbuilding would? Hypothesis: because *Wool*'s own protagonists
(Allison, Juliette) drive the story by noticing small inconsistencies —
a hollow procedure, an oddly specific rule, a suit that shouldn't fail —
and reasoning their way from "that's strange" to a hidden truth. A Pact
built with real, worked-out hidden logic *rewards a reader doing exactly
that*, the way the actual books do; a Pact that's coherent only on the
surface wouldn't. This hypothesis is stronger for also explaining
something already independently decided: Victor's occasional tonal
uncanniness (`CANON.md` 17a) is *already* a "seam a careful reader could
notice" — the same mechanism, arrived at separately. That's a good sign.

**Principle this generates, added now**: the Pact should contain a
*small* number of deliberately placed seams — a clause that's oddly
over-specific, a due-process gesture that doesn't survive scrutiny, a
tonal slip — that a sufficiently careful reader could use to reconstruct
a hidden truth, the same way the books' characters do. This is not
"leave plot holes" — every seam should still be fully explained in
`FACTS.md`/`HYPOTHESES.md`, just not resolved *in the Pact's own text*.
Keep these rare (a handful across the whole work) so the Pact still
reads as coherent to a casual reader and only reveals its seams to a
close one — consistent with Victor's own "consistency and plausibility"
values (`STYLE.md` above): he wouldn't leave a seam he didn't think was
safe to leave.

## Restraint: most of the Pact should be mundane (guiding principle, 2026-09-01)

A corrective to everything above, and arguably the most important guard
against where these principles could drift: **most of the Pact's text
should have no hidden agenda at all.** It should read as genuinely
boring administrative law — quorum rules, filing procedures, routine
definitions — the way most of any real legal code does. This isn't in
tension with Victor/Anna's psychological fingerprints or the planted
seams; it's what makes them work. If every clause secretly encoded
behavioral engineering or compassion-as-camouflage, the Pact would stop
reading like a document that could plausibly sit on a citizen's shelf
and start reading like a puzzle box built for a reader who already knows
to distrust every sentence — which undermines the exact verisimilitude
`FACTS.md`/`HYPOTHESES.md` exist to protect. Rarity is the camouflage:
a handful of real tells land precisely because the surrounding hundred
clauses are exactly what they claim to be.

## Write for three readers (guiding principle, 2026-09-01)

A practical test for any passage being drafted: it should hold up for
three separate readers at once, each taking away something different,
without any of them catching a contradiction.

1. **The ordinary citizen**, reading at face value — gets the stated
   rationale, the civic register, nothing more.
2. **The in-world close/suspicious reader** — the Allison/Juliette
   mold, someone who might tug on a planted seam or notice a due-process
   clause is hollow. Should occasionally be rewarded, rarely (see
   "Restraint" above), never contradicted.
3. **The actual reader of this book** — knows the whole *Wool* saga,
   catches dramatic irony neither in-world reader has access to (e.g.
   recognizing Victor's fingerprints, or the Order-adjacent gestures for
   what they are). This reader can be given more than reader 2 without
   anything being stated outright — irony available externally doesn't
   need to be discoverable in-world.

Use this as a check when a clause feels off: which of the three readers
would break first, and is that acceptable for this particular passage?

## Coherence and consilience (highest priority)

Before drafting any Article, check `FACTS.md` for facts already
established that the new Article touches. After drafting, add any new
facts it asserts or implies — with their in-world explanation and their
consequences for other Articles — back into `FACTS.md`. Don't leave a
rule unexplained just because the Pact's own text doesn't need to state
the explanation; work it out anyway, so it can be checked against later.
If a new Article would depend on one of `FACTS.md`'s open/unresolved
items, resolve that item first rather than drafting around it.

## The "why" heuristic for canon facts

A related but distinct discipline, tracked in `HYPOTHESES.md`: for any
canon fact from `CANON.md` (not just our own invented Pact facts), ask
**why it's true in-world**, form a reasonable hypothesis, and then use
that hypothesis to *generate* more Pact content consistent with the same
underlying logic — not just restate the one fact in isolation. Example:
the show's magnification ban implies a hypothesis ("prevents citizens
from reverse-engineering signal/radio electronics") that, generalized,
should also drive restrictions on private electronics/optics possession,
a controlled IT-only curriculum track, and a licensing exception for
Mechanical's legitimate tool use — see `HYPOTHESES.md` entry 1. Check
`HYPOTHESES.md` before drafting any Article that touches technology,
restricted knowledge, or an unexplained-but-canon rule.

## The Founders' psychology (authorial characterization)

Four load-bearing facts about the in-world authors of the Pact, stated by
the user as project principles (2026-09-01). Everything else in this file
should be read as downstream of these:

1. **They place high value on consistency and plausibility.** The Pact
   should never read as arbitrary or sloppy from the inside — every rule
   should sound like it follows from something, even where that
   something is never stated outright.
2. **They deliberately mislead citizens whenever they judge it
   appropriate.** Deception is a normal, unremarkable tool of
   governance to them, not a moral failure or a last resort — used
   exactly when it's useful and not otherwise.
3. **They are strict, exceptionless utilitarians.** They always take the
   most pragmatic option available, *even when it violates a deeply-held
   moral principle.* No sentimental carve-outs, no principle so sacred it
   overrides a better cost/benefit outcome — not "usually," not "unless
   it's really bad," but always.
4. **Their actual motivations are always internally consistent and
   logical, to a fault.** Whatever they really believe and intend is
   never arbitrary, never a plot hole, never "because the author needed
   it" — it's the product of cold, working reasoning, even when that
   reasoning is monstrous.

**The practical consequence — split every rationale in two:**

- The **stated rationale**: what the Pact's own text (or an official
  explanation) tells citizens. Per (1) and (2), this is always
  plausible-sounding and internally consistent *on its own terms* — but
  may be a deliberate misdirection from the real reason.
- The **true rationale**: what the Founders actually calculated. Per (3)
  and (4), this is always a coherent, pragmatic, unsentimental piece of
  reasoning — the real cost/benefit analysis, however cold.

These don't have to differ every time — sometimes the stated reason *is*
the true one, when honesty happens to be the pragmatic choice. But when
drafting, work out both, even if only the stated one (or neither,
explicitly) makes it into the Pact's actual text. This sharpens the
"gesture without specifics" device already used in Article 1 (e.g. the
sensor-maintenance passage, "such further procedure as that Department
alone is instructed in") — that gesture is now understood as exactly
what a plausibility-obsessed, comfortable-with-deception author would
write. It also explains, retroactively, why the "hollow due process"
pattern (see below) works so well as a model: a family's post-hoc
"representations" that never change an outcome is utilitarian efficiency
(3) wrapped in a plausible-sounding legitimacy gesture (1)+(2).

Use this when filling in `HYPOTHESES.md`: a hypothesis for "why" a canon
fact is true should default to the *true* (utilitarian, logical)
rationale; separately consider what *stated* rationale the Founders would
plausibly have offered citizens instead, if the true one would be
destabilizing to admit.

## The psychologist-author: Victor

The Pact's actual writing was **led by Victor**, the psychologist among
the three founding architects (book-confirmed in *Shift*; see
`CANON.md` sections 2 and 17a). Two consequences:

1. **The Pact should be unusually attentive to psychological
   concerns** — crowd behavior, morale, ritual-as-management, the
   psychological effects of information control — more so than a
   generic legal code would be. When working out a "true rationale" in
   `HYPOTHESES.md`, prefer a crowd-psychology/behavioral-engineering
   explanation over a generic efficiency one when both would fit. The
   cleaning ritual, the wallscreen, the "hollow due process" pattern,
   and controlled schooling are this Pact's real center of gravity, not
   incidental texture.
2. **Victor's own psychological state is deliberately left unclear** —
   he may be a sociopath or have some other disorder shaping the
   writing. Never state or confirm a diagnosis anywhere in the Pact's
   text. Instead let it surface rarely and only as tonal uncanniness: an
   unnervingly clinical moment about something that should carry real
   weight, or an oddly over-precise focus on a behavioral-control
   mechanism where a normal legislator would stay vague. A handful of
   such moments across the whole Pact, not a running tic — it should
   read as a discoverable pattern on close reading, not a gimmick.

## Structural fragility: the seeds of eventual failure (guiding principle, 2026-09-01)

We already know how the wider saga ends: this system of control doesn't
hold — not just in Silo 18, but as a repeated pattern across the fifty
silos. However psychologically astute Victor's design is, it shouldn't
be bulletproof; it should carry a genuine, quiet structural blind spot,
not just the rare deliberate "seam" (which is about discoverability, not
about the design actually being weak). Consistent with real failure
modes of behaviorist social engineering: it assumes citizens stay
legible and predictable indefinitely, and — connecting directly to
authorial drift below — it assumes successors will understand *why* a
mechanism works and not just imitate its form. A system this coherent
should still be the kind of thing that was always going to crack given
long enough and the wrong combination of people; that's more honest to
the source material than an unbeatable design would be, and it's a
different, structural-level companion to the "Restraint" and "seams"
principles above, not a restatement of them.

## Anna Thurman: the secondary author

Anna Thurman also had a major hand in drafting the Pact — secondary to
Victor's (see `CANON.md` section 17b for the full canon basis: she's
shown co-writing the Pact in the show, and is characterized across both
book and show as technically/formally skilled, personally compassionate
at the individual scale, and simultaneously complicit in and a
rationalizer of the systemic harm around her).

**Practical effect on drafting**: there are now two blended authorial
voices, not one.

- **Victor's hand**: cold, systemic, behavioral-engineering-minded (see
  above).
- **Anna's hand**: the Pact's warmer, more personal-scale,
  aphoristic-scriptural register (e.g. "Mothers are a vessel to deliver
  our children into the world..."). Weight her influence toward Articles
  touching kinship, family, marriage, reproduction, and grief (3, 13,
  18, and any family-facing due-process language).
- **Important — don't read her as a softening counterweight to Victor.**
  Per her own characterization (genuinely compassionate individually,
  yet complicit and self-deceiving about systemic harm), her
  contributions should read as *sincere* but function as *legitimizing
  camouflage*: a warmer tone that makes Victor's same cold mechanisms
  more persuasive and less suspicious, whether or not she consciously
  intends that. A clause she pushed for in good faith (e.g. letting a
  condemned offender's family make representations after sentencing)
  can still be functionally hollow, per the "hollow due process" pattern
  above — her sincerity and the clause's real effect are independent.
- **A flagged candidate "seam"** (see the meta-principle below): given
  her own book history of destroying a marriage through manipulation, an
  oddly over-earnest passage on marital fidelity or a parent's grief
  would read, to a reader who knows that history, as uncomfortably
  ironic. Not yet assigned to a specific Article.

## Overall register

**"A text somewhere between the Old Testament and the United States
Constitution."** Statutory bones, scriptural cadence. In practice:

- Default voice is dense, impersonal, mandatory: *"All citizens must...,"
  "No citizen shall...," "[X] is vested in...,"* not second person, not
  casual.
- Occasionally — at thematic high points (founding, reproduction/the
  lottery, communal identity, the Cleaning) — let a clause drop into
  aphoristic, parallelist, quasi-devotional phrasing. Precedent from the
  show: *"Mothers are a vessel to deliver our children into the world,
  just as the Silo is a vessel for the people."* Use sparingly — it's a
  register shift, not the baseline. Article 1 already does this well
  (e.g. the "kept faith with what they were given" line); match that
  frequency, not more.

## The load-bearing trick: hollow due process

The single most useful pattern found: procedure that *sounds* like real
recourse but, read closely, offers none. Show precedent: an offense is
punished "swiftly and severely, without trial," and while the offender's
family may make representations *after the fact*, that "will not affect
the initial term imposed." Use this shape deliberately wherever the Pact
touches Judicial/Sheriff power (Articles 6, 7, 16 especially) — formal-
sounding rights of hearing, appeal, or representation that are real
enough to cite, hollow enough to never actually change an outcome. This
is the throughline that makes the Pact's institutional capture (Bernard,
IT, Judicial) feel earned rather than stated. Per the section above:
this specific clause (family representations after the fact) is a good
candidate to attribute to **Anna** as the sincere-but-camouflaging
author, with Victor's utilitarian calculus responsible for its actual
toothlessness — a small, concrete case of the two-authors model in
practice.

## Structural conventions already locked in (`pact.sty` + Article 1)

- **Article** = chapter, **Arabic**-numbered ("Article 9," not "Article
  IX") — matches the show's own prop-text convention exactly.
- **Section** = numbered subdivision within an Article ("Section 3"),
  printed as "Section N." — also Arabic, also matches the show.
- **Clauses**: lettered `(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)`... via the `clauses`
  environment; nested `(i)`, `(ii)`... via `subclauses`.
- Every Article opens with a one-line italic `\articlenote{}` epigraph
  framing its purpose in slightly more human, less statutory language
  than the clauses that follow (see Article 1's opening line). Keep
  doing this — it's a good release valve before the dense legal prose
  starts.
- **Cross-references are written in prose, not bare numbers**: "under
  Article 9," "as in Article 16," "see Article 15, Section 3" — never a
  bare "(9)" or "§9." This matches both Article 1's existing style and
  the show's own phrasing ("as detailed in Section 11").
- **Numbers spelled out** in running prose for the felt weight of statute
  language ("one hundred and forty years," "one hundred and
  forty-four levels"), even though Article/Section *labels* themselves
  are numerals. Keep this split consistent.

## The Amendment Log: authorial drift over time (guiding principle, 2026-09-01)

Victor and Anna wrote the 22 founding Articles once, at one moment. The
back-matter Amendment Log spans the whole of the silo's history since —
however many years that turns out to be once `FACTS.md`'s open founding-
timeline item is resolved — under many different Mayors and Judicial
heads, none of them a psychologist-architect with Victor's specific
genius. **The Amendment Log should not sound like Victor and Anna wrote
it too.** Later amendments should show their tricks — "hollow due
process," gesture-without-specifics, the aphoristic register — applied
more clumsily, more mechanically, by people who inherited the form
without the original insight: a realistic picture of how institutions
imitate their own founding documents without understanding them, and a
concrete instance of "Structural fragility" above (successors imitating
form, not function). This also gives a lever on the open founding-
timeline question: the *amount* of visible drift in the Amendment Log is
itself evidence for how much time has passed, and can inform — not just
follow from — whatever that resolution ends up being.

## Content-level guardrails

- **Never let the Order leak through.** Where a topic brushes up against
  Order-level secrets (cleaning-suit sabotage, the Safeguard, inter-silo
  knowledge, the Pact's true authorship), the Pact's own language should
  stay solemn and vague rather than technical — it can gesture at "such
  further procedure as [IT] alone is instructed in" (Article 1's own
  phrasing for the sensor-maintenance passage) without ever explaining
  what that procedure is. That gesture-without-specifics move is now a
  reusable device — use it again wherever an Article touches something
  genuinely secret.
- **Prefer institutional/record-keeping framing over modern
  therapeutic framing**, especially in Article 19 (Health): doctors,
  certificates, official causes of death, screenings — not counselors or
  therapy-speak. Consistent with the doctor-falsifies-the-record texture
  found in canon research.
- **Skip "Intactica."** Per the user's decision, this TV-only invented
  rite is not used anywhere in this project — don't reach for it as
  texture for Article 3 or 18.
