\chapter{Of Marriage, the Lottery, and the Right of Birth}
\label{art:marriage}

\articlenote{In which is set down the marriage bond, the lottery by which the silo permits the bearing of children, and the right of every citizen to petition to be a parent, subject to the silo's need to preserve its population in balance.}

\section{The Marriage Bond}
\label{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}

A marriage is the binding of two citizens, made before Judicial and entered in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, by which the two become one household and are held to each other and to the silo in such further duties as this Pact sets down. It is the ordinary way in which two citizens undertake, before the silo and in its hearing, to share their labor, their ration, their dwelling, and, where the lottery grants it, the bearing and the raising of a child. It asks of each party a constancy the silo itself depends upon no less than it depends on breath and water: a household divided against itself cannot be trusted to raise a child in the silo's peace, and a silo built of divided households cannot be trusted to keep its own.

\begin{clauses}
\item A marriage is dissolved only by the death of one of the parties, or by mutual petition granted by Judicial under such terms as Judicial finds just. A marriage dissolved remains entered in the Judicial archive, and does not erase the citizenship rolls of either party.
\item Two citizens may petition to be married before Judicial; Judicial shall not refuse such a petition save where one of the parties is already married or is a ward unable to consent under \artref{art:citizenship}.
\item A marriage entered creates no obligation of children; the marriage bond stands as a household association alone, until and unless the partners petition the lottery under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:lottery-for-children}.
\item Housing gives a married household one dwelling under \artref{art:housing}, fit for the two of them and for such children as the lottery grants them, and does not reckon the two as two citizens living singly.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Lottery for Children}
\label{sec:marriage:lottery-for-children}

The silo can feed only so many, and the number it can feed is fixed by its stores and by what its Departments can do, and not by what its citizens would wish. The bearing of children is therefore governed by a lottery drawn once in the year: citizens petition to bear a child, and the lottery draws from among them such number as the silo can take in.

\begin{clauses}
\item Any citizen, whether married or unmarried, or any two citizens married to one another under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}, may petition to the lottery to be granted the right to bear a child. A petition shall name the petitioner, or both petitioners, and the intended bearing parent, if different.
\item The lottery is drawn by Judicial, with the Mayor and the Head of Supply present, once yearly. The number of places granted is the number of names closed in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship} since the last drawing, and no more; the Mayor, having heard the Head of Supply and the other Heads, may set the number lower where the stores or the Departments' means for the year to come, weighed against the count under \artref{art:citizenship}, require it, and may not set it higher, by any reasoning or in any season.
\item A citizen granted a place in the lottery has leave to bear a child, and the measure fitted under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure} is quieted for such time as permits one conception and bearing. The span allowed for conception is no fewer than three seasons and no longer than one full year, as the Infirmary under \artref{art:health} sets it, and is entered with the place.
\item A citizen drawn in the lottery may pass the leave to bear a child to a married partner, to a sibling, or to another citizen of the same household, Judicial entering and allowing the passing; and the citizen who receives it need not be married or joined to any other.
\item No citizen is compelled to bear a child, and a citizen drawn in the lottery is not bound to use what was drawn. A place not used within the span allowed is given up, and the measure is restored; and the place so given up is counted against the next drawing and is not lost to the silo.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Right of Birth and the Span Allowed for Conception}
\label{sec:marriage:right-of-birth-and-the-span-allowed}

A citizen granted the lottery's leave bears the child within the span given, and not after it; a bearing outside that span is a bearing without the lottery, whatever the cause. A child born within that span and so attested is named and entered in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship} as any citizen is.

\begin{clauses}
\item A child conceived within the span allowed and born to a citizen holding the lottery right shall be named and entered in the census by Judicial within ten days of birth, exactly as any other citizen is named.
\item A child born to a citizen who did not win the lottery, or born outside the span allowed for conception, is still a citizen and shall be named and entered in the rolls; but the citizen or citizens responsible for the child's birth are answerable under \artref{art:crimes}, as set down in \secrefhere{sec:marriage:unsanctioned-children-and-the-answering-for-unlicensed-birth}.
\item The Infirmary keeps, for every citizen of bearing years, the record of the measure under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure} --- its fitting, its quieting, and its restoring --- and the days of every span granted. The record is kept in the Judicial archive under \artref{art:judicial}, and is opened to Judicial alone, and to the citizen named.
\end{clauses}

\section{Unsanctioned Children and the Answering for Unlicensed Birth}
\label{sec:marriage:unsanctioned-children-and-the-answering-for-unlicensed-birth}

A child conceived and borne without the lottery's sanction is no less a citizen than any other; the child receives full standing, a name, and entry in the census. But the adult or adults responsible for the child's conception are answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for the breach of the silo's population law.

\begin{clauses}
\item Judicial finds which citizen or citizens stand answerable for a child conceived without the lottery, hearing the Infirmary's record of the birth, the parent or parents, and whatever else is brought; and the citizens so found are answerable under \artref{art:crimes}, and no others, and the finding is entered with its reasons.
\item The bearing of a child without the lottery does not bar a citizen from petitioning the lottery thereafter; and a citizen who has so borne a child and is later granted a place may use it as any citizen may.
\item No child born outside the lottery shall be treated differently from any other citizen in the silo's law, ration, housing, or any other respect. What is owed is owed by the citizens answerable, and never by the child, and no officer and no Department may visit it upon the child in ration, in dwelling, in schooling, or in the naming of a trade.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Administration of the Lottery}
\label{sec:marriage:administration-of-the-lottery}

The lottery is drawn once in the year, its places are cried before the drawing, and every part of the drawing is entered in the Judicial archive under \artref{art:judicial}. No matter this Pact governs touches a citizen nearer, and the only guard against a hand upon the draw is that the draw be made where every citizen may watch it.

\begin{clauses}
\item The lottery shall be drawn in the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, or at such place as the Assembly determines, and any citizen may witness the drawing. The names of the winners shall be announced publicly and entered in the Judicial archive.
\item The drawing is made with tokens and not with names. Mechanical cuts the tokens under \artref{art:mechanical} to a pattern the Judge keeps, of one wood, one size, and one weight, and each token bears a number and nothing else. The Clerk of Judicial enters against each petition the number of the token given to it, and that entry is the only place in the silo where the name and the number stand together before the drawing is done. The tokens are kept between drawings in the Judicial archive under \artref{art:records}, sealed under \secref{art:records}{sec:records:seal}, and are told over before the seal is broken and again before it is set.
\item Before the tokens are put in, the vessel is shown empty to the Assembly, turned over, and held up. The Clerk counts the tokens into it aloud, one by one, and the count is cried and entered. The vessel is then closed and turned not fewer than three times in the Assembly's sight, and the tokens are drawn from it one at a time by a citizen of majority chosen by the Assembly from among those watching, who is not of a household that petitioned and is not of Judicial. The citizen who draws does not look into the vessel, and no officer of Judicial lays a hand upon the vessel while the drawing is made.
\item Each token drawn is cried by its number, held up, and laid in sight upon the Clerk's table, and no number is cried twice. When the places of that year are filled the drawing stops, and the tokens remaining are counted out of the vessel aloud before the Assembly and that count compared with the count entered before the drawing. Where the two counts do not agree, the drawing is void, no place granted by it stands, and the Clerk carries the matter to Judicial the same day.
\item Only when the drawing is done, and the counts agree, does the Clerk read the names against the numbers cried, in the hearing of the Assembly. The numbers drawn, the names read against them, the count before, the count after, and the name of the citizen who drew are entered in the Judicial archive together, and any citizen may read that entry there.
\item A citizen who believes a hand was laid upon the drawing may carry the matter to Judicial; and Judicial, finding cause, orders the drawing made again, and enters what was found and what was done.
\item A place granted by the lottery is the only thing that quiets the measure under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure}. No other hand may order it quieted --- not the Mayor's, not a Head of Department's, not Judicial's own --- and a Physician who quiets it upon any other word is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}.
\item A citizen not drawn may petition again at every drawing thereafter, and nothing is entered against the citizen for having petitioned; the lottery is drawn once in the year without exception, and no citizen is ever barred from the petitioning of it for good.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Keeping of the Silo's Number}
\label{sec:marriage:keeping-of-the-silos-number}

The silo endures only so long as its number holds steady from one generation to the next. The lottery is how the silo holds it: a death opens a place, a birth fills it, and the silo is neither more nor fewer for the exchange. This is not set down out of hardness. It is set down because a silo that grows beyond its stores does not grow; it starves, and then it is fewer than it was, and the loss falls upon children who had no part in the choosing.

\begin{clauses}
\item This Article takes from no citizen the hope of a child; it governs only when. A citizen not drawn in one year petitions in the next, and the lottery is drawn without fail, in every year, for so long as the silo stands.
\item What the silo can do for children is weighed by the Mayor and the Heads of Departments against the count of the rolls; as the stores run short, or a Department's means fall away, the number of places may be held under the number of names closed since the last drawing, and may never be set above it. The Mayor cries the year's number of places before the drawing, with the count of names closed beside it, that any citizen may see the one against the other.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Fitting and Keeping of the Measure}
\label{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure}

Every citizen bears, from before the age at which a child might conceive or beget, a measure fitted by the Infirmary against conception, that no child be born to the silo but by the lottery's grant. The measure is the silo's and not the citizen's; it is fitted, kept, quieted, and restored by the Infirmary alone, and this Pact treats it as it treats the ration and the dwelling: a thing every citizen carries alike, and none by choice.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Infirmary shall fit the measure to every child before the twelfth naming-day, or at such earlier age as the Head Physician judges the child's growth to require, and shall enter the fitting in the child's record under \artref{art:health}. The child's household shall be told the day of the fitting, and the child shall be told what the measure is and why it is carried, in the words of this section and no others.
\item The fitting is among the treatments this Pact compels under \secref{art:health}{sec:health:charge-and-the-physicians}, and neither the child nor the household may refuse it; a household that withholds a child from the fitting is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for withholding a child from schooling under \artref{art:schooling}.
\item The measure is quieted only upon a lottery's grant under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:lottery-for-children}, by a Physician, for the span that Section provides, and is restored at that span's end whether or not a child was conceived; the Physician enters both the quieting and the restoring upon the citizen's record.
\item A measure found to have failed, whether by the Infirmary's own examination or by the bearing of a child without a grant, is reported to Judicial upon the Form \artref{art:health} provides. Where the failure is the measure's own and not the citizen's doing, Judicial's finding under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:unsanctioned-children-and-the-answering-for-unlicensed-birth} shall say so, and the citizens concerned are not answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for the birth, though the child is entered in the rolls as any other and the place it fills is counted against the next drawing.
\item No citizen may remove, disable, or attempt the quieting of a measure, the citizen's own or another's, save a Physician acting under this section; to do so is to take the lottery's grant without the lottery, and is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} exactly as standing responsible for a birth without the grant.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Sanctioning of a Relationship and the Making of a Household}
\label{sec:marriage:sanctioning-of-a-relationship-and-the-making-of-a-household}

Two citizens who keep company with the intent of a shared household, and are not yet married under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}, may declare that intent before Judicial, and Judicial shall enter it in the rolls as a sanctioned relationship. The declaration binds neither citizen to marriage, and may be withdrawn by either before Judicial at any time.

\begin{clauses}
\item A sanctioned relationship is entered in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship} and reported to Housing under \artref{art:housing}, that the two citizens may be lodged as one household where Housing's stores allow; two citizens lodged as one household without a declaration under this section or a marriage under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond} are lodged unsanctioned, and Housing shall lodge them apart upon discovering it.
\item A sanctioned relationship confers no standing in the lottery beyond a citizen's own; two citizens so sanctioned petition the lottery under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:lottery-for-children} as any citizen petitions.
\item The declaration asks nothing of the two citizens beyond their names. Judicial neither inquires into nor records the reason for it, nor for its withdrawal, and no citizen is answerable under this Pact for keeping company undeclared, save as this section provides for lodging; though Judicial may, where a birth without the grant is in question under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:unsanctioned-children-and-the-answering-for-unlicensed-birth}, take the fact of a relationship undeclared into its finding of who stands responsible.
\item A citizen already married under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond} may not declare a relationship under this section with another, nor may a ward under \artref{art:citizenship} declare one at all.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Widowed, the Orphaned, and the Duties of Kin}
\label{sec:marriage:widowed-the-orphaned-and-the-duties-of-kin}

A household is the silo's smallest keeping, and what a household cannot keep the silo must. This section sets down what falls to kin when a household is broken by death, by the mines, or by the cleaning, and what falls to the silo when kin cannot bear it.

\begin{clauses}
\item Upon the death of a married citizen, the surviving partner keeps the household's dwelling until Housing under \artref{art:housing} reassigns it by the household's new size, and no reassignment shall be made within one turn of the season of the death save at the survivor's own asking.
\item A child whose household is broken by the death, the sentencing, or the cleaning of every adult in it is a ward of the silo under this section, and shall be placed by Judicial with kin who will take the child, or, failing kin, with a household of the same floor that petitions for the child, or, failing both, in the keeping of the nursery and school under \artref{art:schooling} until majority. A child so placed carries the child's own ration under \artref{art:supply} into the household that takes them.
\item The household of a citizen sentenced to the mines keeps its dwelling and its ration for the term, less the sentenced citizen's own share, and the sentenced citizen's kin are not answerable for the offense; the same holds for the household of a citizen sent to clean, as \artref{art:cleaning} provides.
\item A citizen who takes in a widowed parent, an orphaned kin, or a kin retired under \artref{art:health} may petition Housing for quarters enlarged to the household's new size, and Housing shall weigh such a petition before any petition for a change of quarters made for preference alone.
\item No kin is bound by this Pact to take in another, and no citizen is answerable for declining; but a citizen who has taken in kin or a ward, and turns them out without notice to Housing and to Judicial, is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}, the place of that kin in the rolls having been set by the taking-in.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Naming}
\label{sec:marriage:naming}

A citizen's life in this Pact begins not at birth but at naming, and the naming is a thing done before the silo and entered in its rolls, not a thing a household does for itself alone.

\begin{clauses}
\item Within ten days of a birth, the child's household shall bring the child before the Clerk of Judicial, or before a deputy of Judicial sent to the household's floor for the purpose, together with the physician or midwife who attended the birth. The household speaks the name it has chosen; the physician or midwife attests the birth, the day of it, and the name of the citizen who bore the child; and the Clerk enters the name, the day of naming, the household, and the floor in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}.
\item The citizen who bore the child holds the child throughout, or, where that citizen cannot, the married partner of that citizen or such kin as the household names; the Clerk does not take the child out of that citizen's arms, and no office handles a child at a naming.
\item The Clerk asks what the child is called, and the household says the name. The Clerk says the name back, entire, with the household name and the floor, and asks whether it is rightly said; the household says that it is; and the Clerk says it a third time in the entering of it. These are the words of a naming, and with the Preamble read after them they are the whole of what this Pact requires to be said. A household may say whatever else it wishes, and the Clerk enters none of it.
\item The archivist of Judicial makes the child's card under \secref{art:records}{sec:records:occupant-card} within one cycle of the naming and gives it into the hand of the citizen who held the child; the card is the child's from the naming, and is kept for the child by the household until the child is of an age to keep it.
\item Nothing is given to the Clerk, to the deputy, or to Judicial at a naming, and nothing is asked for; a Clerk or deputy who takes anything at a naming, or who lets it be understood that something would be welcome, is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}. What the household gives is the name.
\item A child bears a given name of the household's choosing, and after it the household name of the citizen who bore the child, or of the married partner of that citizen where the household so chooses at the naming; a household name once entered is not changed save by marriage under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}, and then only at the citizen's own asking.
\item A child may be given the name of any citizen living or dead, and no name is reserved to any floor, trade, or office; but a child may not be given a name the Clerk finds to be no name at all, or to be a word this Pact forbids under \artref{art:publicorder}, and the household shall choose again before the ten days are out.
\item The naming is read aloud by the Clerk at the next sitting of the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, with every other naming since the last, that the silo know who has come into it; and the Preamble of this Pact is read at the naming itself, as that Preamble provides, by the Clerk or the deputy, in the hearing of the household.
\item A child born to a citizen sent to clean, or serving a term in the mines, or dead before the naming, is named by the surviving household, or, where none survives, by Judicial, and is entered exactly as any other; the rolls do not record the manner of a parent's absence against a child's name.
\item A naming is not withheld for want of chits, for the standing of a household, for an unlicensed birth under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:unsanctioned-children-and-the-answering-for-unlicensed-birth}, or for any cause this section does not name; and a naming once entered is not undone, whatever is afterward found of the birth, the household answering for the birth and the child answering for nothing.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Dissolution of a Marriage}
\label{sec:marriage:dissolution-of-a-marriage}

A marriage is not lightly made in this Pact and is not lightly unmade; but a household that cannot hold together serves the silo worse divided under one roof than parted under two, and this Pact provides the parting.

\begin{clauses}
\item Two married citizens who would part petition Judicial together under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}; where only one would part, that citizen petitions alone, and Judicial hears the other before ruling. Judicial grants the petition where it finds the household cannot be kept, and may refuse it, once, where it finds the parting sought in haste; a petition refused may be brought again after one turn of the season, and is not refused a second time.
\item Between the petition and the ruling, not less than one turn of the season passes, save where Judicial finds that one partner endangers the other, in which case Housing under \artref{art:housing} lodges them apart at once and the season runs regardless.
\item Upon dissolution, the dwelling is kept by the partner with whom the children of the household, if any, are placed under this section, or, where there are none, by the partner Housing under \artref{art:housing} finds the dwelling better suited to; the other is lodged anew by Housing within one cycle. The goods of the household are divided by the partners' own agreement entered with the Clerk, or, failing agreement, by Judicial's ruling, and the chits of each remain each's own under \artref{art:commerce}.
\item The children of a dissolved marriage are placed with one partner or the other by Judicial, weighing the children's years, the trade and floor of each partner, and the children's own wish where they are of an age to have one; the partner without the children may see them at such times as Judicial sets, and neither partner may be barred from a child's naming-day, schooling, or sickbed by the other.
\item A marriage dissolved does not undo a lottery's grant already given to the household, nor a child already conceived under it; a grant unused at the dissolution is forfeited, and the measure of each partner restored under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure}.
\item Neither partner is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for the parting itself, and the dissolution is entered in the archive as a fact and not as a fault; but a partner who conceals from Judicial a matter bearing upon the placement of a child is answerable as for a false bringing under \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:offenses-against-another-citizen}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Ward and the Household's Duty}
\label{sec:marriage:ward-and-the-households-duty}

A household that has a child has a duty the silo lends it and may take back.

\begin{clauses}
\item A household owes a ward the drawing of the ward's ration under \artref{art:supply}, a place in the household's dwelling, attendance at the schooling of \artref{art:schooling}, the care of the Infirmary under \artref{art:health} when the ward is sick, and correction short of harm; and the household answers under \artref{art:crimes} for a ward's grave breach of this Pact where the breach came of the household's own neglect.
\item A household that cannot or will not keep a ward --- by the death or sentencing of its adults, by want, or by the household's own petition --- shall say so to Judicial, and the ward is placed under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:widowed-the-orphaned-and-the-duties-of-kin} as a ward of the silo; a household that keeps a ward in name and abandons the ward in fact is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for the neglect of assigned labor, the keeping of a ward being a labor the silo assigns.
\item A ward may be taken from a household by Judicial where the household is found, upon a report under \secref{art:schooling}{sec:schooling:correction-of-a-child}, or under \artref{art:health}, to have harmed the ward or to have kept the ward from schooling, from the Infirmary, or from the ration; the household is heard before the ward is taken, save where the danger to the ward will not wait, and the ward is placed under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:widowed-the-orphaned-and-the-duties-of-kin}.
\item A ward of the silo placed with a household under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:widowed-the-orphaned-and-the-duties-of-kin} is that household's ward for every purpose of this section from the day of placing, and the household draws the ward's ration and answers for the ward exactly as for a child of its own.
\item The nursery and school under \artref{art:schooling} that keeps a ward of the silo whom no household has taken stands as the ward's household for every purpose of this Pact, and the teacher who has charge of the ward brings the ward's matters before Judicial as \artref{art:schooling} provides.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Degrees Within Which a Marriage Is Not Made}
\label{sec:marriage:degrees-within-which-a-marriage-is-not-made}

The silo's households are to be plainly ordered, and the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship} are to say without inquiry who is kin to whom. Therefore a marriage is not made, and a child is not borne together, between citizens standing within the degrees this section names; and the Clerk of Judicial reads the two lines before any petition under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond} is granted.

\begin{clauses}
\item A marriage is not made between a parent and a child; between two citizens of one parent or of two, whether of the whole blood or the half; between a grandparent and a grandchild; between a citizen and the child of that citizen's brother or sister; or between the children of two citizens who were themselves the children of one parent or of two. Within these degrees Judicial refuses the petition, and there is no leave, no grace, and no exception, by the Mayor's hand or Judicial's own.
\item Before granting a petition under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:marriage-bond}, the Clerk of Judicial reads the line of each citizen from the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship} against the line of the other, and enters upon the petition that the reading was made and that the degrees are clear; a petition granted without that entry is void, and is entered void, and the citizens may petition again.
\item Where two citizens petition the lottery together under \secrefhere{sec:marriage:lottery-for-children}, or where a place is passed under that Section from one citizen to another who will bear or beget with the passer, the Clerk reads the two lines as for a marriage; and a place is not granted and is not passed where the degrees of this section stand between them.
\item A ward entered in a household under \artref{art:citizenship} is kin, for the keeping of this section, by the line the rolls give the ward, and not by the household that keeps the ward. Where a ward's line is not known, the Clerk enters that it is not known, and the petition is not refused for what the rolls do not say; and a marriage so granted stands, whatever is afterward learned.
\item Where a marriage is found, after its making, to stand within the degrees of this section, Judicial dissolves it, and enters the dissolving and the cause. Where neither citizen knew, neither is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}; where either knew, that citizen is answerable. A child already born of such a marriage is a citizen entire, is entered in the rolls as any other child is entered, and has the cause set nowhere beside its name; and no officer, Head, teacher, or master may put the matter to that citizen or in that citizen's hearing.
\item A citizen may read the citizen's own line at the office of the Clerk, and may petition Judicial to correct it under \artref{art:citizenship}; and a citizen about to petition for a marriage may ask the Clerk, before the petition is made, whether the degrees stand between that citizen and another, and the Clerk answers yes or no, and answers nothing else.
\end{clauses}
