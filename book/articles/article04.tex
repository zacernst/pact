\chapter{Of the Office of Mayor}
\label{art:mayor}

\articlenote{In which is set down the office of the Mayor, the civilian head of the silo, and the powers by which this office governs in concert with Judicial and Assembly.}

\section{The Office of Mayor}
\label{sec:mayor:office-of-mayor}

The silo is governed by a civilian Mayor, who stands as the highest executive authority of the silo, answerable to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly} and to the requirements of this Pact as set down in \artref{art:founding}. The Mayor is chosen by election and confirmed into office through the process set down in \artref{art:succession}. The Mayor resides and keeps the office in the Up Top, in the Mayor's chambers, alongside the office of the Deputy Mayor.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor is a citizen, subject to every duty this Pact lays upon any other citizen, save where an Article expressly grants the Mayor some further power or authority in the conduct of the office itself.
\item The Mayor holds office for a term of five years from confirmation, and continues in office until a successor is elected and confirmed under \artref{art:succession}, an election being held as that term ends; a Mayor may stand again at the end of any term. Within a term the Mayor is removable by a vote of the Assembly as \artref{art:assembly} provides.
\item Where the Mayor is absent, or is unable to keep the office, the Deputy Mayor shall act as Mayor with all the powers of the office, for such time as the Mayor's absence continues, under \artref{art:succession}.
\item The office of Mayor is barred to a citizen afflicted with the Syndrome, and vacated by it, as \secref{art:labor}{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} provides for every office of the silo; upon such a resignation the Deputy Mayor assumes the office under \artref{art:succession}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Mayor's Powers of Confirmation and Appointment}
\label{sec:mayor:mayors-powers-of-confirmation-and-appointment}

The Mayor, in concert with this Pact and the Assembly, holds the power to confirm the heads of every Department and the single Judge who heads the Department of Judicial. The Sheriff is appointed by the Mayor alone, though the Mayor shall weigh the advice of those in and out of the Sheriff's own office. In all cases, the Mayor's judgment is final, and no other office may override such confirmation or appointment once given.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor confirms the Head of each Department --- Mechanical, Information Technology, Supply, Mines, and all others established under this Pact --- by written order, countersigned, where a recommendation was made, by the officer who made it, and filed in the Judicial archive under \artref{art:judicial}.
\item The appointment of the Sheriff is the sole discretion of the Mayor: the outgoing Sheriff may make a recommendation, and the Mayor shall hear it in good faith, but the Mayor is not bound by that recommendation and may appoint any citizen of majority, whether from within the Sheriff's office or without, as the Mayor sees fit.
\item The confirmation of the Judge is made jointly by the Mayor and the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}; the Judge is confirmed once in a lifetime and stands until death or removal by the Assembly. The name confirmed is the name the Officers of Judicial put forward under \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:charge-and-the-judge}, and the Mayor confirms that name or declines it, and names no other.
\item No Mayor shall put a confirmed Head of Department out of office for a disagreement in the ordinary conduct of the silo's affairs. A Head is put out only upon a finding of Judicial that the Head has broken this Pact or has failed in a chief duty of the office, and such a finding is carried to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, which alone removes.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Mayor's Orders and Emergency Authority}
\label{sec:mayor:mayors-orders-and-emergency-authority}

The Mayor may issue standing orders, written and filed in the Judicial archive, directing the conduct of the silo's ordinary affairs in concert with the heads of the relevant Departments. No order of the Mayor may contradict this Pact, nor may any standing order be given in secret --- all orders are entered in the records that any citizen may examine under \artref{art:judicial}. In an emergency the Mayor holds the further powers \artref{art:emergency} sets down, and no others.

\begin{clauses}
\item An order of the Mayor takes effect when issued and is binding on the head of the named Department; a Department head who disputes an order may refer the matter to Judicial for a ruling, and the order does not bind until Judicial has ruled.
\item The Mayor may not order any act that would require citizens to violate the Pact, nor direct the commission of any offense under \artref{art:crimes}. A citizen ordered to break the Pact may refuse the order and refer the matter to Judicial under \artref{art:judicial}.
\item The Mayor may bind the silo itself by a promise or an undertaking --- as, for example, the parting of the stores in a hard shortage, or a burden laid upon a Department to run past the term of the Mayor who laid it --- only with the Assembly's consent, given under \artref{art:assembly} and entered with the promise beside it.
\item The Mayor directs the use of the silo's major reserves --- water, air, the generator's power, the stores of food --- in ordinary times as \artref{art:supply} provides, the Head of Supply setting the ration under the Mayor's general approval; in emergency, \artref{art:emergency} governs.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Mayor and Judicial}
\label{sec:mayor:mayor-and-judicial}

The Mayor and the Department of Judicial stand level with one another, and neither commands the other in what this Pact has given it. The silo's ordinary business is the Mayor's: the work of the Departments, the parting of the stores, the orders of the day, and whatever this Pact has not spoken for. The inquiry into offenses, the trying of citizens, and the saying of what this Pact means where this Pact is silent are Judicial's. Neither office reaches into the other's, and neither may be made to answer to the other for what it has rightly done.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor may refer a matter to Judicial for a ruling if the Pact is silent, and \artref{art:judicial}'s procedure applies to it.
\item The Judge may, by written order, direct the Mayor to enforce a Judicial finding or to comply with a ruling made under \artref{art:judicial}; the Mayor must obey such a written order or refer the dispute to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}.
\item Where the Mayor and Judicial fall out over what this Pact means, the matter is put to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, which may be called in extraordinary sitting to settle it, and whose word settles it.
\item No order of the Mayor's reaches Judicial's inquiry or Judicial's charging; Judicial may inquire into any citizen and charge any citizen, the Mayor not excepted, for any offense under \artref{art:crimes}, and asks no leave of the Mayor to do it.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Mayor and the Assembly}
\label{sec:mayor:mayor-and-the-assembly}

The Mayor calls the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly} and sets the order in which its business is heard, as \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:order-of-business} provides; the Mayor does not govern its votes, and does not govern what it may take up. Once in the year the Mayor stands before it and gives an account of the silo. The Mayor may recommend amendments to this Pact under \artref{art:amendment}, and the Assembly may initiate amendments without the Mayor's consent.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor answers to the Assembly for the conduct of the silo's affairs, and the Assembly may question the Mayor upon any matter of the silo's governance or of the parting of its stores, and the Mayor answers in the hearing of the Assembly or says why the answer is withheld.
\item No standing order, no appointment, and no decision of the Mayor's binds the Assembly; and any citizen, or the Assembly itself, may petition for an extraordinary sitting to take up a decision of the Mayor's again.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Office of Deputy Mayor and Succession}
\label{sec:mayor:office-of-deputy-mayor-and-succession}

The Deputy Mayor is the second-highest office of the silo, charged with assisting the Mayor in the conduct of the office and with assuming the mayoralty should the Mayor die, become incapacitated, or be removed by the Assembly. The office of Deputy Mayor, the manner of the Deputy's own coming to it, and the whole order of succession are set down in full in \artref{art:succession}, which this Article's other provisions bind exactly as they bind the Mayor's own office.

\section{The Forgiveness Holiday}
\label{sec:mayor:forgiveness-holiday}

The Mayor may declare a Forgiveness Holiday, of such duration and upon such notice as the Mayor sets, that a citizen holding a relic or restricted instrument or device forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder} may surrender it, in the manner that Article provides, without fear of answering for the mere keeping, making, or use of the thing surrendered.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen who surrenders a relic or restricted instrument or device under \artref{art:publicorder} during a Forgiveness Holiday is not answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for the keeping, making, or use of the thing surrendered. The thing itself is examined and classified exactly as any other surrendered relic under \artref{art:publicorder}, and a thing classified among the gravest is destroyed or sealed as that Article provides, whatever grace is given the citizen who surrendered it.
\item This grace covers only the keeping, making, or use of the thing surrendered, and no other offense the surrender, or Judicial's examination of the thing surrendered, may bring to light; a citizen who surrenders a relic is answerable exactly as any other citizen for any other violation of this Pact the surrender reveals.
\item A Forgiveness Holiday declared under this section is announced to the whole silo before it begins, that no citizen surrender in ignorance of the grace being offered, and its ending is announced with equal notice; a thing surrendered after a Forgiveness Holiday has ended is answered under \artref{art:crimes} exactly as though no holiday had been declared.
\item The Mayor may declare a Forgiveness Holiday at such interval, and for such cause, as the Mayor judges the silo's peace to require, and need not state more than the fact of its declaration and its duration.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Seating of a Mayor and the Oaths of Office}
\label{sec:mayor:seating-of-a-mayor-and-the-oaths-of-office}

A Mayor is confirmed in one hour, before the Assembly, in a fixed form; and the same hour binds every other citizen whom this Pact seats in an office.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Mayor elected under \artref{art:succession} is seated at the sitting of the Assembly at which the election is counted, or at the next sitting the hall can be kept where the count is carried under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:vote-and-its-counting}, and not before. The Clerk of Judicial reads the count, the Judge enters that the count was read, and the citizen elected then stands.
\item The Preamble of this Pact is read aloud, entire, before the oath is taken, by the Clerk of Judicial in the hearing of the Assembly, as the Preamble itself provides; the reading is not shortened for the length of the sitting, nor for the Mayor having heard it before.
\item The oath is sworn in these words and no others, the citizen standing, the Judge reading each line and the citizen speaking it after: \emph{I am} [the citizen's name], \emph{of} [the citizen's floor and household]. \emph{I take the office of} [the office]. \emph{I will defend this Pact against any hand that would breach it, my own hand first. I will enforce it alike upon the floor I came from and the floor I did not. I will not hold this office above it. I swear it before Judicial and before the Assembly, which may put me out of the office for the breaking of this oath and for no other cause it has not named.}
\item The Judge, the Sheriff, and every Head of Department swear the same oath at the assumption of office, naming the office in the third line, before the Judge, or, where the office sworn is the Judge's own, before the Mayor; the oath is sworn in the hall of Judicial where the Assembly is not sitting, and the Clerk enters the day, the place, and the witnesses. The Preamble is read before the oath of a Judge and of a Head of Department as before the oath of a Mayor, and may be read on the citizen's own floor where the Assembly is not sitting.
\item An officer who will not speak the oath does not hold the office, whatever the election or the confirmation; the Clerk enters the refusal, the office stands vacant, and the vacancy is filled as that office's own Article provides.
\item The oath of office is sworn again at each confirmation into an office, the citizen's oath under \artref{art:citizenship} being sworn once only; and an officer who leaves an office and later takes another swears again for the office taken.
\end{clauses}
