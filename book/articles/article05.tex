\chapter{Of the Deputy Mayor and the Order of Succession}
\label{art:succession}

\articlenote{In which is set down the office of the Deputy Mayor and the manner by which the silo chooses its next Mayor, that the office may never stand vacant and the silo's governance may never pass into uncertainty.}

\section{The Office of Deputy Mayor}
\label{sec:succession:office-of-deputy-mayor}

The Deputy Mayor is the second office of the silo, and stands under the Mayor in ordinary times. The Deputy's duty is to help the Mayor in the conduct of the office, and to hold ready the knowledge and the authority to take up the Mayoralty should the sitting Mayor be unable to keep it, that there be no hour in which the silo has no Mayor. The Deputy Mayor is appointed by the Mayor and confirmed by the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, serves at the Mayor's pleasure and for no longer than the term of the Mayor who appointed the Deputy, and resides in the Up Top alongside the Mayor's chambers; a Mayor confirmed to a further term may appoint the same Deputy again.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Deputy Mayor holds every duty of a citizen under this Pact and is subject to the same law as any other citizen, save where this Article or \artref{art:mayor} expressly grants the Deputy some authority in the conduct of the Deputy's office.
\item The Deputy Mayor reads every record, letter, and standing order of the Mayor's office, and is refused none of them by the Mayor or by any other office, that the Deputy may take up the Mayoralty in any hour without the silo's business stopping while the Deputy learns it.
\item The office of Deputy Mayor is barred to a citizen afflicted with the Syndrome, and vacated by it, as \secref{art:labor}{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} provides; upon such a resignation the Mayor appoints a new Deputy under \secrefhere{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-election-and-succession}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Deputy Mayor's Assumption of the Mayoralty}
\label{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-assumption-of-the-mayoralty}

Upon the death of the sitting Mayor, or upon the Mayor's resignation, or upon the Mayor being removed from office by the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, the Deputy Mayor shall immediately assume the office and powers of the Mayor, and shall serve as Mayor until such time as a new Mayor is elected and confirmed under \secrefhere{sec:succession:election-for-the-next-mayor}.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Deputy Mayor, upon assumption of the Mayoralty, becomes bound by every requirement and power set down in \artref{art:mayor} for the Mayor, and \artref{art:mayor} governs the Deputy's conduct from the moment of assumption forward.
\item A Deputy Mayor who has taken up the Mayoralty may give standing orders, apportion the silo's stores, and do all else the Mayor's office ordinarily does, save one thing: the Deputy confirms no Head of Department and no Judge to hold the office beyond the Deputy's own acting Mayoralty. Such a confirmation waits upon the election and confirming of a Mayor.
\item What a Deputy does in the conduct of the Mayoralty binds the silo as the Mayor's own acts bind it, and is not undone by the coming of a new Mayor, save where this Pact or a ruling of Judicial requires it undone.
\item If the Mayor is absent but not dead or removed --- taken ill, sent as an envoy, or otherwise prevented from the conduct of office for a known or suspected duration --- the Deputy acts as Mayor with all powers including the power to make temporary appointments, such temporary authority ceasing upon the Mayor's return.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Election for the Next Mayor}
\label{sec:succession:election-for-the-next-mayor}

An election for the Mayoralty is held in two cases: at the end of a sitting Mayor's term of five years under \artref{art:mayor}, at the ordinary sitting of the Assembly that falls in that year, which the sitting Mayor convenes as \artref{art:assembly} provides; and when the Mayoralty stands vacant by reason of death, resignation, or removal. In either case no fewer than two citizens shall be proposed for the office. Any citizen of majority standing, with no sentence under \artref{art:crimes} unserved and no restitution ordered there unpaid, may stand for election as Mayor. The Assembly shall conduct the election and determine the process of the same.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Deputy Mayor acting as Mayor in a vacancy does not stand for election to the Mayoralty, that the hand which holds the office through the election have nothing to gain by its outcome.
\item A citizen elected to the Mayoralty must be confirmed by the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly} before taking final office; confirmation shall not be withheld save for cause found by Judicial.
\item The newly elected and confirmed Mayor assumes office and all the powers of the Mayor under \artref{art:mayor} upon confirmation; the outgoing Mayor's term, or the Deputy Mayor's acting Mayoralty, concludes at that moment.
\item Where the election is held for a vacancy, the Assembly, the acting Mayor and the Heads of Departments being heard, shall determine the timing and manner of the election.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Deputy Mayor's Election and Succession}
\label{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-election-and-succession}

The Deputy Mayor is appointed by the sitting Mayor and confirmed by the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}. Should the Deputy Mayor die, resign, or be removed from office before the Mayoralty stands vacant, the Mayor shall appoint a new Deputy, confirmed by the Assembly, to fill the vacancy.

\begin{clauses}
\item A new Deputy Mayor may be appointed at any time the sitting Mayor judges needful, and no more than one Deputy may serve at a time.
\item Should both the Mayor and the Deputy die or be removed simultaneously or in succession before a new Mayor can be elected, the highest-ranking Department head, determined by the order of their first confirmation in office, shall assume the Mayoralty as acting Mayor until the election is held and a new Mayor confirmed.
\end{clauses}

\section{What the Deputy May Sign, and What the Deputy May Not}
\label{sec:succession:what-the-deputy-may-sign}

The Mayor's hand is wanted upon more papers in a day than one citizen can read in it. This section sets down which of them the Deputy may sign, and which the Deputy may not sign at all.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Deputy signs, in the Mayor's name with the Deputy's own beneath it, such of the Mayor's ordinary papers as the Mayor has named in a writing filed with the Clerk of Judicial: the apportionment of chits under \artref{art:commerce}, the approval of the ration under \artref{art:supply}, the posting of notices under \artref{art:sheriff}, the leave for a citizen to lodge in the Down Deep under \artref{art:housing}, and such others as the Mayor there names. The writing is filed at the Deputy's confirmation, may be altered by the Mayor at any time, and every alteration is filed with the Clerk and dated.
\item The Deputy signs none of the following, in the Mayor's name or in the Deputy's own: the confirmation of a Head of Department or of the Judge under \artref{art:mayor}; an order for the opening of a passage countersigned under \artref{art:mechanical}; the declaration of an emergency under \artref{art:emergency}; a Forgiveness Holiday under \artref{art:mayor}; the setting aside of a day under \artref{art:assembly}; a promise binding the silo under \artref{art:mayor}; or the putting of an amendment before the Assembly under \artref{art:amendment}. A paper so signed is void, is entered void by the Clerk, and the Deputy answers to Judicial for the signing of it.
\item A paper the Deputy signs under this section binds as though the Mayor had signed it, and the Mayor may not afterward disown it for not having signed it. The Mayor may revoke it from the day of the revoking, which is entered with the day.
\item Where the Mayor is absent and not dead, resigned, or removed, as \secrefhere{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-assumption-of-the-mayoralty} provides, the Deputy signs what that Section allows; and the Clerk enters upon every such paper that it was signed in the Mayor's absence, with the day the absence began.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Handover of the Mayor's Office}
\label{sec:succession:handover-of-the-mayors-office}

An office is a room full of papers, keys, and chits, and these are counted when they change hands.

\begin{clauses}
\item Within one day of a Mayor's death, resignation, or removal, or of the confirmation of a Mayor's successor, the Clerk of Judicial and the Deputy Mayor go through the Mayor's chambers together and enter in a roll: the standing orders in force, by number and day; the keys the office holds and what each opens; the seal-stamp under \artref{art:records} and its condition; the papers awaiting the Mayor's hand; the chits standing to the office's account under \artref{art:commerce}; and the writing then in force under \secrefhere{sec:succession:what-the-deputy-may-sign}.
\item The roll is signed by both, copied to the archive, and read to the incoming Mayor or acting Mayor before that citizen signs any paper. Nothing is carried out of the chambers before the roll is made, and a thing not found is entered as not found, with the day it was last seen if that can be learned.
\item The seal-stamp of a Mayor who is gone is defaced by the Clerk in the Deputy's presence, and a new one cut under \artref{art:records}; the defacing is entered in the roll.
\item The Mayor's own writings, letters, and notes that are not the office's are given to the Mayor's household as \artref{art:health} provides for a citizen's goods, save such as the Clerk finds to be the office's; and where the household and the Clerk disagree, Judicial rules which they are, and reads no further into them than that.
\item An outgoing Mayor who is living sits with the successor for one cycle, upon the successor's asking and not otherwise, and draws the ordinary chits of the Mayor's office for those days and nothing further.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Deputy's Own Trade, Dwelling, and Chits}
\label{sec:succession:deputys-own-trade-dwelling-and-chits}

The Deputy Mayor is a citizen who has set a trade down, and will one day pick it up again.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen appointed Deputy Mayor leaves that citizen's trade for the term of the office, and does not lose it. The Head of the Department enters the citizen upon the Department's roll as absent in office; the citizen's place in the shifts is filled by a shadow or by another hand; and the place is restored at the office's ending, or a like place where the trade has changed in the meantime.
\item The Deputy Mayor keeps no shadow. The office is not entered by shadowing under \artref{art:labor}, and no citizen is taught it. What the office requires to be known is in the Mayor's records, which the Deputy reads under \secrefhere{sec:succession:office-of-deputy-mayor}, and no office of the silo owes the Deputy anything further.
\item The Deputy is lodged in the Up Top under \artref{art:housing} for the term of the office. The dwelling of the Deputy's household is kept for the household and is not reassigned by Housing, and the Deputy returns to it within one cycle of the office's ending.
\item The Deputy draws the chits of the office under \artref{art:commerce}, which are the Mayor's less one part in three, and draws nothing from the trade set down. While acting as Mayor under \secrefhere{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-assumption-of-the-mayoralty} the Deputy draws the Mayor's chits, from the day of the assumption and not from the day it was entered.
\item A Deputy put out of the office at the Mayor's pleasure under \secrefhere{sec:succession:office-of-deputy-mayor} returns to the trade under the first clause of this section with nothing entered against that citizen's name, the Mayor's pleasure being no cause; and the Mayor enters that the office stands vacant and need not enter why.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Standing Orders of a Mayor Who Is Gone}
\label{sec:succession:standing-orders-of-a-mayor-who-is-gone}

The silo is run day to day by standing orders, and the orders do not fall with the hand that gave them.

\begin{clauses}
\item A standing order of a Mayor stands after that Mayor is gone, until a Mayor revokes it or Judicial finds it contrary to this Pact. No order lapses for want of a hand, and no Department may treat an order as lapsed because the Mayor who gave it is dead.
\item Within one season of taking office, a Mayor reads every standing order in force, as the roll under \secrefhere{sec:succession:handover-of-the-mayors-office} gives them, and enters against each that it stands or that it is revoked. An order the Mayor does not reach within that season stands, and stands until read.
\item An acting Mayor under \secrefhere{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-assumption-of-the-mayoralty}, or under \secrefhere{sec:succession:deputy-mayors-election-and-succession}, revokes a standing order only where Judicial has found it contrary to this Pact, and gives only such standing orders as the silo's ordinary business requires. An order so given ceases of itself upon the confirmation of a Mayor, unless that Mayor enters within one season that it stands.
\item The roll of standing orders in force is read at the ordinary sitting of the Assembly by number, day, and subject, and the orders themselves are open to any citizen in the archive under \artref{art:records}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Deputy in the Assembly and Before Judicial}
\label{sec:succession:deputy-in-the-assembly-and-before-judicial}

Where the Deputy sits, when the Deputy speaks, and how the Deputy answers.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Deputy Mayor sits at the Mayor's left hand at a sitting of the Assembly, speaks when the Mayor gives the floor or when the Assembly calls upon the Deputy, and votes as a citizen of majority and in no other character.
\item Where the Mayor and the Deputy are both before the Assembly upon one matter, the Mayor speaks first, and the Deputy is not required to speak at all; and the Deputy may not be asked by the Assembly, nor by any citizen in it, whether the Deputy agrees with the Mayor.
\item The Deputy answers to Judicial as any citizen answers, for any offense; and answers for the conduct of the office as the Mayor answers under \artref{art:mayor}. A finding against the Deputy is carried to the Mayor, who may vacate the office upon it, and to the Assembly where the finding is of a breach of this Pact.
\item The Deputy is a witness before Judicial as any citizen is, and the office does not excuse the Deputy from being one; but the Deputy is not required to give the Mayor's own words spoken within the Mayor's chambers, save upon the Judge's order, which enters why those words are needed and is entered in the archive whether the words are given or not.
\end{clauses}
