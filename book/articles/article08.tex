\chapter{Of the Department of Information Technology}
\label{art:it}

\articlenote{In which is set down the charge of the Department of Information Technology: the keeping of the silo's servers and its voice across floors, the licensing of instruments no citizen may make for themselves, and such further duties as this Pact elsewhere leaves to that Department's own instruction.}

\section{The Charge of Information Technology}
\label{sec:it:charge-of-information-technology}

The Department of Information Technology is charged with the keeping of the silo's servers, the carrying of word by radio and by wire between floors and offices, the licensing of tools and instruments this Pact elsewhere restricts, and the upkeep of the outward sensors and the wallscreen as \artref{art:founding} sets down.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Department is led by a Head of Information Technology, and keeps its principal offices in the Mid Thirties, though its charge under this Article extends to every floor.
\item Every citizen assigned to a trade of Information Technology serves under the Head of Information Technology in the ordinary conduct of that trade, and remains bound in every other respect to this Pact, to the Sheriff, and to Judicial, exactly as any other citizen is bound.
\item The office of Head of Information Technology is barred to a citizen afflicted with the Syndrome, and vacated by it, as \secref{art:labor}{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} provides; upon such a resignation a successor is named as \secrefhere{sec:it:trades-of-information-technology} provides.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Licensing of Dangerous Instruments}
\label{sec:it:licensing-of-dangerous-instruments}

No citizen may make, keep, or use any lens or glass that magnifies beyond the ordinary power of reading spectacles, any device that sends or receives word without a wire strung and licensed for the purpose, or any fine instrument for the working of metal, wire, or glass, save under a license issued by Information Technology.

\begin{clauses}
\item A license issued under this section shall name the citizen, the tool, and the floor of its use, and shall be entered in a roll kept by Information Technology and shared with Judicial, that the two rolls may be reconciled one against the other at any time either Department requires.
\item A tool or device of the kind named in this section, found in the keeping of a citizen without license, is a relic forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder}, whatever use the citizen claims to have intended for it, and its finding shall be reported to Judicial without delay.
\item Information Technology shall license to Mechanical such tools as \artref{art:mechanical} requires for its own work, under the same manner of numbering, recording, and return set down there.
\end{clauses}

\section{Radios and the Silo's Word}
\label{sec:it:radios-and-the-silos-word}

No wire, radio, or like device for the carrying of word beyond the reach of a citizen's own voice may be built, strung, or operated save by Information Technology or under its license, and no citizen may carry word by such means on another citizen's behalf without that license.

\begin{clauses}
\item Word that must travel between floors, and is not licensed to pass by wire or radio, shall be carried by a porter under \artref{art:records}.
\item Information Technology shall keep such stations for the carrying of official word between the Mayor, the Sheriff, Judicial, and the Heads of every Department as the silo's business requires, and no citizen not assigned to that keeping may make use of them.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Sensors, the Wallscreen, and the Record of the Dead}
\label{sec:it:sensors-the-wallscreen-and-the-record-of-the-dead}

The upkeep of the outward sensors and the wallscreen, their cleaning and their repair, is the charge of Information Technology under \artref{art:founding}, exercised under such further procedure as this Pact does not set down, that procedure being no part of ordinary citizenship.

\begin{clauses}
\item Where a citizen is sent to cleaning under \artref{art:cleaning}, the word of Information Technology that the sensors were kept is sufficient, under \artref{art:citizenship}, for Judicial to close that citizen's name in the rolls, and no further attestation is required.
\item The suit worn by a citizen sent to clean is assembled in the cleaning lab under this charge, to a specification Information Technology alone sets and is instructed in, from cloth, seals, and fittings furnished by Mechanical and Supply, as \artref{art:cleaning} sets down.
\end{clauses}

\section{Relics of Craft and Contrivance}
\label{sec:it:relics-of-craft-and-contrivance}

A relic of craft or contrivance from before the silo's sealing, surrendered under \artref{art:publicorder} or found by Judicial, shall be delivered to Information Technology for examination, that its danger may be judged by those fitted to judge it.

\begin{clauses}
\item Information Technology shall report to Judicial what a relic examined under this section is and what became of it, but need not report how it was examined, nor keep the relic for any citizen's later viewing.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Trades of Information Technology}
\label{sec:it:trades-of-information-technology}

Every trade of Information Technology is entered by the shadowing set down under \artref{art:labor}.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Head of Information Technology names a successor from among the Department's own trades, and that naming stands as though confirmed, unless the Mayor and Judicial jointly object within one turn of the season.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Rolls of License and Their Keeping}
\label{sec:it:rolls-of-license-and-their-keeping}

A license under \secrefhere{sec:it:licensing-of-dangerous-instruments} is a written thing in two places, and is no license until it is in both.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen or a Head of Department who would have an instrument licensed petitions the Head of Information Technology in writing, naming the citizen who will hold it, the instrument, the use, and the floor; the Head, or a licensor of the Department raised by short shadowing under \artref{art:labor}, examines the petition and the instrument, and grants, limits, or refuses the license within one cycle, entering the reason for a refusal.
\item A license granted is entered in the roll of licenses kept by Information Technology, numbered, with the day, the citizen, the instrument by its own number under \artref{art:mechanical} where it has one, the use, the floor, and the licensor's name; the instrument is marked with the license's number by the licensor's own hand, and a copy of the entry is sent to the Clerk of Judicial within one cycle for the roll Judicial keeps under \secrefhere{sec:it:licensing-of-dangerous-instruments}.
\item The two rolls are reconciled at the turn of every season by a licensor and an Officer of Judicial together, entry against entry; a license in one roll and not the other is void until entered in both, and the instrument is called in to Information Technology until it is.
\item A license runs for one year from its grant, and is renewed upon the citizen's presenting the instrument to a licensor within the last cycle of the year; an instrument not presented is called in, and a citizen found holding it beyond the year is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for an unlicensed instrument, whatever the earlier license.
\item The Head of Information Technology may withdraw a license at any time, entering the reason, and the instrument is surrendered to a licensor within one day of the withdrawal's being told to the citizen; a citizen who believes a license wrongly withdrawn may petition Judicial under \artref{art:judicial}, but surrenders the instrument first.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Stations of Official Word}
\label{sec:it:stations-of-official-word}

The word of an office travels by wire or radio only from a station this Department keeps, and only in the hearing of this Department.

\begin{clauses}
\item Information Technology keeps a station of official word in the office of the Mayor, in the station of the Sheriff and in each Deputy's station under \artref{art:sheriff}, in the hall of Judicial, in the offices of the Head of every Department, and in its own principal offices in the Mid Thirties; no station is kept elsewhere, and no wire is strung to any room not named here save under \secrefhere{sec:it:radios-and-the-silos-word} for the carrying of word between these.
\item Each station is kept by a technician of Information Technology on every shift of the day, and no officer speaks by the station save in the technician's presence and with the technician's hand upon it; the technician enters in the station's log the sending office, the receiving office, the hour, and the officer who spoke, as \artref{art:records} requires, and delivers the log to the Clerk of Judicial at each turn of the season.
\item An officer may send by the station only such word as the office's own charge under this Pact requires, and the technician may refuse a word the technician finds to be no word of the office, entering the refusal; a refusal so entered is carried to the Head of Information Technology and to the Judge, who rule upon it together, and the office may send the word by porter meanwhile.
\item No station may be opened, moved, or repaired save by Information Technology, and a station found opened by any other hand is reported to the Judge as interference under \secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:apparatus-of-the-silos-keeping}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Servers and Their Keeping}
\label{sec:it:servers-and-their-keeping}

This Department keeps the silo's servers, and what the servers keep is this Department's charge.

\begin{clauses}
\item The servers are kept in a room of the Department's principal offices in the Mid Thirties, and in such further rooms as the Head of Information Technology names; no citizen not assigned to their keeping enters those rooms, and the rooms are not common areas under \artref{art:sheriff}, nor subject to search under \artref{art:judicial} save upon the Judge's warrant given with the Head's own knowledge.
\item The servers keep such records as this Department keeps in its own charge under this Article, and such further matter as that Department alone is instructed in; the servers are not the Judicial archive, and no record this Pact requires to be kept in the archive is kept only in the servers.
\item The servers draw their share of the generator's power under \artref{art:mechanical} by an agreement the Head of Information Technology and the Head of Mechanical enter with the Mayor at each turn of the season, and in a shortfall the servers' share is lessened in the order \artref{art:emergency} provides, after the market and the common halls and before the farms'; the Head of Information Technology may represent to the Mayor that a lessening endangers the Department's charge, and the Mayor rules.
\item The server technicians of this Department are a trade of full shadowing and of the vetting \artref{art:labor} provides for a trade of trust, and a server technician's record of what is done in the server rooms is kept by the Department and not delivered to the archive, save upon the Judge's warrant given as this section provides.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Examination of a Relic}
\label{sec:it:examination-of-a-relic}

A relic comes to this Department from Judicial under \secrefhere{sec:it:relics-of-craft-and-contrivance}, and goes back to Judicial with a report; this section sets down the manner of both.

\begin{clauses}
\item A relic delivered under \secrefhere{sec:it:relics-of-craft-and-contrivance} is received by a relic examiner of the Department, a trade of full shadowing and of the vetting of a trade of trust, who gives the Officer delivering it a receipt naming the relic by its number in the roll of evidence under \artref{art:judicial}, and enters the receipt in the Department's own roll of relics received.
\item The examiner examines the relic within one season, and reports to the Judge in writing what the relic is, so far as the examiner can say without saying what this Pact does not permit; whether it is a relic of craft or contrivance, a writing, a likeness, or a thing of no craft at all; whether it can teach a citizen anything the Department judges dangerous to the silo's order; and what the examiner recommends as to its class under \artref{art:publicorder}.
\item The examiner need not report the manner of the examination, nor what the relic was found to contain beyond the report's own words, nor keep the relic for any citizen's later viewing, as \secrefhere{sec:it:relics-of-craft-and-contrivance} provides; and a relic the examiner finds to be a device that sends or receives word, or a writing bearing upon the world above, is reported so, and is kept by the Department and not returned to Judicial's store, the keeping entered in both rolls.
\item The report is entered in the roll of evidence beside the relic, and the Judge classifies the relic under \artref{art:publicorder} upon it; the Judge may ask the examiner to attend the classification and answer as to the report, and the examiner attends, and answers within the report's own bounds.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Trades of the Department and the Naming of the Head}
\label{sec:it:trades-of-the-department-and-the-naming-of-the-head}

This Department keeps its own trades more closely than any other, and its Head is named as no other Head is named.

\begin{clauses}
\item Every trade of Information Technology is entered by full shadowing under \artref{art:labor}, and every trade of it save the licensors and the archivists of its own rolls is a trade of trust under that Article, vetted as it provides; the Head of Information Technology keeps the particulars of that vetting, and may require it again of any citizen of the Department at any time.
\item A citizen of the Department who leaves it, by reassignment, retirement, or removal, is seen by the Head before leaving and is bound, by an entry the citizen signs, not to speak of the Department's charge beyond what this Pact sets down; the entry is kept by the Department, and a citizen who breaks it is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for interference under \secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:apparatus-of-the-silos-keeping}.
\item The Head names a successor under \secrefhere{sec:it:trades-of-information-technology} by an entry in the Department's own roll, signed by the Head and by the citizen named, and delivers a copy to the Mayor and to the Judge the same day; the turn of the season within which the Mayor and Judicial may jointly object runs from the delivery, and an objection is made in writing signed by both and entered in the archive, stating the cause.
\item Where the Mayor and Judicial jointly object, the Head names another, and the season runs again; where the Head dies or is removed before a successor is named, the citizen the Head last shadowed in the Department's charge, or, where there is none, the senior technician of the Department by first confirmation, keeps the Department as acting Head and names a successor under this section within one season, as the Head would have.
\item The Head's own shadow, where the Head keeps one, is a citizen of the Department confirmed in a trade of trust, and is instructed in such further matter of the Department's charge as this Pact does not set down; the naming of a shadow is entered in the Department's roll, and the shadow is the successor the Head is presumed to name under this section unless the Head enters otherwise.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Silo's Own Clock}
\label{sec:it:silos-own-clock}

A day is one turning of the silo's own clock, as \artref{art:founding} sets down, and every span this Pact reckons --- the cycle, the season, the year, the term, the notice, the hour of a sitting --- is reckoned from it. The clock is therefore kept as the sensors are kept, and by the same Department.

\begin{clauses}
\item Information Technology keeps the silo's clock, and keeps it in one place; and the hour it shows is the hour for every purpose of this Pact, whatever any other clock, bell, or shift-light in the silo shows.
\item The hour is put out to the floors at the turning of each day, by the stations of official word under \secrefhere{sec:it:stations-of-official-word} and by the posted notices of the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff}, that a citizen on any floor may know the day and the hour without climbing to ask.
\item Where the clock is found to run fast or slow, the Head of Information Technology corrects it, and enters in the Department's roll the day of the correction and its measure; and the Judge is told of a correction greater than one day, and enters it in the archive. A span already begun is reckoned by the clock as corrected, and is neither lengthened nor shortened by the correction.
\item No citizen keeps a clock, nor makes one, nor mends one, save under license under \secrefhere{sec:it:licensing-of-dangerous-instruments}; and a clock kept without license is a restricted instrument under \secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:restricted-instruments-and-devices}.
\item Judicial enters the day upon every record it keeps under \artref{art:records}, taking the day from the clock and from no other reckoning; and where two records disagree as to a day, the record the clock supports is the record that stands.
\item The Head of Information Technology answers to no other office for the keeping of the clock, and is not required to set down the manner of its keeping, the matter being within those the Department alone is instructed in; but the Head answers to Judicial for any day entered in the archive that the clock does not support.
\end{clauses}
