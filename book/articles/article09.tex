\chapter{Of the Department of Mechanical}
\label{art:mechanical}

\articlenote{In which is set down the charge of the Department of Mechanical: the generator, the works of water and air, the reclamation of what is worn, and the narrow road --- the freight lift, and no other --- by which goods may pass where citizens may not.}

\section{The Charge of Mechanical}
\label{sec:mechanical:charge-of-mechanical}

The Department of Mechanical is charged with the keeping of the generator, the works of water and air, the reclamation of the silo's worn substance, and the structural soundness of the shaft itself, so far as such soundness may be tended without opening any passage forbidden under \artref{art:founding}.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Department is led by a Head of Mechanical, raised up from among its own trades by the shadowing entered into under \artref{art:labor}, and confirmed in office by the Mayor.
\item Every citizen assigned to a trade of Mechanical serves under the Head of Mechanical in the ordinary conduct of that trade, though such a citizen remains bound in every other respect to this Pact, to the Sheriff, and to Judicial exactly as any other citizen is bound.
\item The Head of Mechanical shall reside and keep the Department's offices in the Down Deep, among the works described in this Article, and not Up Top.
\end{clauses}

\section{Power, Circulation, and Reclamation}
\label{sec:mechanical:power-circulation-and-reclamation}

The generator that gives the silo its light and its warmth, the works that draw water fit to drink from water fouled, the vents and scrubbers that keep the silo's air breathable, and the reclamation of cloth, metal, and matter too worn for its first purpose are here treated as one charge, for the failure of any one of them, left untended, threatens all the rest.

\begin{clauses}
\item The generator, its housing, and every line and switch that carries its power beyond that housing are the sole charge of Mechanical. No citizen not assigned to that charge may open, adjust, or attempt repair of any part of it.
\item The works of water and air --- the treatment of water drawn from below, the scrubbing and venting of the silo's air, and the lines that carry both beyond the Down Deep --- are likewise the sole charge of Mechanical, exercised alongside Supply where Supply's own stores of water and air are concerned, under \artref{art:supply}.
\item Mechanical shall reclaim, so far as reclamation is possible, the cloth, metal, plastic, and other matter surrendered by citizens or gathered by Supply and Judicial, returning to use what can be made fit for it, and recording, but not destroying without instruction, what cannot.
\item Interference with any part of the generator, the works of water and air, or the labor of reclamation is treated, without exception, as among the gravest offenses under \artref{art:crimes}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Freight Lift}
\label{sec:mechanical:freight-lift}

The one exception named under \artref{art:founding} to the silo's single stair is the freight lift running between the Down Deep and the floors of Supply, built and kept by Mechanical for the carrying of goods too heavy or too many for a citizen's own back.

\begin{clauses}
\item The freight lift carries goods only. No citizen may ride within it, and no citizen may request or be granted passage within it, save a Mechanical or Supply worker in the direct performance of loading, unloading, or the lift's own repair.
\item Every use of the freight lift shall be logged --- the goods carried, the citizen who ordered the carrying, and the floors between which it ran --- and kept in the records of Mechanical under \artref{art:records}.
\item The freight lift shall be run at no greater speed, and shall draw no greater share of the generator's power, than the Head of Mechanical judges the generator can bear without want to any other citizen's need for light, warmth, or water.
\item The making of any device to carry a citizen's own body between floors, whatever its design and whoever proposes it, is forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder}.
\end{clauses}

\section{Structural Work and the Prohibition Against Digging}
\label{sec:mechanical:structural-work-and-the-prohibition-against-digging}

\artref{art:founding} forbids any citizen to dig, bore, blast, or otherwise open a passage not already given by the silo's builders, save under the direct order of the Head of Mechanical and the joint knowledge of the Mayor. This section sets down how such an order is given and kept.

\begin{clauses}
\item An order under this section may be given only in writing, signed by the Head of Mechanical and countersigned by the Mayor, naming the floor, the wall or ceiling to be opened, the reason for the work, and the citizens who will perform it.
\item No such order may be given for the opening of any passage between the silo and the world above, nor for any depth beyond what the builders themselves reached, regardless of the reason offered or the office of the one offering it.
\item A copy of every order given under this section shall be kept in the records of Mechanical and in the Judicial archive.
\item No passage opened under this section may be used for any purpose beyond the work named in the order, and shall be closed and made sound again upon that work's completion, to the satisfaction of the Head of Mechanical, before any citizen but a Mechanical worker may pass it again.
\end{clauses}

\section{Tools, Materials, and Their Licensing}
\label{sec:mechanical:tools-materials-and-their-licensing}

Mechanical's work requires tools and instruments that this Pact elsewhere restricts to every other citizen, among them glasses and lenses beyond the ordinary power forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder}, and fine instruments for the working of metal and wire.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Department of Information Technology shall license to Mechanical such tools as its work requires, each tool numbered, its whereabouts recorded, and its return confirmed upon the completion of the work for which it was drawn.
\item No tool licensed under this section may be carried beyond the floor of its use without the leave of the Head of Mechanical, nor kept by any citizen beyond the day of its return, nor lent, sold, or given to any citizen not licensed under this section in their own right.
\item A citizen found to hold, or to have made, any tool of the kind licensed under this section without such license is treated exactly as though the tool were a relic forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder}, whatever use the citizen claims to have intended for it.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Trades of the Down Deep}
\label{sec:mechanical:trades-of-the-down-deep}

Every trade of Mechanical --- the tending of the generator, the working of water and air, the reclamation of matter, and the keeping of the silo's structure --- is entered as every other trade is entered, by the shadowing set down under \artref{art:labor}, save that no citizen may shadow into the tending of the generator, the licensing of tools under this Article, or the ordering of work under \secrefhere{sec:mechanical:structural-work-and-the-prohibition-against-digging} without also being found fit by the Head of Mechanical, beyond whatever fitness \artref{art:labor} alone would require.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Head of Mechanical shall keep, and pass to their own shadow before leaving office, a full accounting of the Department's tools, its licensed citizens, and its outstanding orders under this Article.
\item No citizen shall be assigned to Mechanical against the citizen's own naming of a trade at the age \artref{art:labor} sets down, save where the Department's need for a given trade cannot otherwise be met, in which case Judicial shall confirm that the assignment is not made in punishment.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Works That Are Not Spoken Of}
\label{sec:mechanical:works-that-are-not-spoken-of}

Beneath the works a citizen may see are works a citizen may not: intakes, drains, and bypass conduits the builders sank where no floor reaches, which the silo cannot do without and cannot afford to have known. This section sets down who keeps them, who may be told of them, and what a citizen does who comes upon one unlooked-for.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Head of Mechanical keeps, in a sealed record in the Judicial archive, the accounting of every such work the builders left within the deepest workings --- among them the intakes, the drainage, and the bypass conduits --- with its place, its condition, and the day of its last tending. The record is opened by the Head of Mechanical, or by the Judge upon a ruling under \artref{art:judicial} that enters the cause of the opening, and by no other hand.
\item The place of such a work is told to no citizen but the citizen set to tend it, and to that citizen only so much of it as the tending requires; the citizen so set is entered in the record by name, and the entry is struck when the setting ends.
\item A citizen who comes upon such a work unlooked-for shall carry the matter the same day to the Head of Mechanical and to Judicial, and shall speak of it to no other citizen, neither before the carrying nor after it. A citizen who speaks of it to another is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}, and the citizen told is held to this clause as though that citizen had come upon the work.
\item A citizen who seeks out, maps, measures, or opens such a work without the Head of Mechanical's setting, or who tampers with one, is answerable among the gravest under \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:offenses-among-the-gravest}.
\item The Head of Mechanical reports the condition of these works once in the year, in private audience, to the Mayor and to the Judge, in such terms as say that the works are kept and do not say where they are. No such work is altered, opened, or left unmended save by the Head of Mechanical's own order and, where the Mayor has been told of that work as this section provides, with the Mayor's knowledge.
\item Where a matter before the Mayor would touch such a work, the Head of Mechanical may tell the Mayor of its existence, its place, and its working, and binds the Mayor to the silence this section lays upon Mechanical's own. Whether the Mayor is told of a particular work is the Head of Mechanical's determination alone; and the Head enters that a telling was made or withheld, and does not enter what was told.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Shifts and the Tending of the Generator}
\label{sec:mechanical:shifts-and-the-tending-of-the-generator}

The generator does not rest, and so the tending of it does not; this section sets down how Mechanical keeps a watch that never ends without keeping any citizen at it past bearing.

\begin{clauses}
\item The generator is tended in three shifts in every day, each of a third of the day by the silo's own clock, and no citizen tends it for two shifts together save in an emergency under \artref{art:emergency} entered by the Head of Mechanical; the Head posts the shifts at the Department's door at each turn of the season, and the rest day under \artref{art:founding} is given to each generator tender in turn, so that the watch is kept and each tender has it.
\item At the change of every shift, the tender going off and the tender coming on together read the generator's gauges, enter them in the generator's own log with the hour and both their names, and speak aloud any fault found or feared; a fault so spoken is entered in the log, and the Head of Mechanical reads the log at the start of every day.
\item Once in every year, at a turn of the season the Head of Mechanical names not less than one season before, the generator is stopped for its full tending, the silo's light and warmth being carried for that time by the reserve Mechanical keeps for the purpose; the Head gives notice of the stopping to the Mayor, the Head of Supply, and every floor by posted notice under \artref{art:sheriff}, not less than one cycle before, and the stopping does not exceed one day save by the Mayor's written order.
\item The full tending is carried out by the generator tenders under the Head of Mechanical's own eye, and every part opened, cleaned, or replaced is entered in the generator's log by part and by name; the log of the full tending is copied to the Judicial archive within one cycle.
\item No citizen may enter the generator's housing during a shift save the tenders of that shift, the Head of Mechanical, and a citizen the Head brings in under the Head's own hand; a shadow tends the generator only beside a master, and never alone, until confirmed in the trade under \artref{art:labor}.
\end{clauses}

\section{Accident in the Works and Its Reporting}
\label{sec:mechanical:accident-in-the-works-and-its-reporting}

The works hurt those who keep them, and the silo owes it to those it hurts to know how.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen hurt in the works, or in any labor of Mechanical, is carried to the nearest aid station of the Infirmary under \artref{art:health} before any other thing is done, and the Head of Mechanical is told the same shift; a death in the works is reported to the Infirmary and to Judicial under \artref{art:citizenship} the same day, and the place of it is left as it was, so far as the works allow, until an Officer of Judicial has seen it.
\item The Head of Mechanical enters every hurt and every death in the Department's own roll of accidents, with the day, the shift, the place, the citizen, the tool or part concerned, and the cause so far as it can be known, and reads the roll to the Department at each turn of the season, that what hurt one not hurt another.
\item Where an accident came of a tool, a part, or a practice, the Head of Mechanical may order the tool withdrawn, the part replaced, or the practice changed, and enters the order beside the accident; where it came of a citizen's own neglect of the Department's practice, the Head corrects it under \artref{art:labor}, and where it came of a citizen's interference, the Head reports it to Judicial under \secrefhere{sec:mechanical:power-circulation-and-reclamation}.
\item A citizen hurt in the works who cannot return to the trade is reassigned or retired under \artref{art:labor} and \artref{art:health}, and the roll of accidents is the Infirmary's warrant for the retirement; the Department does not lose the citizen's dwelling to Housing under \artref{art:housing} on that account within the same year.
\item The roll of accidents is copied to the Judicial archive at each turn of the season, and the Head of Mechanical reports to the Mayor, once yearly, the count of hurts and deaths in the works and what was changed on their account.
\end{clauses}
