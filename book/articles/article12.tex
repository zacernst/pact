\chapter{Of Trade, Chits, and Commerce}
\label{art:commerce}

\articlenote{In which is set down the chit, the silo's currency of trade, the marketplace where citizens buy and sell goods beyond the ration, and the keeping of accounts that make such commerce orderly and just.}

\section{The Chit and the Earning of Chits}
\label{sec:commerce:chit-and-the-earning-of-chits}

A chit is a token the silo issues and the Chit-Keepers answer for under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep}, and it stands for a claim: upon such of the silo's goods as are not given in the ration, and upon the hands of citizens who will work for it. A citizen comes by chits through labor, and by no other lawful means save gift, sale, and the settling of a debt.

\begin{clauses}
\item Every citizen in labor under \artref{art:labor} is paid in chits, at a measure the Heads of Departments set with the Mayor. The measure may differ between trades --- a trade that took years to learn is paid above one that took a cycle, and a trade that costs the body is paid above one that does not --- but no citizen's chits are withheld or lessened as a punishment for work done, save upon Judicial's order for restitution under \artref{art:crimes} or for the settling of a debt under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:debt-and-the-settling-of-it}.
\item A citizen retired from labor under \artref{art:labor} is paid still, in such measure as buys the small goods and comforts the ration does not give, drawn from Supply under \artref{art:supply} upon the Infirmary's attestation of the citizen's continuing need under \artref{art:health}.
\item A citizen serving a term in the mines under \artref{art:crimes} is paid nothing for the term, and the chits standing to that citizen's name are kept in the ledger undrawn as \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:reckoning-of-a-term-in-the-mines} provides; upon the term's ending the citizen is paid again at the measure of the trade Judicial assigns.
\item A citizen's chits are the citizen's own, and are not taken, seized, or turned aside save upon Judicial's order for a debt or a restitution under \artref{art:crimes}. A citizen robbed of chits carries the matter to the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff}; a citizen whose chits cannot be got back petitions Judicial under \artref{art:judicial}, which may order the silo to make the loss good where the loss came of the silo's own failing and not otherwise.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Marketplace and the Sale of Goods}
\label{sec:commerce:marketplace-and-the-sale-of-goods}

Beyond the ration \artref{art:supply} gives every citizen alike, a citizen may buy: a better coat, a sweeter fruit, a mended chair, an hour of another citizen's skill. The market is where this is done, and this section sets down what may pass there and what may not.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen may buy and sell with any other citizen, save that no citizen sells what is the silo's or a Department's --- a worker of Mechanical does not sell the silo's tools, nor a clerk of Supply the silo's stores, and so for every Department alike. A thing sold that was not the seller's is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}, and the thing returns to the silo whatever was paid for it.
\item A citizen of a skilled trade may sell the work of that trade to other citizens for chits, over and above whatever the citizen's Department gives out.
\item The silo keeps markets, in rooms the Head of Supply sets aside upon the floors, and the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff} keeps order in them.
\item A Market Clerk, raised by short shadowing under \artref{art:labor} and appointed by the Head of Supply under \artref{art:supply}, keeps a market: sets the stalls as \secrefhere{sec:commerce:market-days-and-the-stalls} provides, puts out the rules of the floor against spoiled goods and false claims, hears the first complaint in any dispute under Sections~\secnum{sec:commerce:what-a-thing-may-be-asked-for} and~\secnum{sec:commerce:work-a-citizen-does-for-another}, and carries to Judicial or the Sheriff what is beyond the Clerk's hearing. The Market Clerk is an office of Supply, as Housing is.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Chit-Keepers and the Rolls They Keep}
\label{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep}

The chit is worth what the silo says it is worth, and the silo says so through the Chit-Keepers, who count what is issued, answer for what is in hand, and keep the ledger in which every citizen's standing is written.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Chit-Keepers are appointed by the Mayor and kept by the Head of Supply under \artref{art:supply}. They keep two rolls and no others: the master roll of every chit the silo has issued, and the ledger of every citizen's balance, with what has gone out of it and what has come in.
\item A citizen may ask a Chit-Keeper at any time for the reckoning of the citizen's own balance, or for the proving of chits the citizen holds. The Chit-Keeper proves the chits, sets them against the citizen's entry in the ledger, and carries any difference between the two to Judicial the same day.
\item The counterfeiting of a chit, and the keeping of a counterfeit by a citizen who knows it for one, are named among the gravest under \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:offenses-among-the-gravest}; for a chit that is not what it says it is makes a liar of every chit beside it.
\item The Chit-Keepers may, upon the Mayor's written order, revalue the chit where the silo's stores have fallen and the chits in hand have not --- as, the stores being spent and the chits grown too many, the Mayor may order the chit's worth set down. A revaluation is announced to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly} and entered in the Judicial archive, with the old measure and the new set side by side.
\item The Chit-Keepers credit to each Department's account, at the turn of each season, such chits as the Mayor apportions from the silo's issue, and a Head of Department spends from that account and no other as \secrefhere{sec:commerce:barter-without-chits} provides. The apportionment is entered in the archive and read at the ordinary sitting of the Assembly, the whole of it and not a Department's share alone.
\item Upon the closing of a citizen's name under \artref{art:citizenship}, the balance standing in the ledger to that name returns to the silo's issue, the citizen's debts being first settled from it under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:debt-and-the-settling-of-it}; the Chit-Keepers tell the household the sum that stood there, and do not give it. Chits held in token in the dwelling pass to the household with the dwelling's goods under \secref{art:housing}{sec:housing:taking-up-and-the-leaving-of-a-dwelling}.
\end{clauses}

\section{What a Thing May Be Asked For}
\label{sec:commerce:what-a-thing-may-be-asked-for}

The silo does not set the price of what one citizen sells another; a tailor and a customer may come to their own terms over a shirt. The silo speaks only where a price ceases to be a price and becomes a hold upon a citizen's need.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen may ask what price the citizen will for goods or labor, and the other citizen may refuse it. Neither is compelled to buy nor to sell at any price.
\item Where a citizen says that another has asked an unjust price --- a price out of all keeping with the thing or with the need of it --- either carries the matter first to the Market Clerk; and where either is unsatisfied, the matter goes to Judicial, which alone rules a price unjust and orders the exchange undone or the chits returned.
\item A citizen may not ask a price so high that ordinary citizens are shut out of a thing they must have --- as, a seller of water-seals asking more than a household can pay to keep its own stores sound. In such a case the Mayor may set a highest price for that thing under \artref{art:emergency}, the emergency being declared as that Article requires; but this is an order for the whole silo and in an emergency only, and it does not answer, nor stand in place of, Judicial's ruling upon a single unjust exchange under the clause above.
\item No citizen may keep another from selling goods or labor in the market, save where Judicial finds the selling to be fraud, theft, or the passing of a thing forbidden under \artref{art:publicorder}.
\end{clauses}

\section{Debt and the Settling of It}
\label{sec:commerce:debt-and-the-settling-of-it}

A citizen may lend chits to another, and the lending makes a debt. The silo does not compel the paying of it; the Chit-Keepers write it down, and Judicial enforces it where the lender asks and the borrower will not pay.

\begin{clauses}
\item A debt is a promise to repay, made between two citizens and recorded either by their word sworn before a Chit-Keeper or by a note in writing that both have signed. A debt so recorded is enforced by Judicial, and a debt not so recorded is not.
\item A citizen who cannot pay may petition Judicial to lessen the debt or to set the paying of it over a longer span; Judicial may do either where it finds the citizen's want is real and the lender is not thereby ruined.
\item A lender who forgives a debt says so to the Chit-Keepers, and the debt is struck from the ledger. A lender who will not record a forgiveness, or who claims still a debt the citizen has paid, is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}.
\item No citizen is bound to labor without end for a debt. A debt does not outlive the debtor, and does not pass to the debtor's children or to any other citizen; a debt standing at a citizen's death is settled from the balance under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep} so far as the balance reaches, and is struck for the rest.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Work a Citizen Does for Another}
\label{sec:commerce:work-a-citizen-does-for-another}

The silo's skilled hands --- tailors, cobblers, carpenters, rope-makers, and the rest --- may be asked for work by any citizen who will pay for it. The silo does not compel the asking nor the taking; the hand may accept or refuse as it chooses.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen of a skilled trade may take work from other citizens over and above the work the citizen's Department requires. The citizen and the one who asks shall settle, before the work is begun, what is to be made and what is to be paid, and may ask the Market Clerk to enter both.
\item A citizen asked for work may refuse without answering for the refusal, and a citizen who asked may refuse to take or to pay for work that is not what was settled; such disputes are heard first by the Market Clerk, and, where either is unsatisfied, by Judicial.
\item Certain work is for licensed hands only --- as, only a Physician sells a medicine under \artref{art:health}, and only a licensed hand mends what \artref{art:it} licenses. A citizen who offers such work without the license is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Keeping of the Market's Trust}
\label{sec:commerce:keeping-of-the-markets-trust}

A market holds together on a few plain beliefs: that a chit is a chit, that a seller gives what was promised, that a buyer pays. Where those fail, the market does not become unfair; it stops. The keeping of them is a standing charge upon the Sheriff and upon the Chit-Keepers.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Sheriff walks the markets and the landings about them, and may take up any citizen seen at theft, at fraud, or at violence in the course of trade.
\item A citizen cheated in a trade --- given a counterfeit, shorted in chits, or handed less work than was settled --- carries the matter to the Sheriff or to the Market Clerk, who looks into it and, finding fraud, carries the citizen who did it to Judicial.
\item The Chit-Keepers keep the silo's blank chits and the press by which new chits are struck, in a room secured as the Judicial archive is secured. A citizen who steals or tampers with the blanks or the press, or who helps another to, is answerable among the gravest under \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:offenses-among-the-gravest}.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Market Days and the Stalls}
\label{sec:commerce:market-days-and-the-stalls}

A market is a floor, a day, and a roll; this section sets down all three.

\begin{clauses}
\item Supply keeps a market on such floors of the Mids as the Head of Supply names, not fewer than three, and on one floor of the Down Deep near the works; a market is held on such days of the cycle as the Market Clerk posts at the landing, not fewer than three in the cycle and never the rest day, from the first labor of the day to the last.
\item The Market Clerk keeps a roll of stalls, each numbered, and assigns a stall to a citizen who petitions for one, for a season at a time, in the order of petition, save that a citizen confirmed in a trade of craft under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:work-a-citizen-does-for-another} has first claim to a stall on the citizen's own floor's market; a stall not kept on three market days in a cycle is struck from the roll and offered to the next petitioner.
\item A citizen keeping a stall pays Supply, in chits, at each turn of the season, a fee the Head of Supply sets alike for every stall of the same size and posts at the landing; the fee is entered in the citizen's ledger under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep}, and no other charge is made for the stall.
\item A citizen without a stall may sell from the citizen's own hands on a market day, in the market's open ground and not at the landing or the stair, without fee; the Market Clerk may move such a citizen where the press of citizens requires, and no further.
\item The market's ground is kept clean by the citizens who keep stalls, by turns the Market Clerk posts, and a stall-keeper who does not keep the turn is struck from the roll for a season.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Weights and Measures of Trade}
\label{sec:commerce:weights-and-measures-of-trade}

A thing sold by weight or by measure is sold by the silo's weight and the silo's measure, and by no other.

\begin{clauses}
\item Mechanical keeps, under \artref{art:mechanical}, a standard of weight and a standard of length and of volume, proved against the marks the builders left and entered; from these Mechanical makes, marks, and numbers such weights, rules, and measures as the markets and the stations of Supply require, and no other weight or measure is used in trade.
\item The Market Clerk keeps a set of the marked weights and measures at every market and proves every scale and measure used at a stall against them at each turn of the season, entering the proving; a scale or measure found false is taken by the Clerk and given to Mechanical, and the citizen who used it is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for fraud where the Clerk finds the falseness was known.
\item A citizen who doubts a weight or a measure at a stall may ask the Market Clerk to prove it there, and the Clerk proves it in the sight of both; a citizen who asks it three times in a season without cause found pays the Clerk a chit for each further asking.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Chit Itself}
\label{sec:commerce:chit-itself}

A chit is a thing in the hand and an entry in a ledger, and the silo keeps both.

\begin{clauses}
\item A chit is a token stamped by Mechanical under \artref{art:mechanical} to a pattern the Chit-Keepers keep and the Judge holds a copy of, in such values as the Chit-Keepers set and post at every market, and no citizen makes, marks, or alters one; the stock of blank tokens is kept by the Chit-Keepers under lock under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:keeping-of-the-markets-trust}, and the count of every stamping is entered with the Head of Mechanical's name and the Chit-Keepers'.
\item A chit passes from hand to hand in trade without entry, and the token is the whole of the claim it carries; but a citizen may bring chits to a Chit-Keeper and have them entered to the citizen's ledger under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep}, and draw them out again as tokens at any time, and chits so entered are not lost with the token.
\item A chit worn beyond reading is brought to a Chit-Keeper and exchanged for a new one of the same value, the old one given to Mechanical for reclamation and the exchange entered; a chit found to be false is kept by the Chit-Keeper, entered, and reported to Judicial with the name of the citizen who brought it, who is not answerable for the bringing unless the Judge finds the citizen knew it false.
\item A citizen may keep chits in the token at home, and a chit stolen is a theft under \artref{art:crimes} like any other; the silo does not make good a token stolen or lost, and a citizen who would not bear that risk enters chits to the ledger.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Silo's Own Purchases}
\label{sec:commerce:silos-own-purchases}

The silo does not buy what its Departments make; it may buy what a citizen makes, and it pays as a citizen pays.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Head of Department may commission from a citizen of a craft under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:work-a-citizen-does-for-another} a good the Department requires and does not itself make, for chits, at a price agreed before the work begins and entered with the Chit-Keepers; the Head pays from the Department's own account, which the Chit-Keepers keep and the Mayor reads at each turn of the season.
\item No Head may commission from a citizen a good the Department's own trades are assigned to make, nor pay a citizen of the Department's own trades for the labor of the trade beyond the rate under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:chit-and-the-earning-of-chits}; a commission so made is entered as a diversion of the Department's account, and the Head answers for it under \artref{art:crimes}.
\item A citizen may refuse a Department's commission as any other, and a Department may not withhold the ration, the dwelling, or a shadow on account of the refusal.
\end{clauses}

\section{Barter Without Chits}
\label{sec:commerce:barter-without-chits}

Not every trade passes a chit, and this Pact does not require that it should.

\begin{clauses}
\item Two citizens may exchange goods or labor for goods or labor without chits, and no entry is required of it; such an exchange is not a debt under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:debt-and-the-settling-of-it}, and Judicial does not enforce a promise of barter unmet, though a good delivered and not paid for in the good promised is a theft under \artref{art:crimes} where the Judge finds it was never meant to be paid for.
\item No citizen may exchange for anything the ration, a thing of the silo's, a relic unsanctioned under \artref{art:publicorder}, a restricted instrument, a medicine beyond the course a Physician set, or a chit for a chit of greater value; such an exchange is answerable under the Article that governs the thing exchanged.
\item A citizen who exchanges the labor of a trade for goods, beyond the citizen's own hours of that trade under \artref{art:labor}, does so as a private commission under \secrefhere{sec:commerce:work-a-citizen-does-for-another}, and the same limits hold.
\end{clauses}
