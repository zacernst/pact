\chapter{Of Labor, Shadowing, and the Assignment}
\label{art:labor}

\articlenote{In which is set down how the silo's citizens are matched to their work, how they are taught to do it, and the principle by which some labor is chosen freely and some is assigned by need.}

\section{The Labor a Citizen Owes}
\label{sec:labor:labor-a-citizen-owes}

Every citizen, from the sixteenth naming-day onward, is bound to contribute labor to the silo's keeping, in such a trade as the citizen's own aptitude, the silo's need, and this Pact together determine. The silo contains one hundred and forty-four levels, built and provisioned to hold many thousands of souls; the work required to keep so many fed, watered, warmed, aired, and sheltered is immense and various. No citizen's labor is purchased by chits or offered freely; it is owed to the silo itself as a debt of citizenship.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen may petition to enter a trade or profession for which the citizen has aptitude and desire, and such petition shall not be refused save where the silo's need for that trade is already met and other work goes short. The silo's keeping stands before any citizen's preference; but a citizen's own petition is weighed first, and is refused in words that say why.
\item Where a trade is in short supply and the silo's need is critical, the Mayor and the Head of the relevant Department may jointly assign a citizen to that trade, without the citizen's consent, provided Judicial confirms that the assignment is not made in punishment under \artref{art:crimes}.
\item A citizen assigned to a trade without consent shall be tested for fitness in that trade; a citizen found unfit may petition Judicial for reassignment, and Judicial's finding shall bind.
\item A citizen who neglects the labor assigned, or who diverts the silo's tools, materials, or the citizen's own owed labor-time to a private study, pursuit, or fixation of the citizen's own devising, wastes what the silo cannot spare. The Head of the citizen's Department shall first correct such neglect by closer oversight or reassignment to labor better suited to occupy the citizen fully; where the neglect persists notwithstanding, the Head shall refer the matter to Judicial.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Testing of Aptitude}
\label{sec:labor:testing-of-aptitude}

From a citizen's twelfth naming-day forward, the schools under \artref{art:schooling} shall assess each child's aptitude for various kinds of labor: the working of tools, the tending of living things, the care of people, the working with numbers, the keeping of records, and such other aptitudes as the silo's work requires. A citizen reaching majority shall have some knowledge of which trades might suit the citizen's own nature.

\begin{clauses}
\item The testing of aptitude is not a trial to be passed or failed; it is a guidance, carried into the citizen's majority and weighed when the citizen first petitions for entry into a trade.
\item A citizen may petition to enter a trade notwithstanding the aptitude testing's results, but a Head of a Department may weigh the results against the petition: where a citizen's expressed desire runs sharply against demonstrated aptitude, the Head may require an extended trial period or additional testing before accepting the citizen's shadow.
\item A citizen who petitions for a trade for which the silo has no current need, but for which the citizen has clear aptitude, may be entered on a roll of those waiting; if the silo's need for that trade grows, the waiting citizen has first claim when a shadow becomes available.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Three Manners of Entering a Trade}
\label{sec:labor:three-manners-of-entering-a-trade}

A citizen enters a trade in one of three manners, according to how long the trade takes to learn: a full shadowing of years at a departing master's side; a short shadowing of some cycles or a season, where the work is of middling skill; or a plain setting to work, where the work can be learned in the doing of it.

\begin{clauses}
\item Full shadowing is the entry into trades of high skill, into trades that answer for the silo's works, and into trades given knowledge or instruments this Pact keeps from other citizens. Such a shadowing runs not less than three years nor more than seven, as the master alone judges; and the shadow lives and works at the master's side, learning not the hands of the trade alone but the judgment the trade requires, which is the slower of the two to teach.
\item Short shadowing is the entry into trades of middling skill, and into the keeping of rolls and records. Such a shadowing runs not fewer than three cycles and ordinarily not longer than one season, or two where the master or the Head of the Department enters a cause for the lengthening.
\item A plain setting to work, without shadowing, is permitted for labor that asks little skill --- the carrying of goods, the sorting of salvage, the handing out of stores, the mending of common things. A citizen so set to work is taught what the work asks, in the doing of it, and enters no contract of shadowing.
\item A citizen who has shadowed under a master may not be removed from that shadow by the citizen's own request, nor by the master's, save with the other's consent or a finding by Judicial that the shadow is unfit. A shadow abandoned by either party without cause is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for the breaking of an undertaking made before Judicial.
\end{clauses}

\section{Mundane Labor and the Simple Trades}
\label{sec:labor:mundane-labor-and-the-simple-trades}

The silo requires much work of simple character: the carrying of goods and messages, the distribution of supplies, the painting and repair of common surfaces, the sorting of salvage, the cleaning of common areas, the tending of the simple tasks of kitchen and cafeteria. These trades require no formal shadowing, little specialized training, and are open to any citizen of sufficient strength or steadiness for the work.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen wishing to enter such work may do so upon the petition to the relevant Head of Department or, for work not assigned to any Department, to the Mayor. The Head shall assign the citizen to the work or place the citizen on a list of those available.
\item No license, restricted access, or formal testing is required for such work, save that a citizen assigned to the keeping or moving of the silo's stored goods is subject to such reckoning of what is carried against what is delivered as the Head of the Department orders.
\item A citizen engaged in simple labor may be set to other such labor by the Head of the Department at will, with one cycle's notice.
\end{clauses}

\section{Skilled Trades and Full Shadowing}
\label{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing}

Upon some trades the silo itself depends: the tending of the generator and the works of water and air, the working of metal and the mending of machinery, the keeping of the food stores and the growing of crops, the healing of the sick, the keeping of the records, the inquiry into crime and the carrying out of sentences, the teaching of the young. These are learned in years and not in cycles, at a master's side, and are entered by full shadowing and in no other way.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen wishing to enter a skilled trade shall petition the Head of the relevant Department. The Head reads the citizen's aptitude testing, sits with the citizen and hears the citizen's reasons, and accepts the petition or refuses it. A refusal may be carried to Judicial.
\item Where a shadow is accepted, a master who possesses the trade and plans to step down from it shall take the shadow as an apprentice. A master who does not plan to step down may be required by the Head of Department to take a shadow if the Department's needs require it, provided Judicial finds the requirement just.
\item The shadow is tested throughout the shadowing, at set trials and in the ordinary run of the work. A shadow found unfit may be dismissed by the master or the Head of Department, provided Judicial is informed; a shadow dismissed may petition Judicial for reassignment to a different trade.
\item Upon the completion of shadowing, the master shall attest the citizen's readiness to the Head of Department. The Head shall confirm the citizen in the trade, or, if the Head finds the citizen unfit despite the master's attestation, shall refer the matter to Judicial for a ruling.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Trades of Trust}
\label{sec:labor:trades-of-trust}

Some trades are given what other trades are not: the silo's instruments, the silo's records, the silo's children, or the power to lay a hand upon a citizen. These are the trades of trust --- among them the technicians of Information Technology, the workers of the sensors and the wallscreen, the technicians of the cleaning lab, the teachers of the young, the Officers of Judicial, and the Physicians of the Infirmary. A citizen enters such a trade by full shadowing and by the further vetting this section requires, and by nothing less.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen entering a trade of trust shall undergo, beyond the shadowing, such further trial and vetting as the Head of the Department determines; and the vetting may take in an examination by Judicial, an inquiry by the Officers of Judicial, and a weighing of the citizen's steadiness, the citizen's silence, and the citizen's keeping of this Pact.
\item No citizen is compelled to undergo the vetting, and a citizen refusing it is not set to a trade of trust; a citizen may withdraw from the shadowing of such a trade at any point, and is not answerable for the withdrawal.
\item A citizen confirmed in a trade of trust is subject to the vetting for as long as the citizen holds it; the Head of the Department may vet again at any time, and may put the citizen out of the trade where the vetting gives cause, entering that it did and not what it found.
\item What the vetting of a particular trade of trust consists in is not set down in this Pact, but is determined by the Head of the Department and kept in the Judicial archive. No vetting may run against this Pact, nor require of a citizen what this Pact forbids.
\item No citizen afflicted with the Syndrome, as \artref{art:health} describes it, may hold any public office of the silo --- the Mayor, the Deputy Mayor, the Judge, the Sheriff, or the Head of any Department --- nor enter, hold, or continue in any trade of trust under this section. A citizen so afflicted shall report the affliction to the Head Physician and resign the office or role at once, and a successor is named as the Article governing that office provides; where no Article provides, the relevant Head of Department or the Mayor shall proceed by the shadowing and vetting of this Article to confirm one.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Silo's Need for Hands, and the Meeting of It}
\label{sec:labor:silos-need-for-hands}

The silo's need for hands is not the same from one year to the next: citizens retire, citizens die, citizens are sent to clean, and the work itself shifts as the silo ages. This section sets down how that need is reckoned, and how it is met where the citizens' own petitions do not meet it.

\begin{clauses}
\item Each Head of Department tells the Mayor, once in the year and again whenever the Department's need changes, how many hands the Department wants and in what trades. The Mayor, having heard the Heads and the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, sets down the silo's need for the year to come, and the setting down is entered in the archive and read at the ordinary sitting.
\item Where citizens' own petitions for a trade exceed the silo's need for that trade, the excess petitioners are entered on a roll of those waiting, in the order of their petitions; as citizens retire or die, the roll is drawn upon in that order.
\item Where the silo's need for a trade exceeds the number of citizens who have petitioned for it, and the roll of those waiting is exhausted, the Mayor and the relevant Head of Department may jointly assign citizens to that trade, subject to Judicial confirmation as provided in \secrefhere{sec:labor:labor-a-citizen-owes}.
\item No citizen of majority is left without a trade or a labor, and no trade is left empty without a shadow being made ready for it, save in an emergency under \artref{art:emergency}. A citizen who finds no trade to petition for is set by the Mayor to the simple labor of \secrefhere{sec:labor:mundane-labor-and-the-simple-trades}, and keeps a place on the roll of those waiting while so set.
\end{clauses}

\section{Reassignment and Retirement}
\label{sec:labor:reassignment-and-retirement}

A citizen's first trade is not the citizen's last. A citizen may petition to leave one trade for another, having served five years in a trade of full shadowing or one year in simple labor; a Department may move a citizen where the citizen's fitness is in question or the silo's need has shifted; and a citizen may lay down labor altogether at such an age as the Infirmary and the Mayor together set.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen petitioning for reassignment to a different trade petitions and shadows as any citizen entering that trade does, save that the Head of the new Department may set the citizen's aptitude testing aside where years well served in another trade of full shadowing show the aptitude plainly.
\item A citizen removed from a trade for cause --- unfitness, abandonment of the shadowing, or breach of the citizen's duties --- may petition for reassignment to a different trade, and Judicial shall rule on the petition; Judicial may grant reassignment, deny it, or require a period of service in mundane labor before permitting a reassignment to a new skilled trade.
\item A citizen reaching such age as the Infirmary judges the citizen no longer fit for the citizen's current trade may be reassigned to simpler labor, or, if unfit for any labor, may petition for retirement. Retirement ends what this Article asks of the citizen, and the citizen is thereafter kept by the silo under \artref{art:health}.
\item Where a Department's need for a trade falls away, and the citizens of that trade cannot be set to other work within the Department, they are set to another Department's need, or taught a kindred trade, or retired where age and fitness allow it. No citizen is ever put out of labor without another labor or a retirement, and a Head who so puts a citizen out answers to Judicial for it.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Rights and Duties of the Shadowed}
\label{sec:labor:rights-and-duties-of-the-shadowed}

A citizen in shadowing is in a status between ordinary citizenship and full membership in a trade. The shadow owes the master and the silo earnest labor and obedience to the master's teaching; the master, the Department, and the silo owe the shadow fair usage, keeping from harm, and a judgment made upon fitness truly found and upon nothing else.

\begin{clauses}
\item A shadow shall receive the same ration as any other citizen under \artref{art:supply}, and shall not be docked for tools, materials, or lodging during the shadowing period, save where the shadow has been assigned to lodging within the Department's own quarters, as a Down Deep trade requiring residence in Mechanical's own space.
\item A shadow may not be punished by the master for ordinary mistakes in learning, but may be dismissed for willful negligence, refusal to obey instruction, or breach of the Pact. A shadow dismissed draws the chits of the time worked, and owes nothing to the master, the Department, or the silo for the teaching received.
\item A master may not strike, deprive of food, or otherwise assault a shadow, and a shadow subjected to such treatment may appeal to Judicial or the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff} for relief and may be released from the shadowing without penalty.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Contract of Shadowing}
\label{sec:labor:contract-of-shadowing}

A shadowing is not begun by a master's nod; it is entered before Judicial, and it binds as an entry binds.

\begin{clauses}
\item Before a shadowing of either kind begins, the master, the shadow, and the Head of the Department present before the Clerk of Judicial, or before a deputy of Judicial sent to the Department's floor, and enter the contract of shadowing: the manner of entry under \secrefhere{sec:labor:three-manners-of-entering-a-trade}, the master's name and the shadow's, the day the shadowing begins, the span it is to run, the lodging the shadow is to have under \secrefhere{sec:labor:rights-and-duties-of-the-shadowed}, and whether the trade is a trade of trust under \secrefhere{sec:labor:trades-of-trust}.
\item The three sign the entry, and the Clerk gives a copy to each; the entry is kept in the archive under \artref{art:records}, and the shadow's occupant card is entered with the trade and the word \emph{shadow} beside it, which is struck upon confirmation.
\item The span entered is the master's judgment under \secrefhere{sec:labor:three-manners-of-entering-a-trade}, and may be lengthened once by the master, by a further entry with the Clerk, for cause the master states; it may not be shortened save by the confirmation under \secrefhere{sec:labor:ending-of-a-shadowing}, and a shadowing that runs past its span without confirmation or dismissal is reported by the Clerk to the Head of the Department, who rules upon it within one cycle.
\item A shadow of a trade of trust enters, beside the contract, the vetting \secrefhere{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} requires, and the contract does not bind until the Head has entered that the vetting is passed; a shadow who withdraws under \secrefhere{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} before that entry withdraws without mark.
\item No citizen holds two contracts of shadowing at once, and no master takes two shadows at once in one trade, save where the Head of the Department enters that the trade's need requires it and Judicial finds the master able.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Conduct of a Shadowing}
\label{sec:labor:conduct-of-a-shadowing}

What a master owes a shadow, and a shadow a master, between the entry and the confirmation.

\begin{clauses}
\item A shadow works the master's own hours and the master's own shifts, in the master's presence, and is given no work of the trade the master is not present for until the master enters in the shadow's record that the shadow may be; a shadow given such work before that entry is not answerable for what comes of it, and the master is.
\item The master keeps a record of the shadowing, in the Department's own roll, entering at each turn of the season what the shadow has been taught, what the shadow can do alone, and what the shadow cannot yet; the shadow reads the entry and signs it, or enters beside it what the shadow disputes, and the Head of the Department reads every record at each turn of the season.
\item A shadow is fed, lodged, and given the rest day as any citizen, and draws the shadow's chits under \artref{art:commerce} at the rate of the trade's simple labor until confirmed; a shadow lodged in a Department's own quarters under \secrefhere{sec:labor:rights-and-duties-of-the-shadowed} keeps the household's dwelling under \artref{art:housing} where the shadow has one, and Housing does not reassign it for the shadowing's span.
\item A shadow may be sent by the master to another master of the same trade, in the same Department, for a season, for teaching the master cannot give; the sending is entered in the record, and the second master enters the season's teaching, and the first master's contract stands.
\item Where the master falls sick, retires, dies, or is removed before the span runs, the Head of the Department names another master of the trade within one cycle, and the contract is entered anew with the Clerk under \secrefhere{sec:labor:contract-of-shadowing} for what remains of the span, the seasons already served counted; a shadow whose master is sent to clean is so re-entered without mark, and the archive does not record the cause of the re-entry against the shadow's name.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Ending of a Shadowing}
\label{sec:labor:ending-of-a-shadowing}

A shadowing ends in one of four ways, and each is entered.

\begin{clauses}
\item Confirmation: upon the span's end, or before it where the master enters that the shadow is ready, the master attests the shadow's readiness to the Head of the Department under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing}, and the Head confirms the shadow in the trade by an entry with the Clerk of Judicial, or refers the matter to Judicial as that Section provides; the shadow is of the trade from the entry, and the master's record is closed and kept in the archive.
\item Dismissal: a master or a Head who finds a shadow unfit under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing} enters the finding and the cause with the Clerk, and the shadow is dismissed from the entry; a shadow dismissed may petition Judicial under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing}, and Judicial hears the master, the Head, and the shadow, and may restore the shadowing, end it without mark, or end it with the mark the master entered.
\item Release: a shadow who withdraws from a trade of trust under \secrefhere{sec:labor:trades-of-trust}, or a shadow and master who agree to part under \secrefhere{sec:labor:three-manners-of-entering-a-trade}, enter the release with the Clerk, and the shadowing ends without mark; the shadow petitions another trade under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing} as any citizen, and the seasons served are credited to a shadowing of the same trade under another master where the Head so enters.
\item Abandonment: a shadow who leaves the master's hours and the trade's floor for one cycle without the master's leave, or a master who withholds the trade's teaching from a shadow for one season without cause entered, has abandoned the contract, and the other party enters it with the Clerk; the party who abandoned is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as \secrefhere{sec:labor:three-manners-of-entering-a-trade} provides, and the shadowing is ended or re-entered under \secrefhere{sec:labor:conduct-of-a-shadowing} as the Head judges.
\item Upon confirmation, the shadow inherits what the master steps down from: the master's tools licensed under \artref{art:it} and \artref{art:mechanical}, re-licensed to the shadow within one cycle; the master's place in the Department's shifts; and, where the master steps down from a trade lodged in a Department's own quarters, the master's quarters, the master being lodged by Housing under \artref{art:housing} within the season.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Master's Own Standing}
\label{sec:labor:masters-own-standing}

A master is a citizen of the trade who has undertaken to leave it, and the silo keeps faith with the undertaking.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen of a trade of full shadowing becomes a master by entering, with the Head of the Department, an undertaking to step down from the trade upon a shadow's confirmation, or upon a span the citizen names; a citizen may be required to take a shadow under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing} without such an undertaking, and is then a master for the shadow's span only, and keeps the trade after.
\item A master who has undertaken to step down keeps every standing of the trade until the shadow is confirmed, and steps down on the day of confirmation; a master who steps down is reassigned under \secrefhere{sec:labor:reassignment-and-retirement} to such lighter labor of the same Department as the Head gives, or retires under \artref{art:health} where the Infirmary finds the master fit for none, and keeps the dwelling under \artref{art:housing} for one season after stepping down whatever the reassignment.
\item A master's judgment of a shadow's readiness under \secrefhere{sec:labor:ending-of-a-shadowing} is the master's alone, and no Head or officer may order a master to attest a shadow ready; a Head who finds a master withholding attestation without cause refers the matter to Judicial, which may name another master of the trade to examine the shadow and attest in the first master's place, entering why.
\item A master who has taught three shadows to confirmation in one trade is entered in the Department's roll as a master of the trade for life, and may be asked by the Head to examine any shadow of the trade, or to sit with Judicial in a matter under \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing} touching the trade, and is not required to take a further shadow.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Citizen Whose Body or Mind Cannot Bear the Labor}
\label{sec:labor:citizen-who-cannot-bear-the-labor}

Not every citizen is given a body or a mind that will do all the silo asks of it. Such a citizen owes the silo labor still, and the silo owes such a citizen a labor that can be done. This section is not the retirement of \secref{art:health}{sec:health:retirement-and-the-care-of-the-aged-and-infirm}, which comes at the end of a working life, nor the Syndrome of \secref{art:health}{sec:health:fitness-for-trades-of-trust-and-the-syndrome}, which bars what it bars; it governs a citizen who will work for forty years at something, and must be found the something.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Infirmary attests, at the examination of \secref{art:health}{sec:health:care-a-citizen-must-take} and at any time after, that a citizen cannot bear labor of a given kind --- by sight, by hearing, by limb, by breath, by strength, by the carrying of weight upon the stair, or by the cast of the citizen's mind. The attestation sets down what the citizen cannot do and, at no less length, what the citizen can. It is entered in the citizen's record under \artref{art:health} and copied to the Head of the Department the citizen is set to, and to no other Department and no other office.
\item A citizen so attested names a trade as any citizen does under \secrefhere{sec:labor:testing-of-aptitude} and \secrefhere{sec:labor:skilled-trades-and-full-shadowing}, and the Head weighs the attestation and not the citizen's bearing or the Head's own guess at it. Where no trade of that Department can be fitted to the attestation, the Head says so in writing, with the trades considered set down, and sends the citizen to the Mayor, who finds the citizen a labor under \secrefhere{sec:labor:silos-need-for-hands}. Such a citizen is never left without labor, and is never set to labor the attestation says the citizen cannot bear; a Head who so sets a citizen answers to Judicial for it.
\item The silo bends the labor where the labor will bend: the hour, the floor, the length of the stair, the tool, the lamp, the shift, and the setting of another hand alongside. The Head enters what was bent and what the bending cost the Department, and the cost is not reckoned against the citizen's chits under \artref{art:commerce} nor against the citizen's standing in the trade.
\item What this Pact does by writing, by posting, and by petition, it does for such a citizen by another hand. A notice posted under \artref{art:sheriff} is read aloud to a citizen who cannot read it, by the Deputy or Peacekeeper of the floor, upon the day of its posting. A petition is made by the Clerk's hand as \artref{art:judicial} provides. The occupant card is carried and shown by a citizen of the household where the citizen cannot carry or show it. And the silence of such a citizen is never taken for consent, and never taken for refusal, but is asked again by another hand.
\item The household in which such a citizen is entered under \artref{art:citizenship} sees the citizen to the Infirmary, to the schoolroom under \artref{art:schooling}, and to the examination this Pact requires. Where the household cannot, Housing under \artref{art:housing} enters the citizen in another household that undertakes it, with the citizen's own ration and such further measure as the Head of Supply sets; and a household that so undertakes does not lose its dwelling by the enlargement, nor its place in any petition under \artref{art:marriage}.
\item Such a citizen is a citizen entire. The citizen is counted in the rolls, counted in the Assembly present under \artref{art:assembly}, and counted against every petition this Pact reckons by a part of the silo; may marry under \artref{art:marriage} and petition the lottery; may hold any trade the attestation does not bar, the trades of trust under \secrefhere{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} not excepted; and is heard before Judicial in the citizen's own voice, or by the voice of a citizen the citizen names. Nothing in this section makes a citizen a ward under \artref{art:citizenship}, and no attestation under it may be read as making one.
\item The attestation is read again by a Physician at each turn of the year, and is changed where the citizen has changed, for better or for worse. A citizen may ask at any time that it be read again, and may carry a Physician's refusal to the Head Physician, and the Head Physician's refusal to Judicial.
\end{clauses}
