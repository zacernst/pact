\chapter{Of Housing and the Floors}
\label{art:housing}

\articlenote{In which is set down the keeping of living quarters, the assignment of floors by the silo's zones, what a household answers for in the place it is given, and the rule that every citizen shall have shelter and no citizen more than is needful.}

\section{The Charge of Housing}
\label{sec:housing:charge-of-housing}

Every citizen shall have a place to sleep that is fit for the citizen's household and within what the silo has to give. Dwellings are kept by Housing, which is an office of the Department of Supply under \artref{art:supply} and answers to the Head of Supply. No citizen holds a dwelling as a thing owned; no citizen takes up a dwelling or a floor but by Housing's assignment; and no assignment is moved or changed save by the same hand that made it, which shall not move it out of favor or spite.

\begin{clauses}
\item A dwelling is given to a household by the count of its citizens and by its need: a citizen living alone, an aged citizen without household, or a ward too young to keep itself, a single room or small quarters; two citizens married or joined under \artref{art:marriage}, quarters that will hold the two of them; a household with children, quarters that will hold them all, with a further room as each child is born under the lottery of \artref{art:marriage} and the rooms of the floor allow.
\item A household may change what is within a dwelling --- the paint, the standing of the furnishings, a small mending --- and shall leave the dwelling as it was found when it gives the dwelling up; what touches the walls, the floor, the ceiling, or the dwelling's works is mended by the menders of Supply under \artref{art:supply} and by no other hand.
\item A floor is dwelt upon chiefly by the citizens of the trades set to that floor under \artref{art:labor}, and by their households, that a trade's hands be within reach of the trade's work and the silo not be kept waiting upon the stair.
\item The Head of Supply may take back a dwelling where the household has not kept it, where the household has broken \secrefhere{sec:housing:keeping-of-dwelling-quarters}, or where the citizen's trade has moved to another floor; the taking back is given in writing with the cause set down in it, and the household may carry it to Judicial, which hears the keeper and the household and rules.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Zones and the Division of the Silo}
\label{sec:housing:zones-and-the-division-of-the-silo}

The silo is divided into three zones by the character of the work done there and the citizens assigned to each, exactly as \artref{art:founding} sets them down: the Up Top, its uppermost levels, where the Mayor, Judicial, the Sheriff, the chief schoolrooms of the young, and the common areas of assembly and congregation are kept; the Mid Thirties and the Mids beyond them, where most citizen households are located, and the markets, the Infirmary, Information Technology, and the ordinary trades of the silo are carried out; and the Down Deep, the silo's lowest levels, where the Department of Mechanical, the Department of Mines, the generator, the water treatment and stores, and the deep-storage of the silo's reserves are kept, and where only the citizens assigned to those Departments shall ordinarily dwell.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen may not occupy a dwelling in the Down Deep save where that citizen is assigned by trade to the Department of Mechanical, the Department of Mines, or to a support trade kept deep for the works of those Departments, and no other citizen, save by the explicit written permission of the Head of Supply, may lodge or sleep in the Down Deep overnight.
\item The Up Top is reserved for the officers and works of the silo's governance, the chief schoolrooms of the young, and the common gathering of the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}; which does not hinder the keeping of a schoolroom in the Mids and in the Down Deep under \artref{art:schooling}, nor the housing of its teachers upon the floor they teach; dwellings in the Up Top are given by the Mayor, the Head of Supply being heard, and are given ordinarily to the citizens holding the offices of \artref{art:mayor} and of Judicial under \artref{art:judicial}, and to such teachers and scribes as \artref{art:schooling} and \artref{art:records} require, and to no citizen for the view.
\item A citizen of the Mids may petition to work in the Up Top or the Down Deep, and the petition is weighed as \artref{art:labor} provides; a citizen granted it is given a dwelling on the floor of the work, or leave to keep the Mids dwelling and walk the stair each day, which leave is entered and may be withdrawn.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Keeping of Dwelling Quarters}
\label{sec:housing:keeping-of-dwelling-quarters}

A citizen occupying a dwelling is answerable for the condition of that dwelling and for the conduct of the citizen's household within it. This Pact does not require that neighbors be fond of one another, or even civil; it requires only that a household be no danger and no breach to the dwellings around it and to the floor it stands on.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen's household shall not produce vermin, filth, or odor that spreads beyond the dwelling itself, nor shall a citizen's household contain more citizens than the dwelling is assigned to accommodate, nor shall a citizen harbor a citizen unknown to the rolls or hide a child born without the lottery's sanction.
\item A citizen may not keep an animal not sanctioned under \secref{art:supply}{sec:supply:sanctioned-animals-kept-by-citizens}, nor forbid entry to the menders of Supply where a repair to the dwelling's systems is required.
\item A citizen may not modify the structural integrity of a dwelling, nor seal off doors or passages, nor make permanent alterations without the approval of the Head of Supply and the approval, where relevant, of the Head of Mechanical for concerns affecting the silo's structure.
\item A citizen who breaches this section's requirements is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}; if the breach endangers the floor's stability or the water, air, or waste-flow systems under Mechanical's charge, it is among the gravest offenses under \artref{art:crimes}.
\end{clauses}

\section{What Is Private in a Dwelling, and What Is Not}
\label{sec:housing:what-is-private-in-a-dwelling}

A citizen's dwelling is the one place in the silo that is the citizen's own, and what passes within it is the household's. Yet the door is not a wall this Pact cannot reach: where the silo's safety or the keeping of this Pact requires it, and only then, the door is opened, and this section sets down by whose hand and upon what warrant.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Sheriff may enter a dwelling to stop a crime in progress, to investigate a death, or to arrest a citizen for an offense. In all other cases, the Sheriff shall request entry, and may enter only if a citizen of the household consents or if the Judge has ordered a search under \artref{art:judicial}.
\item The Officers of Judicial, when executing a search ordered by the Judge under \artref{art:judicial}, may search a dwelling and the citizen's private possessions; such a search is made in the waking hours of the silo's day, save where the Judge's order says otherwise and why, breaks no more than the searching requires, leaves the dwelling as near as may be to what it was, and is reported to Judicial for the archive under \artref{art:judicial} with a list of everything taken.
\item A search made without the Judge's written order, in a case this section does not except, is a breach of this Pact; the household may refuse the door, and may carry the matter to Judicial, which hears it and enters the finding against the officer's name.
\end{clauses}

\section{That No Citizen Go Unsheltered}
\label{sec:housing:that-no-citizen-go-unsheltered}

No citizen sleeps on the stair. Every citizen shall have shelter fit for the citizen's household, and no citizen is put out of a dwelling save for a cause this Pact names; and the Head of Supply, the Mayor being heard, answers to the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly} that no citizen who keeps this Pact is left without a place to sleep.

\begin{clauses}
\item Where a citizen is displaced from a dwelling by catastrophe, by the citizen's release from the mines or from cleaning, or by the moving of the citizen's trade to another floor, the Head of Supply gives new quarters within one cycle, and gives the household a place to sleep the same day.
\item A household to be put out of a dwelling for a breach of this Article may carry the matter to Judicial; and Judicial may find the breach smaller than it was called, or set a day by which the household shall make it good and stay if it does, or uphold the putting out and send the household to a holding room until Housing assigns it anew.
\item A citizen is owed a dwelling and is not owed the choosing of it. A citizen may petition for other quarters, and the Head of Supply weighs the petition honestly and answers it in writing; but no citizen's wish binds the Head where the silo's need runs the other way, and a petition refused may be made again after one season.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Roll of Dwellings}
\label{sec:housing:roll-of-dwellings}

Every dwelling in the silo has a number, and every number has a household or is entered empty.

\begin{clauses}
\item Housing keeps a roll of every dwelling, by floor and by number, entering for each its size in rooms, the household assigned to it and every citizen of that household by name, the day of assignment, and, for a dwelling empty, the day it was left and its condition; the roll is delivered to the Clerk of Judicial at each turn of the season under \artref{art:citizenship}, and the count of dwellings empty is reported to the Mayor with it.
\item No dwelling is entered to two households, and no household to two dwellings, save a citizen of a Department lodged in its own quarters under \artref{art:labor} who keeps a household's dwelling elsewhere, which is entered so.
\item A dwelling is a single room or a set of rooms with a door that closes, a light, water, a vent of air, and a place for waste, as the builders made it; a room without these is not a dwelling under this Article and is not assigned as one, whatever a citizen would take.
\item The roll is open to any citizen at Housing's own office in the Mids, as to the number and size of dwellings on any floor and which stand empty, and to no citizen as to the households of others.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Petition and the Assignment}
\label{sec:housing:petition-and-the-assignment}

A dwelling is asked for in writing and given in writing, and the order of giving is fixed.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen who requires a dwelling, or a different one, petitions Housing in writing, or by the keeper's hand where the citizen cannot write, stating the household, its size, the floor of the citizen's trade, and the cause; the keeper enters the petition in a roll of petitions with the day, and the petition stands until granted or withdrawn.
\item Housing assigns dwellings as they stand empty in this order, and no other, whatever the day of petition: first, a household newly made by marriage or by a sanctioned relationship under \artref{art:marriage}; second, a household granted a lottery's place under \artref{art:marriage} whose dwelling has no room for a child; third, a citizen returned from the mines, a partner lodged anew upon a dissolution under \artref{art:marriage}, or a citizen displaced under \secrefhere{sec:housing:that-no-citizen-go-unsheltered}; fourth, a household enlarged by the taking-in of kin under \artref{art:marriage}; fifth, a citizen whose trade has moved to another floor; and last, a citizen who petitions for preference alone. Within each order, the earlier petition is granted first.
\item An assignment is made in writing, signed by the keeper and countersigned by the Head of Supply, naming the household, the dwelling by floor and number, and the day the household may take it up; a copy is given to the household, a copy entered in the roll of dwellings, and a copy sent to the Clerk of Judicial for the rolls of citizenship under \artref{art:citizenship}.
\item A household assigned a dwelling takes it up within one cycle of the day named, or the assignment lapses and the dwelling passes to the next petition, the household's own petition standing where it stood; a household that cannot take it up within the cycle for a cause the keeper finds good may have one further cycle, entered.
\item A citizen who believes an assignment made out of the order this section fixes may petition Judicial under \artref{art:judicial}, and Judicial hears the keeper and rules; an assignment found out of order is not undone, but the citizen wronged is given the next dwelling of the same size that stands empty on the same floor, before any other petition.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Taking-Up and the Leaving of a Dwelling}
\label{sec:housing:taking-up-and-the-leaving-of-a-dwelling}

A dwelling is handed over as it is found, and taken back as it was handed.

\begin{clauses}
\item Before a household takes up a dwelling, a mender of Supply goes through it with a citizen of the household, and the two enter its condition in a writing kept with the assignment: every room, its light, its water, its vent, its door, and the common furnishings this Pact gives with a dwelling --- a bed for each citizen, a table, a chair for each, a shelf, a lamp --- by count and by condition; both sign, and the household answers for the dwelling from the signing.
\item A household leaving a dwelling gives Housing one cycle's notice where it can, restores the dwelling as \secrefhere{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} requires, and goes through it again with a mender of Supply on the day of leaving; what is found wanting or broken beyond the ordinary wear of living is entered, and the household makes it good in chits under \artref{art:commerce} at a rate Mechanical sets for the thing, or in labor at Housing's direction, before the household's next assignment is made.
\item A dwelling left by the death of its whole household, or by a household's sentencing or cleaning, is gone through by a mender of Supply with a Deputy of the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff}, and the household's goods are given to kin under \artref{art:marriage} who claim them within one cycle, or to Mechanical for reclamation after, and the giving entered.
\item A dwelling stands empty no longer than one cycle after it is left and made good, save where Housing enters that no petition stands for a dwelling of its size on its floor; a dwelling empty beyond a season is reported to the Mayor with the reason.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Dwelling's Systems and Their Keeping}
\label{sec:housing:dwellings-systems-and-their-keeping}

What runs into a dwelling and out of it is the silo's, and a household keeps it as the silo's.

\begin{clauses}
\item The water that enters a dwelling, the vent that airs it, the line that lights it, and the pipe that carries its waste are the works of Mechanical under \artref{art:mechanical} to the wall of the dwelling and within it, and no household opens, stops, alters, or draws upon any of them beyond the tap, the switch, and the vent's grille the builders gave.
\item A household keeps the vent's grille clear, the tap closed when not drawing, the waste-pipe free of what it was not made to carry, and the light out when the household sleeps; a household found wasting water or light, or fouling the waste-pipe, is warned by the mender once, entered, and answerable under \artref{art:crimes} thereafter as for the diversion of stores.
\item A fault in any of the dwelling's systems is reported by the household to Housing the same day, and a mender of Supply comes within one cycle, or within one day where the fault is a leak, a stopped vent, or a smell of the works; a household that does not report a fault answers for what the fault does to the dwelling and the floor.
\item A mender of Supply, or a citizen of Mechanical the Head sends, enters a dwelling to reach its systems upon notice to the household of one day, or without notice where the fault endangers the floor, and enters the entry and what was done; the household may be present and may not refuse the entry, as \secrefhere{sec:housing:keeping-of-dwelling-quarters} provides.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Common Rooms of a Floor}
\label{sec:housing:common-rooms-of-a-floor}

A floor is more than its dwellings, and what lies between them is kept by all of them together.

\begin{clauses}
\item The washrooms, the landing, the floor's own common room where the builders gave one, and the passages between dwellings are the common ground of the floor, kept by the households of the floor by turns the floor's Deputy or Peacekeeper under \artref{art:sheriff} posts at the landing, each household taking its turn by the number of its dwelling.
\item A household that does not keep its turn is entered by the Peacekeeper, and after three entries in a season is referred to Housing, which may assign the household's next turn doubled, or, after three such referrals, revoke the dwelling under \secrefhere{sec:housing:charge-of-housing}; a household of one citizen retired or sick under \artref{art:health} is excused its turn upon the Head Physician's word, entered.
\item The common room of a floor may be used by the households of the floor for any gathering this Pact does not forbid under \artref{art:publicorder}, without leave, and by any other gathering only with the leave of the floor's Deputy; a gathering in a common room is a gathering of the floor for the purposes of \secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:rumor-despair-and-the-spread-of-discontent}.
\item A dispute between households of a floor over the common ground, its turns, or its use is brought to the floor's Peacekeeper, who resolves it by word or refers it to the Deputy, and thence to the Sheriff under \artref{art:sheriff}; such a dispute is no matter for Judicial unless the Sheriff so refers it.
\end{clauses}
