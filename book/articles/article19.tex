\chapter{Of Health, the Infirmary, and the Mind}
\label{art:health}

\articlenote{In which is set down the charge of the Infirmary and of the Physicians who tend it, the care a citizen is required to take, the isolation of the sick, the finding of a cause of death, and the keeping of those who can no longer earn their keep by labor.}

\section{The Charge and the Physicians}
\label{sec:health:charge-and-the-physicians}

The Department of the Infirmary is charged with the health of every citizen: the prevention of disease, the care and recovery of the sick and injured, the certification of fitness for trade and for labor, and the keeping of the records of every citizen's health that the Pact requires. The Infirmary keeps its floors within the Mids, and is tended by the Physicians of the Infirmary under a Head Physician, raised up and confirmed by the shadowing of \artref{art:labor}.

The Infirmary's charge extends to the disorders and afflictions of a citizen's mind no less than of the citizen's body: a Physician who examines a citizen troubled in mind keeps the same record, in the same confidence, and by the same procedure as for any other complaint, and no citizen is treated for such a trouble save by a Physician under this Article.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Physician of the Infirmary is licensed by the Head Physician to undertake medical care, and may administer medicines, set broken bones, suture wounds, treat a citizen's disorders of mind, and determine the cause of death under \artref{art:citizenship}; no other citizen may undertake such care save where no Physician is available and immediate aid is required to preserve life.
\item The Infirmary shall keep records of every citizen's health that the Infirmary observes or is told of: inoculations, treatments received, injuries, illnesses, and such conditions as affect the citizen's fitness for certain labors. These records are kept in the Judicial archive under \artref{art:judicial}, and remain confidential, open to the citizen named and to Judicial only, save where this Pact elsewhere requires disclosure.
\item A citizen shall have the right to refuse medical treatment, save where the Pact expressly compels it: the treatment of a communicable disease that threatens the silo; the examination required by \secrefhere{sec:health:care-a-citizen-must-take}; the fitting and keeping of the measure under \secref{art:marriage}{sec:marriage:fitting-and-keeping-of-the-measure}; and the examination and restraint of a citizen whom the Judge, upon a referral or petition under \secrefhere{sec:health:citizen-in-danger-of-own-hand}, orders examined and kept.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Care a Citizen Must Take}
\label{sec:health:care-a-citizen-must-take}

A citizen's health is the citizen's own, and is also the silo's: a body that fails before its time takes with it the labor the silo counted upon. Every citizen is therefore bound to come to the Infirmary to be examined, inoculated, and kept from disease, at such times as the Head Physician and the Mayor set down. A citizen who will not come may be set by the Head of the citizen's Department to lighter labor, or retired where there is no lighter labor to set the citizen to.

\begin{clauses}
\item Every citizen of majority comes, at such turns as the Head Physician sets, to be examined: the body looked over, the citizen's health record read against it, and the citizen attested fit or unfit for the trade the citizen holds. A citizen attested unfit is set to other labor, or restricted in the labor held, under \artref{art:labor}.
\item A citizen who refuses the examination, or who puts it off again and again, may be brought to it by the Sheriff, Judicial being told the same day; and a citizen so brought may be heard before Judicial as to whether the examination was required of the citizen at all.
\item The Infirmary and the Department of Supply keep open to every citizen such places as serve the body's strength --- the yards for exercise, the baths, the steam rooms, and the grounds where citizens contest at games. No citizen is compelled to use them, and no citizen is marked for not using them. Where a Physician sees a citizen's strength failing in earnest, the Head Physician may advise lighter labor or other labor, and enters the advice in the citizen's record.
\item Every citizen of majority is to know where the nearest aid station stands, how a burn and a wound are dressed, how a catching illness shows itself, and that it must be reported. The teaching of these things falls to the schooling of \artref{art:schooling} and to the Infirmary itself, and is taught again to every citizen at each turn of the year in the common halls.
\end{clauses}

\section{Communicable Illness and the Isolation of the Sick}
\label{sec:health:communicable-illness-and-the-isolation-of-the-sick}

A catching illness in a silo has nowhere to go but through it. The Infirmary may therefore set apart a citizen believed to carry such an illness, compel the treatment of it, and keep the citizen from the company of others; and no citizen's unwillingness stands against this, there being no second silo to send the illness to.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Physician who believes a citizen to be suffering from a communicable disease shall isolate that citizen, notify Judicial and the Sheriff, and undertake such treatment as the Physician judges necessary. A citizen so set apart is given food, water, and care, and is told each day how long the Physician expects the setting apart to run, but may not leave the place of it without the Head Physician's written leave.
\item A citizen who violates the isolation ordered by a Physician, or who knowingly exposes others to a communicable illness, is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} for endangerment.
\item The silo's water, its air, the carrying away of its waste, and the handling of its food are watched by the Infirmary together with the Department of Mechanical under \artref{art:mechanical} and the Department of Supply under \artref{art:supply}, that no illness travel by the works that serve every floor at once.
\end{clauses}

\section{Death and the Determination of Cause}
\label{sec:health:death-and-the-determination-of-cause}

When a citizen dies, a Physician of the Infirmary shall examine the body and determine the cause of death, entering the finding in the Infirmary records and reporting to Judicial under \artref{art:citizenship}. The silo counts its dead as carefully as its living, and knows by these findings what it is that kills it.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Physician who examines a body shall, to the best of the Physician's knowledge, determine and report the cause of death, entering the finding upon the Infirmary's own Form of Death --- the citizen's name and naming-day, the floor and hour the body was found, the examining Physician's own name, and the finding itself, set down in that order and no other --- including whether the death is believed to be by illness, injury, age, or by the citizen's own will outside the clean request under \artref{art:cleaning}.
\item Where a citizen is believed to have caused the citizen's own death by means other than a clean request under \artref{art:cleaning}, the Physician shall report this finding to Judicial upon the same Form, and Judicial shall investigate and determine whether the finding is accurate before closing the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}. This investigation is not delayed; Judicial shall make its determination within a season.
\item Where a citizen dies by the cleaning procedure under \artref{art:cleaning}, the Form of Death this section otherwise requires is not completed, and a Physician need not be present; Information Technology under \artref{art:it} shall certify instead, by its own record and in its own keeping, that the sensors were properly maintained and the procedure was carried out as ordained, and this certification is sufficient for Judicial to close the rolls, provided the cleaning was either sentenced by the Judge under \artref{art:crimes} or sought by the citizen's own final request under \artref{art:cleaning}, and no evidence suggests otherwise.
\item A Physician who falsifies a cause of death, or who fails to report a suspected self-killing to Judicial, is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} among the gravest offenses.
\end{clauses}

\section{Retirement and the Care of the Aged and Infirm}
\label{sec:health:retirement-and-the-care-of-the-aged-and-infirm}

A citizen who can no longer perform the labor assigned to the citizen --- whether by age, illness, or injury --- may be retired from labor under \artref{art:labor} and shall be cared for by the silo. The Infirmary and the Department of Supply together see to the housing, the food, the medicine, and the comfort of the retired, and the silo does not reckon their keep a debt.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen may petition for retirement after reaching the age at which the Head Physician and the Mayor jointly determine that further labor is no longer expected or possible. A citizen may also petition for early retirement if a chronic illness or permanent injury makes the citizen's current trade impossible; such a petition shall be referred to Judicial for a ruling.
\item A retired citizen continues to receive the ration of a citizen under \artref{art:supply}, plus additional provisions for medicine or care if the Infirmary judges it necessary. A retired citizen's housing is assigned under \artref{art:housing} and may not be revoked save by the Head of Supply in consultation with the Head Physician.
\item A retired citizen who recovers strength or fitness sufficient to undertake lighter labor may petition to return to work; and where the Head Physician and the Head of the Department allow it, the citizen enters the trade again and draws thereafter the ordinary ration alone.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Citizen in Danger of the Citizen's Own Hand}
\label{sec:health:citizen-in-danger-of-own-hand}

A Physician has the keeping of those the Physician tends. Where a Physician believes a citizen to stand in present danger of the citizen's own hand, the Physician may carry the citizen's name to Judicial, that the citizen be examined and kept safe.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Physician who believes a citizen to stand in present danger of the citizen's own hand, or of a swift undoing of mind or body, may carry the citizen's name to Judicial for examination and safekeeping, upon the Physician's own judgment. No Physician is required to carry a name for a danger that is lesser, or that is only a slow decline; and a Physician who does not carry such a name is not answerable for what follows.
\item A citizen who fears the citizen's own hand may come to Judicial without a Physician, and ask the Infirmary's care, and the asking is not a charge and is not entered against the citizen's trade; and such a citizen is heard before Judicial as to whether any keeping is needed, and of what kind, and for how long.
\item A Physician who observes in a citizen a persistent fixation upon some matter this Pact does not teach or require, carried to the neglect of the citizen's own health, household, or labor, may carry the citizen's name to Judicial under this section, upon the Physician's own judgment; no Physician is required to carry it. A name so carried is not itself a charge against the citizen, and creates no mark upon the citizen's record under \artref{art:records} save the fact of the referral and its resolution.
\end{clauses}

\section{Fitness for Trades of Trust and the Syndrome}
\label{sec:health:fitness-for-trades-of-trust-and-the-syndrome}

What a trade of trust is given, it must be fit to hold. The Infirmary and Judicial together keep watch that a citizen holding such a trade under \artref{art:labor} remains fit for it, and that where a citizen is not, the matter is seen to at once and the citizen put out of the trade if it must be.

\begin{clauses}
\item A Physician may at any time carry to Judicial the name of a citizen holding a trade of trust, where the Physician believes the citizen's health, of mind or of body, so far failed as to endanger the silo or to put the trade beyond the citizen's doing. The carrying is kept close until Judicial has found whether there is cause to put the citizen out, and a citizen found still fit is told that a name was carried and is not told by whom.
\item The Syndrome is a wasting of the nerves, beginning in a tremor of the hands and advancing to the failing of the mind and the body's governance, neither inherited nor passed from one citizen to another. No citizen afflicted with it may hold a trade of trust or any public office, as \secref{art:labor}{sec:labor:trades-of-trust} provides; a citizen diagnosed with it shall report the diagnosis to the Head Physician, resign any such role or office as that Section requires, and undertake the care the Infirmary recommends.
\item The Infirmary keeps close its record of which citizens are afflicted with the Syndrome, and opens it to Judicial only as to a citizen who holds a trade of trust or petitions for one. The finding does not mark a citizen unfit for ordinary labor, and no Head may treat it so; it bars the citizen from that labor alone which answers for the silo's keeping or for the lives of other citizens.
\end{clauses}

\section{Childbirth and the Nursery Floor}
\label{sec:health:childbirth-and-the-nursery-floor}

Every citizen the silo will ever have comes into it through the Infirmary, and the Infirmary keeps the coming as carefully as it keeps the going.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Infirmary keeps, upon its own floors in the Mids, a nursery floor for the bearing of children, with rooms for the bearing, rooms for the lying-in after it, and the keeping of the newly born; the midwives of the Infirmary, a trade of full shadowing under \artref{art:labor}, keep it under a Physician the Head Physician names.
\item A citizen granted a lottery's place under \artref{art:marriage} is seen by a midwife at each cycle from the granting of the place, and a citizen found to be carrying is entered in the nursery's roll, with the day the child is looked for; the household is told the day, and the citizen is set to such labor under \artref{art:labor} as the midwife judges the carrying allows, and to no heavier.
\item A citizen brought to the bearing is attended by a midwife and, where the midwife asks it, by a Physician, and by such of the citizen's household as the citizen wishes and the room allows; the bearing is entered with the day and the hour and the names of those attending, and the child's birth is attested to Judicial under \artref{art:citizenship} by the midwife who attended it.
\item A citizen who has borne a child rests from labor for one season from the bearing, drawing the ration and keeping the dwelling as before, and returns to the trade thereafter at such labor as the Head Physician judges; the married partner or sanctioned partner of the citizen rests from labor for one cycle from the bearing, and the household draws the child's ration from the day of naming.
\item A child born dead, or dead before naming, is entered in the nursery's roll and not in the rolls of citizenship, and is given to the soil under \artref{art:supply} as any citizen is, with the household present; the lottery's place the child would have filled is counted against the next drawing as a name closed, and the household's span is not extended on its account, nor its grant renewed but by the lottery.
\item The midwives keep the newly born on the nursery floor for such days after the bearing as the Physician judges, and no child leaves the nursery floor before naming; a household takes the child home at naming, and a household that cannot is given the nursery's keeping under \artref{art:schooling} until it can.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Floors and Stations of the Infirmary}
\label{sec:health:floors-and-stations-of-the-infirmary}

The Infirmary is one Department on many floors, that no citizen be farther from care than a citizen can be carried.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Infirmary keeps its own floors in the Mids, as \artref{art:founding} sets down: the wards of the sick, the rooms of the Physicians, the store of medicines, the nursery floor under \secrefhere{sec:health:childbirth-and-the-nursery-floor}, and the rooms of the dead under \secrefhere{sec:health:death-and-the-determination-of-cause}.
\item The Infirmary keeps, beyond its own floors, an aid station in each zone: one Up Top near the cafeteria and the station of the Sheriff, one in the Down Deep near the works and the workings, and one on such further floor of the Mids as the Head Physician names; each is kept by an Infirmary aide of short shadowing under \artref{art:labor} on every shift of the day, with such medicines and dressings as the Head Physician sets, and a Physician is sent to it upon the aide's word.
\item A citizen hurt or fallen sick is carried to the nearest aid station, or to the Infirmary's own floors where the hurt allows the climb, by the citizens nearest, who may not be refused the stair's passage for it under \artref{art:sheriff}; a citizen who cannot be carried is kept where the citizen lies and a Physician sent down.
\item The store of medicines is kept under lock by the Head Physician, and every medicine given is entered against the citizen's record with the Physician's name; no medicine is sold in the market under \artref{art:commerce} save by a Physician, as that Article provides, and no citizen keeps a medicine beyond the course the Physician set.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Keeping of the Retired}
\label{sec:health:keeping-of-the-retired}

A citizen retired under \secrefhere{sec:health:retirement-and-the-care-of-the-aged-and-infirm} has given the silo a life's labor, and the silo keeps the citizen in the same measure it keeps every other, and asks only what the citizen can give.

\begin{clauses}
\item A retired citizen keeps the dwelling under \artref{art:housing} the citizen held at retirement, or, where the citizen lived in a Department's own quarters, is lodged by Housing in the Mids within one cycle, near kin where kin will have the citizen; a retired citizen's dwelling is not reassigned for the silo's need save by the Head of Supply with the Head Physician, as \secrefhere{sec:health:retirement-and-the-care-of-the-aged-and-infirm} provides.
\item A retired citizen draws the ration as any citizen, and the sustenance in chits under \artref{art:commerce}, and the medicines the Infirmary judges needful; a retired citizen may take such light labor as the Head Physician allows and a Head of Department will give, for chits, and is not bound to it.
\item A Physician or an aide sees every retired citizen at each turn of the season, in the citizen's dwelling where the citizen cannot climb, and enters the citizen's condition; a retired citizen found unable to keep the dwelling or the ration alone is lodged by Housing with kin who will take the citizen, or in the wards of the Infirmary, and the citizen's household under \artref{art:marriage} is told.
\item A retired citizen keeps every standing of a citizen: the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, the petition under \artref{art:judicial}, the oath once sworn; and a retired citizen's word before Judicial or the Assembly is not weighed the less for the citizen's years.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Word to the Household}
\label{sec:health:word-to-the-household}

A death is entered in the archive on a Form; it is told to a household by a Physician, in the Physician's own words, and this Pact sets down only what those words must contain.

\begin{clauses}
\item Upon a citizen's death, a Physician goes to the household within the same day and tells them of it, and of the cause as the Form of Death under \secrefhere{sec:health:death-and-the-determination-of-cause} will enter it, and of the day and place the body will be given to the soil under \artref{art:supply}; the Physician tells the household no more of the manner of a death than the Form records, and no less.
\item The household may see the body in the rooms of the dead before it is given to the soil, on the day of the giving, for such time as the household asks and the Infirmary's floors allow; a household barred from the body by Judicial's order in a matter under \artref{art:judicial} is told that there is such an order, and no more.
\item Where the Form enters a death as by the citizen's own will outside the request under \artref{art:cleaning}, the Physician tells the household so, and that Judicial will make a finding under \artref{art:citizenship} before the name is closed, and the household may be heard at that finding; the Physician does not tell the household what the finding will be.
\item Where a citizen died by the cleaning under \artref{art:cleaning}, the Physician does not go, there being no Form; the household is told by the Officer or Sheriff who conducted the citizen, on the day of it, that the citizen has gone out, and the household may take the citizen's place at the wallscreen if the household wishes, and may not be compelled to.
\item The household of a citizen dead is not visited again by the Infirmary on the death's account, save at the household's asking, and the death is not spoken of by any officer to the household again save as this Pact requires for the rolls, the dwelling, and the ration.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Giving to the Soil, and the Mourning}
\label{sec:health:giving-to-the-soil-and-the-mourning}

What the silo fed, the silo takes back, as \artref{art:supply} provides. This section sets down the manner of the taking back, and what is owed to a household in the days around it; for a household left to itself in such days does not come whole again to its work, and the silo has need of it whole.

\begin{clauses}
\item The giving to the soil is made upon the third day after the death, that the household may be gathered and the trade released; sooner where the Head Physician enters a cause of health for the haste, and later only by the Head Physician's written entry, which names the day and the reason.
\item There attend the household; such kin as \artref{art:marriage} names, who are told the day by the Clerk of Judicial; the citizens of the dead citizen's trade, so many as the Head of the Department can release from the shift without stopping the works; and, where the household asks it, a citizen of a gathering of belief under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:gatherings-of-belief}. The Physician who found the cause attends, or sends another Physician, and says in the hearing of those gathered that the finding was made, and does not say what it was.
\item There is read once, from the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, by the Deputy or Peacekeeper of the floor: the citizen's name, the floor and household, the trade, and the count of the citizen's years. Then such words are said as the household chooses, by whom the household chooses, and the silo requires none and forbids none. Then are said the words of the giving, and no others are required of any citizen present: \emph{What the silo fed, the silo takes again, and gives again, and is not lessened.}
\item The household keeps of the dead citizen's goods one set of clothing, the citizen's own tools where not licensed under \artref{art:it} or \artref{art:mechanical}, the citizen's writings where licensed under \artref{art:publicorder}, and one further thing of the household's choosing, which is entered in the household's goods. The rest goes to reclamation under \artref{art:mechanical} and to Supply under \artref{art:supply}, and the citizen's occupant card is struck and delivered to the Clerk of Judicial with the Form of Death.
\item The household is excused its labor for three days from the day of the death, and a married partner, a parent, or a child of the dead citizen for one cycle. The days excused are not docked from the ration, are not paid for in chits, and are not counted against the citizen's labor under \artref{art:labor}; a Head of Department who docks them answers to Judicial. A household that asks to return sooner returns sooner, and is not asked why.
\item Housing does not take back the dwelling of a household that has lost a citizen until one season has passed, whatever the household's count has become, save where no citizen of the household remains in it.
\item The name is read once at the next ordinary sitting of the Assembly, among the names closed in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, and is not read at any sitting thereafter; and the Day of Remembrance under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:days-the-silo-keeps} reads no name at all, as that Section provides. A citizen who would have the name again reads it in the rolls, where it stands and is never struck.
\item Where a citizen has been sent to clean, there is no giving to the soil, the body not returning to the silo. The household is excused its labor as this section provides, and keeps the goods this section allows, and the name is read once at the next ordinary sitting exactly as any other name is read, without the cause set beside it; and an officer, a Head, or a teacher who sets the cause beside it answers to Judicial.
\end{clauses}
