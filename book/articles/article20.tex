\chapter{Of Assembly and the Common Halls}
\label{art:assembly}

\articlenote{In which is set down the Assembly of citizens, the common halls where the Assembly gathers, the speaking of citizens before the Assembly, and the will of the Assembly to amend this Pact or overturn the Mayor's or Judicial's acts where the Pact requires it.}

\section{The Assembly of Citizens}
\label{sec:assembly:assembly-of-citizens}

The Assembly is the body of all citizens of majority, gathered in one place or by messenger-relay across the floors under \artref{art:records}, to hear the Mayor's report and the concerns of the silo, to confirm or remove the Mayor from office, to confirm or remove the Judge from office, to ratify amendments to this Pact, and to rule on disputes between the Mayor and Judicial where this Pact requires their judgment.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor convenes the Assembly once yearly, at a time of the Mayor's choosing, to report on the silo's condition and to make recommendations to the citizens. Any citizen of majority may attend this ordinary Assembly and speak.
\item The Assembly may be convened in extraordinary session by petition of citizens of majority numbering one fifth of the full count of the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, or by the Mayor, or by Judicial where this Pact requires the Assembly's judgment on a matter between the Mayor and Judicial. An extraordinary session must be called within two cycles of the petition or circumstance requiring it.
\item No Assembly session shall last longer than three days without recess; a matter that requires deliberation longer than three days shall be put to the Assembly again, not in continuous session.
\item A citizen may send a message to the Assembly in their stead by porter under \artref{art:records}, if the citizen is unable to attend in person; such a message may be read aloud in the Assembly and is recorded in the archive.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Common Halls and the Gathering}
\label{sec:assembly:common-halls-and-the-gathering}

The Assembly gathers in the large common halls on the Up Top, which are kept by the Department of Supply under \artref{art:supply}. These halls are kept for the Assembly, and for such other gathering as the Mayor allows under \artref{art:mayor} or as \secrefhere{sec:assembly:gatherings-of-belief} permits.

\begin{clauses}
\item The main common hall of the Assembly, upon the uppermost level, holds several hundred citizens. The Mayor sees that there is seating for those who come and air enough for them, and where there is not, the sitting is not held in that hall. Where more citizens would attend than the hall will hold, the rest gather at the lesser halls, and the business of the sitting is carried to them by porter-relay or, where Information Technology under \artref{art:it} has given the means, by radio relay to the floors.
\item The Sheriff keeps order in the common halls where the Assembly gathers, and may remove a citizen who disrupts the Assembly, provided the citizen is given the reason for removal and may request a hearing before Judicial as to whether the removal was lawful.
\item A citizen making a spectacle or a threat in the Assembly halls is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for public disruption or endangerment of the Assembly itself.
\item The common halls that serve as cafeterias open before the first labor of the day and close after the last, save the Down Deep's own hall, kept to the hours of the shifts of Mechanical and Mines; a citizen whose labor keeps unusual hours may petition the relevant Head of Department for standing leave to draw food outside these hours, and such leave, once granted, need not be asked again.
\item Where more citizens would witness a cleaning at the wallscreen upon the uppermost level than the hall will hold, seating is granted by the same lottery \artref{art:marriage} provides for the bearing of children, drawn separately and for this purpose alone, that the granting of a scarce place be seen to rest on chance and not on favor.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Hearing Before the Assembly}
\label{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly}

Any citizen may petition to speak before the Assembly, and the Assembly shall hear petitions from any citizen who wishes to raise a matter of silo concern. The Mayor sets the time and order of speaking, but may not refuse a citizen the right to speak, save where the citizen has already disrupted an Assembly.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen wishing to speak must declare the subject of the address, and the Mayor may set limits on the time of speaking, that no single citizen hold the floor beyond the measure the Mayor sets alike for all, and that a matter of great length be divided among several speakers or several sittings.
\item A citizen's address to the Assembly is entered in the archive under \artref{art:judicial} and may not be censored or removed, save where the address itself is sedition or forbidden speech under \artref{art:publicorder}, in which case the Judge may review the archive copy and rule whether the address was rightfully censored.
\item A citizen who speaks to the Assembly is protected from retaliation under this Pact; any citizen punished by a Department, the Sheriff, or the Mayor for the fact of having spoken to the Assembly is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for breach of the Pact.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Assembly's Powers of Confirmation and Removal}
\label{sec:assembly:assemblys-powers-of-confirmation-and-removal}

The Assembly, together with the Mayor or Judicial as the case requires, confirms citizens to the highest offices and may remove them. The Mayor holds office for the term \artref{art:mayor} sets, and within that term holds it at the Assembly's pleasure; the Judge holds office for life, and at the pleasure of the Assembly and the Mayor together.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Assembly, at the ordinary sitting that falls in the fifth year of a Mayor's term, elects the Mayor for the term next following, as \artref{art:succession} provides, and confirms the citizen so elected.
\item The Assembly, in extraordinary session, votes on the removal of the Mayor by petition or by recommendation from Judicial; a removal vote passes upon the affirmative vote of two thirds of the Assembly present.
\item The Assembly, in extraordinary session and joined by the Mayor, votes on the confirmation of a new Judge or the removal of a sitting Judge; a confirmation requires a simple majority, and a removal requires two thirds.
\item At every ordinary sitting the Assembly hears the Mayor's account of the year past: the decisions of consequence, the standing orders given, and what stores and what share of the generator's power were parted out and to which Department; and any citizen may thereafter ask Judicial to rule whether a decision so reported broke this Pact.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Amendment of This Pact}
\label{sec:assembly:amendment-of-this-pact}

This Pact may be amended only by the Assembly, upon petition from the Mayor or from citizens of majority numbering one fifth of the full count of the rolls, and the amendment must pass by two thirds of the Assembly present. Amendments are entered in the archive and in a separate roll of amendments under \artref{art:amendment}.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor may recommend an amendment to this Pact, and shall put the text of the amendment proposed before the Assembly for its deliberation and its vote.
\item A citizen, or citizens together, may petition for an amendment, provided the petition is signed by citizens of majority numbering not fewer than one fifth of the full count of the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}; and the Assembly shall vote upon the amendment so proposed.
\item An amendment ratified by the Assembly is part of this Pact from the day of its ratifying, and binds the silo from that day forward exactly as though it had been written at the founding.
\item No amendment shall contradict an earlier amendment, or a provision of this Pact, without saying plainly which provision it repeals or replaces; every amendment is entered in the roll and in the archive, that any citizen and Judicial alike may see how this Pact has grown and what it has let go.
\item Every petition this Pact reckons by a part of the silo is reckoned against the full count of the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, and no other number, that count being the only count this Pact keeps; that a citizen not of majority cannot sign such a petition does not lessen the count against which the petition is reckoned.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Vote and Its Counting}
\label{sec:assembly:vote-and-its-counting}

A vote of the Assembly is counted, not judged; this section sets down how the count is made and what it includes.

\begin{clauses}
\item A vote is taken by the raising of hands in the hall, counted by tellers named by the Judge from among the Officers of Judicial, not fewer than three, who count separately and agree their count aloud before the Clerk of Judicial enters it.
\item Where citizens gather at secondary halls under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:common-halls-and-the-gathering}, or upon their own floors, each such gathering votes in the same manner before the Deputy or Peacekeeper of that floor, who enters the count, the day, and the hour, seals it, and sends it by porter under \artref{art:records} to the hall. A count arriving before the close of the sitting is added to the count of the hall; a count arriving after is entered in the archive but not added, and the fact of it is read at the next sitting.
\item The Assembly present, wherever this Pact reckons a vote by it, is the count of every citizen of majority who voted in the hall or by a floor count added under this section, for and against together; a citizen who attends and does not raise a hand is not counted present for that vote.
\item A vote on the removal of the Mayor or the Judge, the confirmation of the Judge, the election of the Mayor, or an amendment of this Pact is entered in the archive with its count for and against, and read aloud at the sitting's close; a vote on any other matter is entered with its result alone.
\item No citizen may be asked by any officer how the citizen voted, nor be answerable for it; a vote is not a speech under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly}, but the protection of that section extends to it.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Order of Business}
\label{sec:assembly:order-of-business}

A sitting of the Assembly keeps an order, that what the silo must hear is heard before what any citizen would have it hear.

\begin{clauses}
\item An ordinary sitting hears, in this order: the reading of the rulings under \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:rulings-where-this-pact-is-silent}, since the last sitting; the readings this Pact elsewhere requires at the ordinary sitting --- the namings under \artref{art:marriage}, the count of the rolls and of their reconciling under \artref{art:citizenship}, the relics classified under \artref{art:publicorder}, and the sentences of officers under \artref{art:crimes}; the Mayor's report under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:assemblys-powers-of-confirmation-and-removal}; the reckoning of the stores under \artref{art:supply}; the report that the green list has been kept under \artref{art:emergency}; the election of the Mayor where \secrefhere{sec:assembly:assemblys-powers-of-confirmation-and-removal} requires it; such amendments as stand proposed under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:amendment-of-this-pact}; and the petitions of citizens under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly}, in the order of their entry with the Clerk of Judicial.
\item An extraordinary sitting hears the matter for which it was called and no other, save that a citizen's petition touching that matter may be heard.
\item The Mayor may not adjourn a sitting before its order of business is complete, save for the recess \secrefhere{sec:assembly:assembly-of-citizens} requires or where the Sheriff finds the hall unsafe; a sitting so adjourned resumes on the next day the hall can be kept.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Days the Silo Keeps}
\label{sec:assembly:days-the-silo-keeps}

A silo that never gathers in gladness will gather in anger. These are the days the silo sets aside, and what is done upon them.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Day of the Founding is the first day of the first season of the year. Upon it the Preamble of this Pact is read aloud on every floor, by the Deputy or Peacekeeper of that floor under \artref{art:sheriff} or by a teacher under \artref{art:schooling}, in the hearing of such citizens as gather; and no labor is done upon that day save the labor \secrefhere{sec:assembly:days-the-silo-keeps} excepts.
\item The Day of Remembrance is the first day of the third season of the year. Upon it the silo remembers together those it has lost since the last such day. No name is read. The day is kept because a silo that counts only its living forgets what the living cost it; and what is remembered upon it is not any citizen in particular, but that the silo endured the losing of them, and went on. The reading of a name belongs to the giving to the soil under \artref{art:health} and to the sitting of the Assembly that follows it, and to no other day and no other hour, and a citizen who would hear a name again reads it in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, where it stands.
\item The rest day is one day in each cycle, as \artref{art:founding} provides. It is kept floor by floor and shift by shift, as each Head of Department appoints, that the works not stop; and a Head who does not appoint it is answerable to Judicial, the day being owed to the citizen and not given at the Head's pleasure.
\item The Mayor may set aside a further day for a cause the Mayor states --- the completing of the generator's full tending under \artref{art:mechanical}, the seating of a Mayor under \artref{art:mayor}, a Forgiveness Holiday under \artref{art:mayor}, or such other cause as the silo's peace requires --- and may set aside not more than two such days in a year without the Assembly's consent under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:assemblys-powers-of-confirmation-and-removal}.
\item Upon a day the silo keeps, the works of water, of air, and of power keep their shifts; the Infirmary keeps its stations; the Sheriff keeps the watch; and the crops and the sanctioned animals are tended as on any other day. A citizen so kept at labor is given the day back within the same cycle, and the giving back is entered in the Department's roll.
\item The Head of Supply sets aside through the year, out of the ordinary stores and not out of the reserve, such measure as the kept days require, and gives upon each a ration above the ordinary, alike to every citizen and alike on every floor. No Department may draw upon that measure before the day, and a Head who does so answers under \artref{art:supply} as for the diversion of stores.
\item Upon a day the silo keeps, no petition is received, no vote is counted, no hearing is held, and no sentence is carried out, a cleaning least of all; and the day is not counted against the span of a term in the mines, nor against any span this Pact sets for the doing of a thing.
\item A day the silo keeps is entered in the archive under \artref{art:records} by its day and its cause, and the roll of such days is read at the ordinary sitting of the Assembly, that the silo may see how many days it gave itself and why.
\end{clauses}

\section{Gatherings of Belief}
\label{sec:assembly:gatherings-of-belief}

This Pact does not say what a citizen may believe, and does not ask. It says where belief may gather, and upon what terms, that a gathering be a gathering and not a second Assembly.

\begin{clauses}
\item Citizens may gather for belief in the common room of a floor under \artref{art:housing}, or in a lesser hall of this Article when the Assembly has no use of it, upon an entry made with the Clerk of Judicial naming the floor, the room, the hour, how often the gathering meets, and one citizen of majority who answers for it. The Clerk enters the gathering and gives the citizen a copy, and does not refuse the entry save upon a ground this section names.
\item A gathering so entered teaches nothing of the founding, of the world above, or of the reason the silo was built that runs contrary to \artref{art:founding}. A citizen who so teaches is answerable under \artref{art:publicorder}, and the entry of that gathering is struck by the Clerk upon Judicial's finding, and may be entered again after one year by another citizen who answers for it.
\item A gathering is not a petition and is not a count. It does not speak to the Assembly as a gathering, does not put a name forward for any office, and does not stand for its citizens in any matter before Judicial, the Mayor, or a Head of Department. A citizen of a gathering petitions, speaks, and votes as a citizen, and in no other character.
\item The citizen who answers for a gathering delivers to the Clerk of Judicial, at each turn of the season, the count of those who came and not their names; and no officer of the silo may require the names, and no gathering may be made to keep them.
\item What is written, copied, or read aloud for a gathering is licensed under \artref{art:publicorder} as any other writing is, and no writing of a gathering is copied beyond the floor it was entered upon without that license.
\item No citizen is compelled to attend a gathering, and no citizen is compelled to stay away from one. A citizen's trade, ration, dwelling, schooling, standing before Judicial, and place in the lottery under \artref{art:marriage} do not turn in any part upon attending or not attending; a Head of Department, a master, or an officer who weighs it answers to Judicial for the weighing, and the citizen weighed against is restored to what was lost.
\item A citizen of a gathering may sit with the dying and with a bereaved household, in the dwelling or in the Infirmary, where the household asks it, and may say over the giving to the soil under \artref{art:health} such words as the household asks for. The Physician's record enters that the sitting was asked for, and does not enter what was said; and the Infirmary does not refuse the asking, nor send for it unasked.
\end{clauses}
