\chapter{Of Emergency and the Suspension of Ordinary Law}
\label{art:emergency}

\articlenote{In which is set down what constitutes an emergency that threatens the whole silo, and how the Mayor, Judicial, and the Heads of Departments may act beyond the ordinary Pact to preserve the silo's survival.}

\section{The Declaration of Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:declaration-of-emergency}

An emergency exists when the silo, or a great part of the citizens in it, stands in danger of not surviving: a ruin of the works of water or air, a failure of the generator, a breach of the silo's outer wall, an illness running loose through the floors, or such other peril as the Mayor and Judicial together find to be of that kind. In an emergency, ordinary procedures may be set aside, but only by the Mayor and Judicial acting together, and never for longer than one season without renewal of the emergency by Assembly vote.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor declares an emergency and tells Judicial and the Heads of Departments the same hour. Judicial may put the declaration in question, and having done so shall rule whether the peril is truly of the kind this section names; and that ruling stands against every other word.
\item An emergency suspends this Pact's ordinary procedures for a time set by the Mayor and Judicial jointly, not to exceed one season; if the emergency continues beyond one season, the Mayor must petition the Assembly for renewal of the emergency, and the Assembly must ratify the renewal by simple majority vote.
\item During an emergency the Mayor may set citizens to work beyond the hours their labor ordinarily asks, may take stores, tools, and hands from one Department and give them to another as the peril requires, and may shut the stair to the passage of citizens between floors, so far as the keeping of the light, the water, the air, and the food requires it and no further.
\item During an emergency, Judicial may hear and judge an offense with such swiftness as the emergency requires, and the hearing \artref{art:judicial} grants for an offense not among the gravest may be shortened but not refused. No measure of correction beyond those \artref{art:crimes} names may be given, and no offense not named among the gravest may be answered by cleaning, in emergency as in ordinary times, whatever order is given by any office.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Green List}
\label{sec:emergency:green-list}

An emergency leaves no time for deciding. The Mayor and the Heads of Departments therefore keep beforehand a list, called the green list, known to Judicial and to the Head of every Department, which sets down for each kind of peril what is to be done and in what order --- which floors are sealed, how the water and the air are rationed, in what order the generator's power is given and withheld, and what the silo can do without and for how long. The list is written again once in the year and kept in the Judicial archive.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen given an order out of the green list in an emergency obeys it as a standing order of the Mayor, and does not refuse it and does not wait upon it, unless Judicial rules, in the emergency or after it, that the order was not required by the peril at all.
\item The green list is no part of this Pact, and is not read by citizens and not posted, save that where a citizen has carried an order to Judicial, Judicial reads so much of the list as bears upon that order. The list is kept under seal in the archive under \artref{art:records}, and what stands in it, and what is changed in it, is the Mayor's and the Heads' determination alone.
\item The green list is written by the Mayor, Judicial and every Head of Department being heard, and is gone over not less than once in the year, before the ordinary sitting of the Assembly; that it was gone over, but nothing of what it says, is reported at that sitting, so that the Heads know what stands and every citizen knows that something stands.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Duration and the End of Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:duration-and-the-end-of-emergency}

An emergency, once declared, holds for the time set by the Mayor and Judicial. At the end of that time the emergency is lifted of itself, and this Pact runs again entire, and nothing further need be done to make it run. An emergency that is to continue must be renewed, and an emergency not renewed is over.

\begin{clauses}
\item At the end of the time set, every measure taken beyond this Pact ceases, and every citizen set to other work returns to the citizen's own. A citizen held, or set to the labor of the emergency, is returned to the citizen's trade, and is paid in chits under \artref{art:commerce} for the hours worked beyond what the trade asks, by the Mayor or by the Department that had the use of the citizen.
\item An emergency renewed beyond one season requires Assembly ratification; an emergency lasting longer than two seasons must be ratified anew by Assembly vote once per season thereafter.
\item An order given in an emergency that breaks this Pact further than the declaration allows is answerable under \artref{art:crimes}; and the citizen who obeyed it is not answerable for having obeyed, the answering falling wholly upon the officer whose order it was.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Holding of a Final Request in Abeyance}
\label{sec:emergency:holding-of-a-final-request-in-abeyance}

In the gravest emergency, where the silo's survival itself is at stake, the Mayor and Judicial may jointly hold in abeyance the requirement under \artref{art:cleaning} that a citizen requesting to go outside be sent to the cleaning without delay. A citizen whose request is so held stays within the silo and is set to such labor as the peril requires, and is told, in words, that the request stands and has not been refused.

\begin{clauses}
\item A citizen's final request under \artref{art:cleaning} may be held in abeyance, not suspended, during an emergency, by the Mayor and Judicial jointly, for the duration of the emergency only; a citizen whose request is held in abeyance may renew it at any time after the emergency ends.
\item A citizen whose request is held in abeyance may ask Judicial to rule that the peril no longer stands against the silo's surviving and that the request should be answered; and Judicial rules within one cycle of the asking, and enters the ruling.
\item No citizen's request shall be permanently denied or forgotten; if the emergency lasts longer than the citizen's own life, the citizen's death is recorded in the rolls without the cleaning, and the request is entered in the archive as unfulfilled but not denied.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Necessity and the Restraint}
\label{sec:emergency:necessity-and-the-restraint}

What this Article gives is given to keep the silo standing, and for nothing else: not to enlarge the Mayor's hand, nor Judicial's, nor a Head's, nor to settle in an emergency what could not be settled out of one. A citizen may come to Judicial after an emergency and ask whether an order given in it was needed for the surviving; and where Judicial finds it was not, Judicial orders what is owed to be made good.

\begin{clauses}
\item Within one season of an emergency's end, Judicial reads every order given under it against the peril declared, and finds of each whether it was needed. The findings are entered in the archive and read at the next ordinary sitting of the Assembly, the whole of them; and where an order is found to have gone beyond the peril, Judicial orders what is owed made good to the citizens it fell upon.
\item A citizen who dies or is hurt in the carrying out of an order Judicial has found to have been needed is entered in the archive by name, with the order beside the name; and the citizen's household may petition for what is owed as \artref{art:crimes} provides for restitution, and is not refused on the ground that the order was lawful.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Announcement of an Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:announcement-of-an-emergency}

An emergency binds no citizen who has not been told of it, and the telling is done by one road for every floor.

\begin{clauses}
\item Upon the declaration under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:declaration-of-emergency}, the Mayor sends the fact of it, the kind of emergency, and the measures first ordered by the stations of official word under \artref{art:it} to the Sheriff, the Judge, and the Head of every Department, and by the same stations to each Deputy's station under \artref{art:sheriff}; the Deputies send Peacekeepers to every landing of their zones, who cry the emergency at the landing and post the Mayor's notice there, in the Mayor's own words, within one hour of its reaching the station.
\item A citizen who hears the emergency cried goes to the citizen's own dwelling, or to the citizen's own floor of labor where the citizen's trade is one the emergency requires, and remains there until a further order reaches the landing; a citizen on the stair goes to the nearest landing and waits upon the Peacekeeper's word.
\item Every further order of the Mayor under this Article reaches a floor by the same road, and is posted at the landing with the hour, and no order binds a floor before it is posted there; a Peacekeeper who posts an order enters the hour, and the entries of every landing are gathered by the Deputy and delivered to the archive when the emergency is lifted.
\item Word that cannot wait for the stair passes by the stations as \artref{art:records} allows, to the Deputy or Peacekeeper of the floor and no further; word from a floor to the Mayor passes by porter, or by the Peacekeeper to the Deputy's station and thence by the station of official word.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Sealing of a Floor}
\label{sec:emergency:sealing-of-a-floor}

A floor is sealed only against a danger that would otherwise pass beyond it, and a sealed floor is not a floor abandoned.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor may order a floor, or a range of floors, sealed against the passage of citizens where the emergency is a disease under \artref{art:health}, a fouling of the air or the water, or a breach of the silo's structure; the order names the floors, the cause, the Peacekeepers who keep the seal at the landings above and below, and the day it is to be reviewed, which is not more than one cycle after; and no seal stands beyond one season save by the Assembly's renewal under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:duration-and-the-end-of-emergency}.
\item A sealed floor draws the ration from its own station under \artref{art:supply}, filled by Supply's runners to the landing and no further, and draws its water and air as before; where the emergency is a fouling of the water or the air, Mechanical carries the sealed floor's water to the landing and Supply its air in such vessels as it keeps for the purpose, and the Infirmary's aide is sent within the seal and remains there.
\item A citizen within a sealed floor may send word by the Peacekeeper at the landing, who carries it to the Deputy's station as \secrefhere{sec:emergency:announcement-of-an-emergency} provides, and the household of a citizen sealed on a floor not the citizen's own is told where the citizen is; a citizen may not pass the seal in either direction save a Physician, a Peacekeeper, or a citizen of Mechanical the Head sends, and a citizen who does is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for the violation of isolation under \artref{art:health}.
\item A citizen who dies within a sealed floor is examined by the Physician within the seal, and the Form of Death under \artref{art:health} is sent out by the Peacekeeper, and the body is kept within the seal in such manner as the Physician orders until the seal is lifted, and given to the soil under \artref{art:supply} then.
\item The Judge reviews every seal at the day the order names, upon the reports of the Physician, the Head of Mechanical, and the Deputy, and enters whether the cause stands; a seal whose cause the Judge finds to have passed is lifted the same day, whatever the Mayor's order said.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Ration in Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:ration-in-emergency}

The ration is lessened in emergency as it is lessened at any shortfall, alike for every citizen; this section sets down only the order in which the silo's other draws are lessened before it.

\begin{clauses}
\item Upon a shortfall of food, water, air, or power in an emergency, the Mayor orders the lessening in this order and no other, each draw lessened to what the emergency requires before the next is touched: the market under \artref{art:commerce} is closed; the recreation of \artref{art:health} and the lights of the common halls are stopped; the servers' share under \artref{art:it} is lessened; the farms' share under \artref{art:supply} is lessened, and the beds let go dry in the order the Head of Supply enters; the reserve under \artref{art:supply} is opened by the Mayor's written order; and last, the ration itself is lessened under \artref{art:supply}, alike for every citizen.
\item The works of \artref{art:mechanical}, the Infirmary, and the stations of official word are not lessened in any emergency save where the Head of Mechanical enters that the generator cannot bear them, and then by the Head's order and not the Mayor's.
\item The Head of Supply reports to the Mayor and the Judge at each cycle of an emergency what the stores hold and how many cycles they will hold at the emergency's draw, and the report is posted at every landing with the Mayor's orders under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:announcement-of-an-emergency}.
\item A citizen who draws, keeps, or sells in an emergency beyond the ration the emergency allows is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for the diversion of stores, and the grace of that Article's first offense is not given for it.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Labor of Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:labor-of-emergency}

The silo's work does not stop for an emergency; it changes hands.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor may assign any citizen of majority to any labor the emergency requires, beyond the citizen's trade and beyond the ordinary hours of \artref{art:labor}, by an order posted at the landing naming the floor, the labor, and the Head under whom it is done; a citizen so assigned works under that Head as under the citizen's own, and the Head enters the citizen in the Department's roll for the emergency's span.
\item The rest day under \artref{art:founding} is not owed in an emergency, and no citizen is owed a substitute for it; but no citizen labors more than two shifts in any day save the tenders of the works, and every citizen assigned is fed at the labor's floor from the station there.
\item A citizen assigned, in an emergency, to the labor of a trade of trust under \artref{art:labor}, or to the works, or to the workings, works only beside a citizen confirmed in that trade and does nothing alone; a citizen so assigned is not thereby of the trade, and is not vetted, and is bound by the entry \secref{art:it}{sec:it:trades-of-the-department-and-the-naming-of-the-head} provides where the labor was that Department's.
\item A citizen hurt or killed in the labor of emergency is entered in the roll of accidents of the Department under whose Head the labor was done, and the household petitions for restitution under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:necessity-and-the-restraint} and \artref{art:crimes}; a citizen who cannot return to the citizen's own trade after is reassigned or retired under \artref{art:labor} as for a hurt in the trade.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Lifting of an Emergency}
\label{sec:emergency:lifting-of-an-emergency}

An emergency ends as it began, by a declaration told to every floor, and what it cost is counted after.

\begin{clauses}
\item The Mayor lifts an emergency by a declaration sent and posted as \secrefhere{sec:emergency:announcement-of-an-emergency} provides, naming the day and the hour; every seal, every assignment of labor, every lessening under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:ration-in-emergency}, and every order given under this Article ceases at that hour, save such as the Judge continues under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:duration-and-the-end-of-emergency} for a stated cause and a stated span, entered.
\item Within one cycle of the lifting, every Department returns its citizens to their own trades and floors, Housing under \artref{art:housing} lodges anew any citizen whose dwelling the emergency took, and Supply resumes the ration in full and reports to the Mayor the day the reserve will be made good.
\item Within the same cycle, the Deputies deliver to the archive the entries of every landing under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:announcement-of-an-emergency}, the Physicians the Forms of every death within a seal, and every Head the roll of the emergency's labor and accidents; the Judge's review under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:necessity-and-the-restraint} begins upon the delivery, and is read at the next sitting of the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}.
\item The Mayor's report at that sitting states the emergency's cause, its span, the measures ordered, the stores spent, the citizens hurt and dead, and what the green list under \secrefhere{sec:emergency:green-list} is to be changed to on the emergency's account; the change is entered before the next ordinary sitting, as \secrefhere{sec:emergency:green-list} provides, and the Assembly is told that it has been.
\end{clauses}
