\chapter{Of Amendment and the Continuance of the Pact}
\label{art:amendment}

\articlenote{In which is set down how this Pact may be altered by the silo's citizens acting together, what no amendment may diminish, and what stands beyond the Assembly's reach altogether, that the silo's continuance not be undone by well-meant hands.}

\section{The Process of Amendment}
\label{sec:amendment:process-of-amendment}

This Pact may be amended by the Assembly, gathered in Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}, upon proposal from the Mayor or upon petition from citizens of majority numbering not fewer than one fifth of the full count of the rolls under \secref{art:citizenship}{sec:citizenship:count-of-the-rolls}, that count being the only count this Pact keeps, and the count read last before the petition is lodged being the count the petition is reckoned against. An amendment requires the affirmative vote of two thirds of the Assembly present.

\begin{clauses}
\item The text of a proposed amendment shall be made known to all citizens not fewer than two cycles before the Assembly vote, in the manner \secrefhere{sec:amendment:making-known-of-a-proposed-amendment} sets down, and citizens shall have the right to speak to the amendment in the Assembly or to send messages under \artref{art:records} raising objections or support.
\item An amendment, once ratified, is entered in the Amendment Log maintained under \artref{art:records} and becomes part of this Pact from the day of ratification forward. All earlier editions of this Pact remain in the archive for the record.
\item No amendment may be repealed save by a later amendment, ratified in the same manner as the first.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Limits on Amendment}
\label{sec:amendment:limits-on-amendment}

Certain truths are so fundamental to the silo's nature that no amendment may alter them, lest the silo cease to be a place where citizens may live.

\begin{clauses}
\item No amendment may eliminate a citizen's right to petition for a final request to go outside, nor may an amendment make that request answerable as an offense under this Pact.
\item No amendment may eliminate the lottery for children under \artref{art:marriage}, nor may an amendment increase a citizen's burden of labor beyond what an ordinary citizen can bear.
\item No amendment may remove Judicial's right to rule where this Pact is silent, nor may an amendment subordinate Judicial to the Mayor's authority without the Assembly's explicit consent.
\item No amendment may eliminate a citizen's right to know the content of this Pact, to view records concerning the citizen under \artref{art:records}, or to speak before the Assembly under \artref{art:assembly}.
\item No amendment may codify inequality before the law; every citizen shall remain equal under the Pact, save where the Pact itself explicitly provides differences by office, trade, or age.
\item No amendment may alter the founding as \artref{art:founding} records it, nor compel any Department to disclose the manner, location, or working of any matter this Pact elsewhere commits to that Department's own procedure and does not itself set down.
\item An amendment touching any limit named in this section takes no effect upon the Assembly's vote alone. It further requires the written concurrence of the Judge and the Head of Information Technology, given only upon their joint finding that the amendment works none of the harms this section forbids. An amendment lacking such concurrence is void, whatever majority approved it, and is struck from the Log as though never ratified.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Amendment Log}
\label{sec:amendment:amendment-log}

Amendments to this Pact, ratified by the Assembly, are recorded in a separate roll under \artref{art:records} and are printed at the end of every copy of this Pact, as its Appendix, so that any citizen may see how the law has grown and changed.

\begin{clauses}
\item Each amendment is entered in its order of ratification, with the text of the amendment in full and a brief notation of what Article or Section it amends or adds.
\item The Amendment Log is maintained in the Judicial archive, and a backup copy is kept by the Department of Supply under \artref{art:supply}.
\item Any citizen may petition Judicial to examine the Amendment Log and to ask for an explanation of any amendment's import or consequence.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Form of a Petition for Amendment}
\label{sec:amendment:form-of-a-petition-for-amendment}

A petition that would alter this Pact is a long paper carried a long way, and the silo has learned to be exact about such papers.

\begin{clauses}
\item A petition for amendment is written in one hand at its head, and carries, before any name is set to it: the words of the amendment proposed, entire, as they are to stand in this Pact; the Article and Section the amendment would alter, add, or repeal, named as this Pact names them; and the day the petition was opened. A petition that does not carry all three is not received, and the Clerk of Judicial enters the refusal and what was wanting.
\item A petition may be begun by any citizen of majority, and need not be begun by the citizen who wrote it. The citizen who opens the petition is entered as its bearer, and the bearer answers to Judicial for the keeping of the paper, for the truth of what is written at its head, and for nothing else.
\item A citizen signs a petition by writing the citizen's own name, floor, and household, or, where the citizen cannot write, by setting a mark to it before one witness of majority who is not the bearer and who writes the name beside the mark and the witness's own beneath it. One name is one signature, whatever the household, and no citizen signs for another, not a spouse for a spouse nor a parent for a child of majority.
\item The words at the head of a petition are not altered after the first name is set to it. Where the bearer would alter them, the petition is closed, the alteration is entered, and a new petition is opened, and the names upon the closed petition do not carry over to it.
\item A citizen may strike that citizen's own name from a petition at any hour before it is lodged, by saying so to the bearer or to the Clerk, and the name is struck in the Clerk's hand with the day beside it and is not counted. No citizen may strike another's name, and a bearer who strikes a name not that bearer's own, or who refuses to carry a citizen's striking to the Clerk, answers under \artref{art:crimes}.
\item A petition lies open for not more than one year from the day it was opened. At the year's end it is lodged with the Clerk as it stands or it lapses, and a lapsed petition is entered as lapsed with the number of names it bore.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Counting of the Names}
\label{sec:amendment:counting-of-the-names}

\begin{clauses}
\item A petition lodged with the Clerk of Judicial is counted by the Clerk within one cycle, in the presence of the bearer if the bearer wishes to be present, and the count is entered with the day: the names set to it, the names struck, the names found twice, the names not found in the rolls, and the number remaining.
\item A name is not counted where the citizen is not of majority, where the name is not open in the rolls under \artref{art:citizenship}, where the name appears twice upon the same petition, or where the name is set to it in a hand the Clerk finds not to be the citizen's and not witnessed as this Article requires. The Clerk enters against each such name why it was not counted, and the entry names the ground and nothing further.
\item Where the Clerk does not count a citizen's name, that citizen may ask Judicial to rule upon it, and the Judge rules within one cycle. Where the Judge finds the name should have been counted, it is counted from the day of the petition's lodging and not from the day of the ruling.
\item A petition reaching the number \secrefhere{sec:amendment:process-of-amendment} requires is carried by the Clerk to the Mayor and to the Assembly, and the Assembly takes it up at its next ordinary sitting or at an extraordinary sitting called for it under \artref{art:assembly}. The Mayor does not decline to carry a petition so counted, and may say against it whatever the Mayor wishes to say, in the hearing of the Assembly.
\item A petition falling short of the number is entered as fallen short, with its count, and the Clerk says so to the bearer in writing. The paper itself is kept in the archive for ten years, and any citizen may read it there.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Making Known of a Proposed Amendment}
\label{sec:amendment:making-known-of-a-proposed-amendment}

A silo of a hundred and forty-four floors is not told a thing by telling it once at the top.

\begin{clauses}
\item From the day an amendment is put before the Assembly, whether by the Mayor or by petition, its words are posted in full in the common hall of every zone and at the landing of every tenth floor, and are carried by the porters under \artref{art:records} to every floor that has no common hall; and the posting is kept legible, and a torn or taken posting is replaced within one day.
\item The words are read aloud, entire, at the ordinary sitting of the Assembly before the vote is taken, and once upon each floor where ten citizens of majority ask the Clerk for a reading, that no citizen be bound by words that citizen has not been able to read.
\item The Clerk enters the day the posting was made in each zone, and the Assembly does not vote upon an amendment before two cycles have passed from the latest of those days. A vote taken sooner is void, whatever its majority, and the amendment is not entered in the Log.
\item A citizen may send to Judicial, in writing or by a porter's word taken down, an objection to the amendment or a support of it, and every such message is kept with the amendment's papers and the number of them, for and against, is read to the Assembly before the vote. The number is read; the names are not.
\item Where an amendment is altered in the Assembly's debate, the altered words are posted afresh and the two cycles run again from that posting. An alteration that only corrects a misspelling, a wrong number of an Article or Section, or a plain slip of the hand is entered as such by the Clerk and does not begin the time again.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Printing of a Ratified Amendment}
\label{sec:amendment:printing-of-a-ratified-amendment}

An amendment that is not in the copies is an amendment the silo does not know it has.

\begin{clauses}
\item Within one season of an amendment's ratifying, the Clerk of Judicial enters it in the Amendment Log and carries its words to every copy of this Pact kept under \secref{art:records}{sec:records:copying-and-keeping-of-this-pact}: by the printing of a fresh Appendix where a copy is printed afresh, and in the meantime by a sheet set in at the Appendix, in the Clerk's hand, bearing the amendment's number, its words, the Article and Section it alters, and the day of its ratifying.
\item The master copy of this Pact is the copy held in the Judicial archive and sealed under \secref{art:records}{sec:records:seal}. Where any two copies differ, the master copy governs, and a copy found to differ is corrected against it and the correction entered. No citizen is answerable for having obeyed a copy that was wrong, and the Clerk answers for its being wrong.
\item The schoolroom copies under \artref{art:schooling} are corrected first among all the copies, and the teacher of each school enters that it was done and reports it to Judicial with the school's roll under \secref{art:schooling}{sec:schooling:records-of-schooling-and-attendance}; and no child is taught from a copy from which a ratified amendment is wanting.
\item A copy of this Pact in a citizen's own keeping is corrected by the citizen upon the Clerk's notice, and the Clerk gives the sheet to any citizen who asks for it without charge of chits. A citizen is not answerable for an uncorrected copy in that citizen's own keeping, the amendment binding from its ratifying whatever the copies say.
\item Where the Appendix of a copy is reprinted, nothing that stood in it before is left out. A repealed amendment is printed with the amendment that repealed it noted beside it, and is not struck from the Appendix; for the silo is to see what it has let go as plainly as what it holds.
\end{clauses}

\section{An Amendment That Fails, and One Brought Again}
\label{sec:amendment:amendment-that-fails-and-an-amendment-brought-again}

\begin{clauses}
\item An amendment that does not pass is entered in the archive, with its words, the count of the vote, and the day; it is not entered in the Amendment Log, which holds only what was ratified.
\item An amendment that has failed is not brought before the Assembly again, by proposal or by petition, within one year of the vote that failed it. The Clerk judges whether an amendment brought again is the same amendment, by its words and by what it would do, and not by the name it is given; and the Clerk's judgment in this may be put to the Judge by the bearer or by the Mayor.
\item An amendment brought again after the year is made known afresh under \secrefhere{sec:amendment:making-known-of-a-proposed-amendment}, and the names upon the earlier petition are not counted toward the later one.
\item An amendment touching a limit named in \secrefhere{sec:amendment:limits-on-amendment} that fails for want of the concurrence that Section requires, the Assembly's vote having carried it, is entered as void with the ground of the voiding; and it is not brought again within one year, and the concurrence withheld is entered with the day it was withheld and by whom.
\end{clauses}

\section{Words That Contradict What Is Already Written}
\label{sec:amendment:words-that-contradict-what-is-already-written}

\begin{clauses}
\item An amendment says plainly which words of this Pact, or of an earlier amendment, it repeals or replaces, and an amendment that says so plainly works that repeal and no more. An amendment that does not say so is not void for the omission, but is read, so far as it can be read, to stand beside what is already written and not against it.
\item Where an amendment cannot be so read, and its words and an earlier provision cannot both be obeyed, the later ratifying governs the earlier so far as the two cannot stand together, and no further. The Judge enters which words are displaced, in what measure, and from what day, and the entry is printed in the Appendix beside the amendment.
\item No amendment repeals a limit named in \secrefhere{sec:amendment:limits-on-amendment} by implication, by contradiction, or by any means save by saying that limit's words and striking them with the concurrence that Section requires. Where an amendment would work such a repeal without saying it, the amendment is void in that measure and stands in the rest.
\item Where two amendments ratified at the same sitting cannot stand together, the one carried by the greater number governs; and where the numbers are equal, neither takes effect, and the Clerk enters that neither did and why.
\item Judicial rules upon any question of what an amendment has displaced, upon the asking of any citizen, and such a ruling is entered in the archive and read at the next ordinary sitting of the Assembly.
\end{clauses}

\section{The Continuance and the Renewal of the Pact}
\label{sec:amendment:continuance-and-the-renewal-of-the-pact}

Here the Pact comes back to where it began, and says once more, in the language of law, what the Preamble said in the language of plain speech.

\begin{clauses}
\item This Pact is made for the continuance of the silo and for the keeping of every citizen who dwells in it. It endures while the silo stands, and binds every citizen born in the silo or entering it, from the citizen's naming under \artref{art:citizenship} to the closing of the citizen's name.
\item Every citizen of majority swears to know this Pact, to keep it, and to call others to the keeping of it, under \secref{art:citizenship}{sec:citizenship:citizens-oath}; and the Mayor, the Judge, the Sheriff, and every Head of Department swear at the assumption of office to defend this Pact against any hand that would breach it, and to enforce it alike upon the floor they came from and the floor they did not, in the words and in the manner \secref{art:mayor}{sec:mayor:seating-of-a-mayor-and-the-oaths-of-office} sets down. No officer of the silo, however seated, stands above this Pact, and none is excused from it by the office held.
\item A citizen who keeps this Pact in full is sound, in the same word and the same sense a wall or a seal is sound; and the silo's endurance is nothing more, and nothing less, than the sum of so many sound citizens as it can keep. This is not a figure of speech. It is the measure by which every Article here is to be read, and the reason this Pact is strict where a gentler law would do, in a hull that cannot be pierced in one place without the whole of it taking on water.
\item Where any part of this Pact is silent, the silence is not permission. It is a gap, and the gap is carried to Judicial under \artref{art:judicial} and ruled upon there, and is not filled by any one citizen's will, nor by any one officer's, nor by the custom of a floor, however long that floor has kept it.
\item This Pact is amended by the means this Article sets down and by no other. It is not suspended, not in an emergency under \artref{art:emergency}, not by the Mayor, not by Judicial, not by the Assembly, and not by the agreement of all three; and an officer who treats it as suspended is answerable under \artref{art:crimes} as for the gravest of offenses, whatever the cause pleaded.
\item Should the silo cease to stand, or should no citizen dwell in it, this Pact ends with it, having nothing left to keep. While the silo stands and citizens dwell in it, this Pact governs, and governs the hands that wrote it no less than the hands that read it now.
\item Let it be read, then, not because a citizen was told to read it, but because a citizen has read here exactly why.
\end{clauses}
