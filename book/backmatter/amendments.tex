\section*{Amendments Ratified Since the Founding}

The following amendments have been ratified by the Assembly of the silo since this Pact was first adopted. Each is entered below in the order of its ratification, with the text of the amendment and a note of its effect. The Pact does not itself reckon a calendar from the founding, and this Log follows the Pact's own practice: an amendment's place here is fixed by its order, not by a year that binds it to a count no citizen is asked to keep.

\subsection*{Amendment 1: The Housing Proviso}

\textit{Ratified in the silo's early years, not long after the offices of this Pact had settled into their ordinary practice.}

``The Assembly, being satisfied that the power to alter this Pact is the power \artref{art:amendment} gives it and no power beyond it, and having heard the Judge say that the power may be used, amends \secref{art:housing}{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} by the addition of one clause and of nothing else, in these words: `A citizen whose dwelling is revoked under this Section may petition Judicial to reduce or stay the revocation, provided the citizen has made a good-faith effort to remedy the breach that occasioned it and sufficient time has passed for remedy to have been possible.' And it is set down here, that no doubt arise afterward: that this amendment does not touch the grounds of revocation this Section sets down, nor the charge of Supply under \artref{art:supply}, nor any other Section of this Pact; that the clause added stands last among the clauses of \secrefhere{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} and in no other place; and that the remainder of \secrefhere{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} is unchanged in every word of it.''

\textit{Effect}: Judicial may allow a citizen to mend a breach before the dwelling is revoked. The entry is the longest in this Log and makes the shortest change in it: the Assembly, using for the first time a power the Pact had given it at the founding, recited the power, named what it was not doing, and said twice that nothing else had changed.

\subsection*{Amendment 2: The Testimony Entered into the Lottery}

\textit{Ratified in the generation after Amendment 1, upon a petition of citizens rather than the Mayor's proposal, the first amendment so brought.}

``\secref{art:marriage}{sec:marriage:keeping-of-the-silos-number} is amended to add, before its clauses, the following: `It is recorded, though not by the name of the household, that a citizen who waited three years for a place in the lottery, and buried a stillborn child in the fourth, came before the Assembly and said that this balance, hard as it had been to bear, was the same balance that had kept her own mother fed as a child, and asked that no citizen be spared the waiting who had not first been spared the alternative. This Pact does not require any citizen to feel as she felt. It asks only that every citizen know such a citizen has lived, and judged as she judged.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:marriage:keeping-of-the-silos-number} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: No requirement of the Section is altered; a citizen's testimony is set within it, that the lottery's necessity be heard in a citizen's voice as well as the Pact's. The Assembly's record notes that the petitioners first asked the Judge whether a citizen's words might be entered into the Pact at all, and that the Judge ruled they might, provided the household went unnamed, as no other citizen is named anywhere in the Pact.

\subsection*{Amendment 3: The Rumor Proviso}

\textit{Ratified in the same generation as Amendment 2, upon the Mayor's proposal, after a season the Mayor's own report to the Assembly called a season of loose talk.}

``\secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:rumor-despair-and-the-spread-of-discontent} is amended to add: `Forasmuch as a rumor is a leak in the hull no less than a crack in the wall, and a hull is kept whole by the many hands that stop it and not by the one that made it, every citizen who hears a rumor of the kind this Section forbids shall report it to the Sheriff within one hour of the hearing, and the Sheriff shall enter the report, the hour, and the name of the citizen reporting, and no report so entered shall be held against the citizen who made it, whatever the rumor proved.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:publicorder:rumor-despair-and-the-spread-of-discontent} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A duty to report rumor is added to the duty \secrefhere{sec:publicorder:forbidden-word} already lays upon a citizen who hears the forbidden word, and an hour is set for it. The Judge afterward ruled, under \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:rulings-where-this-pact-is-silent}, that the hour is reckoned from the citizen's reaching a landing where a Deputy is posted, and not from the hearing itself, no citizen upon the stair being able to keep it otherwise; the ruling stands entered beside the amendment.

\subsection*{Amendment 4: The Retirement Entitlement}

\textit{Ratified some years after Amendment 2.}

``\secref{art:health}{sec:health:retirement-and-the-care-of-the-aged-and-infirm} is amended to add: `A retired citizen draws, beyond the ordinary ration, a stipend for medicine, which the Chit-Keepers under \artref{art:commerce} reckon at every turn of the season thus: the whole cost of the common treatments the Infirmary dispensed in the four seasons last past, divided by the count of retired citizens standing in the rolls at the last count under \secref{art:citizenship}{sec:citizenship:count-of-the-rolls}; and where the cost of a citizen's treatment exceeds the stipend, the excess is entered against the Infirmary's account as a debt of the silo's keeping and not against the citizen's own. The Infirmary gives a retired citizen the same care, in the same order, as a citizen still in labor, and keeps no separate list.' The remainder of \artref{art:health} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: Retirement carries a medicine stipend and the Infirmary's ordinary care, and a treatment costing more than the stipend is not charged to the citizen. What the stipend is in any season is an answer to a sum: it rises in a season of much sickness and falls in a season of little, and falls again when many citizens retire, the price of nothing having changed. The Chit-Keepers' note explaining this is entered in the archive beside the amendment, and is four lines long.

\subsection*{Amendment 5: The Appeal of Judicial Sentence}

\textit{Ratified some years further on.}

``\secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:judgment-and-sentence} is amended to add: `It being the sense of the Assembly that a term served well is a term that has done its work, and that the silo is not served by keeping below a citizen the silo could use above, a citizen sentenced to the mines under \artref{art:crimes} may petition Judicial for the review of the sentence when the citizen has served half the term first set; and the Judge, upon such a petition, may shorten what remains of the term where the citizen has kept good conduct and given honest labor throughout, or where new matter has come to light bearing upon the citizen's guilt. No review under this amendment shall shorten a term by more than half of what was first imposed, that the sentence keep its weight.' The remainder is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A citizen in the mines may ask, at the half term, to have the rest of it shortened. The Judge's statement under \artref{art:records} found no conflict with \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:reckoning-of-a-term-in-the-mines}, which suffers a term to be shortened only upon the Judge's own reading of the Head of Mines' reports or upon a petition the Head of Mines joins: the joining, the statement reasons, is required of a petition brought under that Article and not of one brought under this amendment, which makes its own way to the Judge. \artref{art:crimes} stands accordingly among the unamended provisions of this Log. The Judge further ruled, no term in the mines being set in days, that half a term is reckoned to the nearer whole season, and, where the seasons are odd in number, to the greater of the two.

\subsection*{Amendment 6: The Right to Counsel}

\textit{Ratified in the years following Amendment 5.}

``\secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:judgment-and-sentence} is amended to add: `A citizen accused of an offense not among the gravest under \artref{art:crimes} may ask that another citizen speak for the accused at the hearing. Judicial grants the asking where the citizen named is not under charge and is not barred from the Assembly by a judgment. The citizen so named speaks once, after the accused and before the finding. Judicial sets the order and the length.' The remainder is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: An accused citizen may have another citizen speak at the hearing. The amendment is four sentences, and three of them say what Judicial keeps.

\subsection*{Amendment 7: The Bounding of the Forgiveness Holiday}

\textit{Ratified some years after Amendment 6, upon the Mayor's own proposal, in a season the Mayor's report called a season of many surrenders.}

``\secref{art:mayor}{sec:mayor:forgiveness-holiday} is amended to add: `No Forgiveness Holiday shall be declared within five years of the ending of the last, nor shall any run longer than one turn of the season; and the Mayor shall report to the Assembly, at the sitting next following, the number of things surrendered and the number classified among the gravest, without the names of the citizens who surrendered them.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:mayor:forgiveness-holiday} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: The Holiday, which the Pact left wholly to the Mayor's judgment as to interval and cause, is bounded in both, and a count of what it yields is made known to the Assembly.

\subsection*{Amendment 8: The Syndromic Retirement}

\textit{Ratified well after Amendment 6, in a season this Log does not further mark.}

``\secref{art:labor}{sec:labor:reassignment-and-retirement} is amended to add: `Forasmuch as no citizen is well served by being kept in a labor the citizen can no longer bear, and the Infirmary is not the keeper of a citizen whom a Department will not release, and a bed in the Infirmary is a bed the silo has only so many of, a citizen in whom the Syndrome is found may petition for retirement by the same process as any other citizen leaving labor, and shall not wait upon the age at which retirement is ordinarily given. The Head Physician and the Mayor shall approve such a petition within one cycle of its filing, and a Head of Department shall not withhold the citizen from it.' The remainder is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A citizen found to have the Syndrome may retire at any age, and the Department the citizen labors in cannot hold the citizen back. The amendment was brought at the Head Physician's asking, and the recital of principle at its head names the Infirmary's beds twice before it names the citizen once.

\subsection*{Amendment 9: The Restoration of the Holiday}

\textit{Ratified after Amendment 8, upon a petition of citizens, the second amendment so brought. The text as ratified does not survive in the archive entire: the leaf of the roll bearing it is wanting, and what stands here is the Judge's reconstruction from the archive's marginal entry and the recollection of the tellers, entered as authoritative by a ruling under \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:rulings-where-this-pact-is-silent}, at the sitting next following.}

``\secref{art:mayor}{sec:mayor:forgiveness-holiday}, as amended by Amendment 7, is restored to its form before that amendment, Amendment 7 being repealed entire [\ldots] save that the count of things surrendered [\ldots] be kept [\ldots] as the Mayor judges [\ldots].''

\textit{Effect}: The bound upon the Holiday is lifted, and the Mayor's judgment as to its interval and cause stands as the Pact first gave it. Whether the count of things surrendered is still required, the reconstruction does not settle; the Mayor's reports since have given it in some years and not in others.

\subsection*{Amendment 10: The Floor Reassignment Procedure}

\textit{Ratified some time after Amendment 8.}

``The Assembly, being satisfied that the power to alter this Pact is the power \artref{art:amendment} gives it and no power beyond it, amends \secref{art:housing}{sec:housing:zones-and-the-division-of-the-silo} by the addition of one clause and of nothing else, in these words: `A citizen may petition the Head of Supply for reassignment to the housing of another floor, and the Head of Supply shall weigh the petition with regard to the citizen's trade, the silo's need, and the room standing empty upon the floor asked for. A denial may be carried to Judicial, and Judicial shall confirm the denial where the Head of Supply's reason is plainly stated and grounded in the silo's need.' And it is set down here, that no doubt arise afterward: that this amendment does not touch the division of the silo into its zones, nor the charge of Supply under \artref{art:supply}, nor any other Section of this Pact; and that the remainder of \secrefhere{sec:housing:zones-and-the-division-of-the-silo} is unchanged in every word of it.''

\textit{Effect}: A citizen may ask to be moved to another floor and may carry a refusal to Judicial, which confirms it where a reason is given. The entry copies the form of Amendment 1 closely, down to the recital of the amending power and the saying twice that nothing else has changed; what it does not copy is Amendment 1's substance, which was to give Judicial something to decide. The Sheriff's report of the first year under this amendment gives the number of petitions carried to Judicial as nine, and the number of denials Judicial did not confirm as none.

\subsection*{Amendment 11: The Assembly Quorum}

\textit{Ratified later still.}

``\secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:assembly-of-citizens} is amended: `An extraordinary sitting of the Assembly called to take up a matter of urgent need may proceed with not fewer than one third of the full count of the rolls under \secref{art:citizenship}{sec:citizenship:count-of-the-rolls} present in the hall or counted from the floors under \secrefhere{sec:assembly:vote-and-its-counting}, provided the sitting has been called as this Article requires and every citizen has been given not fewer than one cycle's notice of it; a sitting with fewer present may hear, but may not vote.' The remainder is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: The Pact had set no least number for an extraordinary sitting; this amendment sets one, that a vote taken with few present not be disputed afterward. The number is reckoned against the full count of the rolls, as every part of the silo is reckoned under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:amendment-of-this-pact}, and so counts toward itself every child who cannot come. The Clerk's entries since record four extraordinary sittings that heard a matter and did not vote upon it for want of the third, and the notice of one cycle has twice been said in the Assembly to be longer than an urgent matter will wait.

\subsection*{The Judge's Entry Concerning the Numbering}

\textit{Entered by the Judge under \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:rulings-where-this-pact-is-silent}, upon the Clerk's report to Judicial, at a sitting this Log does not mark.}

``It is found that two leaves are wanting from the roll of amendments between the entry standing here as the eleventh and the entry standing here as the twelfth. The leaves were not torn: the cut is clean, is made with a blade, and runs the height of the roll, and the stitching was opened and closed again by a hand that knew how to do it. What the leaves bore cannot now be known. The archive's marginal entry for those leaves is wanting also, and the tellers' counts for the sittings of that span are wanting, and no citizen living has been found who can say what was ratified there, the Clerk having asked of every office and entered the asking.

It is found further that the entries following the wanting leaves were renumbered, at some time this Judge cannot fix, to close the gap in the sequence, so that the numbers now borne by the entries from the twelfth forward are not the numbers those amendments were ratified under. An amendment cited by its number in any record older than this entry is therefore not certainly the amendment that now bears that number, and a citizen or an officer reading such a citation is to read the amendment's words and not its number.

No amendment is here declared void, and none is restored, this Judge having nothing to restore it from. The Log's own preface, which says that an amendment's place here is fixed by its order and not by a year, is left standing, with this entry set beside it; for the order is what the Log has, and the order is now, in part, a reconstruction. Let it be read as one.''

\textit{Effect}: None upon the Pact. The Log keeps the numbering it was found with, and keeps this entry with it. The Clerk's practice since has been to cite an amendment by its words and its subject, and to give its number second.

\subsection*{Amendment 12: The Reporting of Anomalous Signal and Device}

\textit{Ratified late among those entered in this Log, at the recommendation of the Department of Information Technology, following a matter the Department represented to the Assembly as touching the whole silo's safety. The Assembly's own record of the deliberation preceding the vote does not survive in the Judicial archive in complete form; what is reproduced below is reconstructed from the surviving fragments and the Judge's own summary, entered afterward at Judicial's own insistence that no amendment stand in this Log without some account of its passage.}

``\secref{art:publicorder}{sec:publicorder:apparatus-of-the-silos-keeping} is amended to add: `Where Information Technology represents to Judicial that a device, signal, or apparatus discovered by a citizen may bear upon matters this Pact does not commit to public record, the citizen who discovers it shall report the discovery to Information Technology directly, and shall not first report it to the Sheriff or to Judicial, notwithstanding any other provision of this Article; and the citizen shall thereafter answer such questions as Information Technology puts to the citizen concerning the manner, place, and circumstance of the discovery. This is not to be understood as a diminishment of Judicial's charge to investigate offenses under this Pact, but as a necessary sequencing of that investigation, that the silo's safety not be made to wait upon a report reaching Information Technology only after having passed through other hands. No citizen who complies with this Section in good faith shall be found to have violated the ordinary reporting requirement this Section otherwise imposes, whatever the outcome of Information Technology's own review proves to be.' The remainder of \artref{art:publicorder} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A discovery a citizen might once have carried first to the Sheriff or to Judicial is now carried first to Information Technology. The report of the incident that prompted this amendment does not survive in the archive. The concurrence \artref{art:amendment} requires of the Judge and the Head of Information Technology together, for any amendment touching what this Pact keeps from ordinary record, was given by both offices and is entered in the archive without further note.

\subsection*{Amendment 13: The Narrowing of the Right to Counsel}

\textit{Ratified after Amendment 12, in a season the Assembly's own call to vote described only as necessary to the Pact's continued good order, without further particular given.}

``\secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:judgment-and-sentence}, as amended by Amendment 6, is further amended: `The right granted by Amendment 6 to bring a speaking-citizen before Judicial does not extend to an offense under \artref{art:publicorder}, Sections~\secnum{sec:publicorder:forbidden-word}, \secnum{sec:publicorder:false-teaching-of-the-founding-and-the-past}, \secnum{sec:publicorder:restricted-instruments-and-devices}, or \secnum{sec:publicorder:apparatus-of-the-silos-keeping}, it being the judgment of the Assembly that a hearing upon such an offense ought not be made an occasion for the very speech, teaching, relic, or discovery those Sections forbid.' The remainder of \secref{art:judicial}{sec:judicial:judgment-and-sentence} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A citizen accused of the forbidden word, the false teaching of the founding, the keeping of a restricted instrument, or interference with the apparatus of the silo's keeping may no longer bring a speaking-citizen to the hearing Amendment 6 otherwise grants. The Assembly's record of its own deliberation is entered in the archive in summary only.

\subsection*{Amendment 14: The Recitation Affirmed}

\textit{Ratified after Amendment 13, upon the Mayor's proposal, in a season the Mayor's report called a season of forgetting among the young.}

``\secref{art:schooling}{sec:schooling:founding-and-the-limits-of-instruction} is amended to add: `And the teacher shall, before the recitation is taught, say to the children that the silo is a vessel with a hull, and a hull cannot be pierced in one place without the whole of it taking on water, as the Preamble of this Pact says, that the children know why the recitation is kept, and keep it the more gladly for knowing.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:schooling:founding-and-the-limits-of-instruction} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A sentence of the Preamble, which every child already hears read at the opening of the school year under \secref{art:schooling}{sec:schooling:rest-days-and-the-school-year}, is to be said again before the recitation is taught. The Judge's statement under \artref{art:records} found no conflict with any provision of this Pact, and no provision altered.

\subsection*{Amendment 15: The Ordinary Pause}

\textit{Ratified some years after Amendment 14, upon the Mayor's proposal at the Sheriff's asking.}

``\secref{art:sheriff}{sec:sheriff:stair-and-the-landings} is amended to add: `The ordinary pause for rest upon the stair or a landing, beyond which a citizen may not remain there, is a pause of no longer than a count of two hundred, or of four hundred for a citizen carrying a load, or a child, or retired under \artref{art:health}; and the Peacekeeper posted at a landing shall keep the count for any citizen who pauses, and move the citizen on at its end.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:sheriff:stair-and-the-landings} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A pause the Pact left to the Sheriff's judgment is given a length. The Sheriff's report to the Assembly at the sitting following gave the number of citizens moved on under this amendment in its first season as none, the Peacekeepers having found no citizen who paused so long that they could reach the count before the citizen moved of their own accord.

\subsection*{Amendment 16: The Naming of Officers in Assembly}

\textit{Ratified after Amendment 15, upon the Mayor's proposal at the recommendation of the Judge.}

``\secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly} is amended to add: `The protection this Section gives a citizen who speaks to the Assembly does not extend to an address that names an officer of the silo and imputes to that officer a breach of this Pact, save where the citizen has first brought the matter to Judicial under \artref{art:judicial} and Judicial has ruled upon it; an address so made without a ruling is struck from the archive, and the citizen is answerable as for a false bringing under \secref{art:crimes}{sec:crimes:offenses-against-another-citizen}, where Judicial afterward finds the imputation false.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A citizen who would say before the Assembly that an officer has broken this Pact must first have said so to Judicial. The Assembly's record of the sitting enters three addresses struck under this amendment at the sitting next following its ratification, without their text.

\subsection*{Amendment 17: The Mayor's Presence at the Tending}

\textit{Ratified upon a petition of the citizens of Mechanical, the first petition of citizens brought from the Down Deep, after a stopping of the generator for its full tending that ran three days beyond the one day \artref{art:mechanical} allows without the Mayor's written order --- the order having been given, the Head of Mechanical represented to the Assembly, too late to be of use.}

``\secref{art:mechanical}{sec:mechanical:shifts-and-the-tending-of-the-generator} is amended to add: `The Mayor shall be present in the Down Deep, at the generator's housing, for the whole of the full tending, and shall sign the log of it beside the Head of Mechanical; and where the tending must run beyond one day, the Mayor's order under this Section is given there, in the log, and not sent.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:mechanical:shifts-and-the-tending-of-the-generator} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: The Mayor attends the yearly full tending and signs its log. The amendment does not say what the Mayor's presence at the housing is to accomplish, and Mechanical's petition, which survives entire in the archive, does not say either.

\subsection*{Amendment 18: The Notice of Revaluation}

\textit{Ratified twice, the first ratification having been entered in error: the tellers' count under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:vote-and-its-counting} had added a floor count that arrived after the close of the sitting. The Judge struck the first entry, and the amendment was put again at the sitting following, and carried. Both entries stand in the archive; this Log enters the second.}

``\secref{art:commerce}{sec:commerce:chit-keepers-and-the-rolls-they-keep} is amended to add: `No revaluation of chits under this Section takes effect until one cycle after its announcement to the Assembly, and the Chit-Keepers shall post the revaluation, and the day it takes effect, at every market and every dispatch of porters within one day of the announcement.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:assembly:hearing-before-the-assembly} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: A revaluation is announced one cycle before it binds. The Assembly's record of the first vote, and the floor count that was wrongly added, are entered under \secref{art:assembly}{sec:assembly:vote-and-its-counting} as that Section provides for a count arriving late.

\subsection*{Amendment 19: The Keeping of the Green List}

\textit{Ratified after Amendment 18, at the recommendation of the Department of Information Technology, upon the Mayor's proposal.}

``\secref{art:emergency}{sec:emergency:green-list} is amended such that the green list is maintained by the Mayor together with the Head of Information Technology, whose concurrence every updating of the list requires, in consultation with Judicial and the Heads of the other Departments as that Section otherwise provides; and the report to the Assembly that the list has been kept is made by the Mayor and the Head of Information Technology together. The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:emergency:green-list} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: The green list, which this Pact gave the Mayor to keep in consultation with Judicial and the Heads, is now kept by the Mayor and the Head of Information Technology together, and no updating stands without both. The concurrence \artref{art:amendment} requires, for an amendment touching what this Pact keeps from ordinary record, was given by the Judge and the Head of Information Technology; the Judge's statement under \artref{art:records} found no conflict with any provision of this Pact.

\subsection*{Amendment 20: The Housing Proviso Restated}

\textit{Ratified last of those entered in this Log, upon a petition of citizens.}

``\secref{art:housing}{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} is amended to add: `Where a citizen's dwelling is to be revoked under this Section, and the citizen has tried in good faith to mend what occasioned the revoking, and there has been time enough for the mending, the citizen may ask Judicial that the revoking be lessened or stayed, and Judicial may so order.' The remainder of \secrefhere{sec:housing:charge-of-housing} is unchanged.''

\textit{Effect}: This Log enters the amendment as ratified. Its effect, beside Amendment 1, is none that the Judge could find, and the Judge's statement under \artref{art:records} says so; the Assembly's record does not show that Amendment 1 was read before the vote.

\section*{Unamended Provisions}

The following provisions of the Pact have not been amended since its founding and remain in their original form:

\begin{itemize}
\item \artref{art:founding}: Of the Founding and Purpose of the Silo
\item \artref{art:citizenship}: Of Citizenship and the Census
\item \artref{art:succession}: Of the Deputy Mayor and the Order of Succession
\item \artref{art:it}: Of the Department of Information Technology
\item \artref{art:mines}: Of the Department of Mines
\item \artref{art:supply}: Of Supply, Hydroponics, and the Ration
\item \artref{art:crimes}: Of Crimes and Their Punishments
\item \artref{art:cleaning}: Of the Cleaning
\item \artref{art:records}: Of Records, Porters, and the Post
\item \artref{art:amendment}: Of Amendment and the Continuance of the Pact
\end{itemize}

The fact that an Article remains unamended does not indicate that it is unchanging or unchangeable---only that no amendment ratified by Assembly has yet altered it. The founding principles of the Pact remain firm; the amendments recorded above show how the silo's citizens have chosen to clarify, extend, restrict, and occasionally soften the Pact's application over time.
